Wood vs. Vinyl Fence: 15-Year Head-to-Head

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

The most common fence decision in America: wood privacy or vinyl? Wood wins install day by ~$1,925. Vinyl wins year 15 by ~$575 — and never asks for a weekend with a stain brush. Here's the full math.

15-year amortization

Wood needs staining every 2–3 years to reach its 15–20 year life (~$500 hired per 150-ft cycle, 5 cycles in 15 years). Vinyl needs an annual rinse (~$0). Straight-line, no discounting — illustrative.

The crossover:
Vinyl pulls ahead around year 11–12 — right when a wood fence starts looking tired if staining slipped. And the $575 gap understates it: vinyl's 25–30 year life means at year 15 it's barely middle-aged, while wood is entering its final third.

When wood still wins

  • You'll sell within ~7 years. The buyer gets a decent fence; you never pay for most of the staining. Wood's install-day savings are real money in this window.
  • You DIY the staining. Materials run ~$150–$250 per cycle vs. $500 hired — five DIY cycles cost ~$1,000, keeping wood ahead for the full 15 years (plus your weekends).
  • Short runs. Vinyl's panel system has fixed costs that punish small jobs; wood scales down linearly.
  • You prefer the look. Honest answer: plenty of people just like wood. Cost isn't the only variable.

When vinyl wins

  • You're staying 10+ years. The math above is the whole story — vinyl crosses over and keeps pulling away.
  • You won't maintain wood. Be honest: skipped staining turns a 20-year fence into a 12-year one, and then the comparison isn't close at all.
  • HOA or strict aesthetics. Vinyl looks identical in year 12 as year 1 — no graying, no warping, no neighbor complaints.

DIY materials estimated at ~70–75% of the installed material line (no contractor markup, retail lumber pricing). Budgeting estimates, not quotes.

Fences reward DIY more than most projects on this site: the skills are basic (level, plumb, consistent spacing), mistakes are visible and fixable, and the labor share of the bill is huge — roughly a third of the installed price. The gotchas: post depth (below frost line, or they'll heave), property lines (a survey is cheaper than a neighbor dispute), and underground utilities (call 811 before you dig — it's free and it's the law in most states). Vinyl is slightly less forgiving DIY than wood because panels come in fixed sizes; measure twice, and confirm the panel system before you buy. Wood vs. vinyl FAQs

Can I mix — vinyl in front, wood in back?

Yes, and it's a legitimate budget play: vinyl where it's visible, wood where it's not. Just know the wood section still needs its staining schedule, and the transition needs a clean post detail.

Does vinyl crack in cold climates?

Cheap vinyl can — look for UV inhibitors, thicker walls, and impact modifiers rated for your zone. Quality vinyl handles freeze-thaw fine; bargain panels are where the horror stories come from.

How much does it cost to remove an old fence?

Typically $3–$6 per linear foot for demo and haul-away — a 150-LF fence adds $450–$900 before the new build starts. Some quotes include it, some don't: ask explicitly, because "fence replacement" pricing sometimes assumes the old one vanishes by magic.

Can I install a fence myself to save money?

Yes — fences are among the most DIY-friendly projects. On a 150-LF run you save roughly $1,500–$2,500 in labor. The non-negotiables: call 811 before digging, set posts below the frost line, and confirm your property line (a survey beats a neighbor dispute).

Does a new fence increase home value?

A well-built privacy fence typically recoups 50–70% of its cost at resale, more in family-oriented neighborhoods where buyers actively want one. It's not a profit center — it's a use-and-enjoy improvement that happens to return part of its cost.

Wood or vinyl on a sloped yard?

Wood adapts to slopes more gracefully (it can be racked or stepped in small increments); vinyl panels step in fixed increments, which looks chunkier on steep grades. Budget +10–20% either way for sloped installs.
