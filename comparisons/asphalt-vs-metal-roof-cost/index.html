Asphalt vs. Metal Roof Cost: The 30-Year Head-to-Head

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

The most common roofing question we see: "is metal worth double the price?" We priced both roofs on the exact same house, itemized every line — then ran the clock forward 30 years. The answer surprised us too.

The 30-year amortization

Divide each total by its rated lifespan and the ranking inverts:

Architectural asphaltStanding-seam metal
Installed total$17,120$33,890
Rated lifespan (mid)27.5 years55 years
Cost per year of rated life$623/yr$616/yr
Installs needed in 30 years1.09 (second roof at ~yr 27.5)0.55 (one roof, with room to spare)
30-yr prorated cost + maintenance*$19,276$18,485
Roof life remaining at year 30~25 yrs (on roof #2)~25 yrs (on roof #1)

* Prorated: total × (30 ÷ lifespan) + maintenance estimates ($600 shingles / ~$0 metal). Shingle math assumes you actually replace at ~27.5 years — which is the part everyone forgets to budget.

Here's the uncomfortable truth for shingle fans: metal is cheaper per year from day one — not after some distant break-even point, immediately. The entire premium is a financing problem, not a value problem. And at year 30, the shingle owner is on roof #2 while the metal owner still hasn't replaced anything.

Metal isn't one price: which metal system?

The worked example above assumes standing-seam steel — the residential gold standard. But "metal roof" covers four distinct systems, and the price spread between them is bigger than the gap between shingles and entry-level metal:

Metal system2026 installed $/sqftRated life
Screw-down / exposed fastener$4.50–$1525–45 yrs
Standing seam (steel)$8–$1840–70 yrs
Stone-coated steel / metal shingles$8–$1530–50 yrs
Copper$25–$4070+ yrs

Screw-down range: Angi July 2026 ($3.50–$15) and Bill Ragan Roofing 2026 ($4.50–$6.50/sqft, steel panels, labor + materials). Standing seam corroborated by Koalaty Roofers ($12–$20/sqft) and Best Roofing Now ($10–$18). Copper from Best Roofing Now 2026.

The budget play that flips the comparison: a screw-down steel roof at ~$6/sqft on the same 2,236-sqft roof works out to roughly $13,400 installed — actually cheaper than architectural shingles at $17,120. The catch is maintenance: exposed fasteners work loose through thermal expansion, so expect a re-screw cycle roughly every 10–12 years plus washer replacement. The "near-zero maintenance" claim applies to standing seam, not screw-down. Budgeted properly, screw-down's 30-year math stays competitive with shingles; ignored, the leaks erase the savings. Standing seam remains the value king on cost-per-year.

The resale angle: what buyers pay for

If you might sell before the 30-year math fully plays out, the relevant number is resale recoup — and here shingles briefly edge ahead on percentage:

MaterialAvg job costResale value addedCost recouped
Asphalt shingles (mid-range)$30,500$20,50067.2%
Standing-seam metal$48,400$31,40064.9%

Per the 2025–26 Remodeling Magazine Cost vs Value Report (US national averages, as reported by HomeworthHQ, 2026). iBuyer's 2025 compilation shows the same ordering: asphalt 56–68%, metal 48–61%.

Two caveats keep this honest. First, percentages hide dollars: metal adds $10,900 more resale value ($31,400 vs. $20,500) even at a slightly lower recoup rate. Second, "resale ROI" only applies if the roof is selling-age. A shingle roof sold at year 22 of a 25-year life triggers buyer discounts and insurance headaches that never enter the ROI table — while a mid-life metal roof with 30+ years remaining removes the roof from the buyer's risk calculation entirely. Appraisers and insurers notice that difference; the percentage table doesn't.

Energy: the cool-roof footnote

In hot climates there's a genuine operating-cost edge for metal that the amortization table misses. Metal roofs reflect solar heat — the Department of Energy cites this as a cooling-load reducer — and ENERGY STAR-certified "cool" metal roofs must meet minimum solar-reflectance standards, with documented cooling savings in hot regions. The savings are climate-dependent (meaningful in the Sun Belt, near zero in cool zones) and won't flip the decision on their own, but over a 55-year service life in a hot climate they're a real line item in metal's favor. Check the ENERGY STAR product criteria to see which finishes qualify.

When asphalt still wins

  • You're selling within 10–15 years. You won't own the replacement; buy the cheaper roof and let the next owner decide.
  • Cash flow is the constraint. $16,770 extra today is real money. A roof you can afford beats a roof you can't.
  • Your HOA or historic district mandates shingles. Some do. Check before dreaming.

When metal wins

  • You're staying 20+ years. The per-year math favors metal immediately and the gap only widens.
  • Hail, wildfire, or heavy snow. Metal's durability isn't theoretical — it's the difference between a claim and a shrug.
  • You can capture the insurance discount. In hail/fire zones the premium savings compound over 55 years.
  • You hate re-roofing. One and done has real lifestyle value.

Asphalt vs. metal FAQs

How much more does a metal roof cost than asphalt shingles?

About 98% more upfront at standard tiers: ~$33,890 vs. ~$17,120 on the same 2,000-sqft-footprint home (2,236 sqft of roof) — a $16,770 difference at October 2026 prices. Run your own numbers in the roof cost calculator.

Is a metal roof worth the extra cost?

Per year of service, yes — ~$616/yr vs. ~$623/yr for architectural shingles, cheaper from day one. Over 30 years the prorated cost is ~$18,485 vs. ~$19,276, and the metal roof still has ~25 years of life left at year 30.

Are metal roofs noisy in the rain?

No. Residential standing-seam installs over solid decking, underlayment, and attic insulation — rain noise is roughly the same as shingles. The noisy version is uninsulated metal over open purlins, i.e., a barn.

Can you install a metal roof over shingles?

Sometimes — one existing layer can stay underneath in many jurisdictions, saving ~$1.50/sqft in tear-off. But you can't inspect the decking and the trapped layer adds weight. Full tear-off is the better roof; overlay is the cheaper one.

Does a metal roof lower homeowners insurance?

Often, especially in hail and wildfire zones where the Class A fire rating and impact resistance earn real discounts. Get the insurance quote before choosing — the savings compound over a 55-year roof life.

Do metal roofs attract lightning?

No. Lightning hits the tallest object, not the most conductive — a metal roof doesn't attract strikes. If lightning does hit, metal is actually safer than alternatives: it's noncombustible, so it won't ignite like wood shakes can, and it disperses the charge across the structure. Standard grounding handles the rest.

What's the cheapest metal roof option?

Screw-down (exposed fastener) steel panels — roughly $4.50–$6.50 per sqft installed for the panels at 2026 contractor pricing, which can land a full roof below the cost of architectural shingles. The trade-off is maintenance: exposed fasteners need periodic re-tightening (about every 10–12 years) as thermal expansion works them loose. Budget that maintenance and the 30-year math stays competitive; ignore it and leaks erase the savings.
