How replacement timing moves the numbers

Every table above uses mid-range lifespans — but real floors die early or late, and the swing changes the verdict. Two sensitivity checks worth running before you commit:

  • Pets and kids compress soft-floor life. Carpet rated 5–15 years in a pet home trends toward 5–7, pulling its crossover with LVP from ~year 10 to ~year 7 — and turning Scenario 4's rental math even uglier. LVP's 15–25 year range barely moves with dogs.
  • Refinishing stretches wood's lead. One ~$2,500 refinish at year 15 resets a solid-hardwood floor for roughly 15% of a reinstall — the crossover-vs-LVP math above ignores this, and including it pulls hardwood's advantage years earlier.
  • DIY shifts the day-one column, not the slope. Installing LVP yourself erases ~$2,750 of labor per 1,000 sq ft, widening its early lead — but replacement cycles don't change, so the crossover years barely move. DIY buys margin, not a new winner.

The honest move: run your scenario twice — once with mid-range lifespans, once with pessimistic ones. If the same material wins both, that's your floor. If the winner flips, you're in a close call where resale value and DIY plans should break the tie. The flooring calculator prices any square footage so you can rerun these in seconds. Comparison FAQs

Which flooring is cheapest over 20 years?

LVP, at ~$7,700 for 1,000 sq ft — it lasts the full 20 years with no replacement. Laminate runs ~$8,433 (one replacement at year 15); carpet ~$11,000 (one replacement at year 10). Solid hardwood costs the most upfront ($16,775) but the least per year (~$0.22/sq ft/yr) — a 40-year answer to a 20-year question.

Is tile or LVP cheaper for a kitchen?

LVP: ~$1,540 installed for 200 sq ft. Tile: ~$2,695. Per year, tile wins (~$54/yr vs ~$77/yr) because it lasts 2–3× longer. Choose by budget horizon and DIY plans: LVP if you might remodel within a decade or want a weekend DIY; tile if this is the forever kitchen and you're hiring the labor anyway.

Why is tile labor so expensive?

Thinset mortar work, precision cutting around fixtures, and grout — usually across multiple visits with cure days between. At $4–$10/sq ft, tile labor routinely exceeds the tile's own material cost. It's skilled, slow, unforgiving work: a bad tile job costs more to demolish and redo than the labor you tried to save. Always get tile labor quoted as its own line item.

What flooring should I put in a rental property?

LVP. It's waterproof, tenant-proof, and at ~$7.70/sq ft installed it survives 15+ years of turnover — where carpet needs replacing every 5–7 years. On 1,600 sq ft over 14 years, LVP costs ~$12,320 total versus ~$26,400 for carpet with two replacements. Laminate is the budget backup for dry units only.

When does hardwood become cheaper than LVP?

On pure install cost, not until ~year 40 for 1,000 sq ft: LVP's $7,700 stretches 20 years per install while hardwood costs $16,775 upfront. Hardwood's real mid-horizon argument is resale — 70–80% cost recovery and +2.5–3% home value (NAR) — which LVP can't match. Buy hardwood for the decades and the resale, not for a 10-year payback.

Does DIY change which material wins?

It narrows the gap but doesn't flip the economics. A 180 sq ft bedroom: DIY LVP runs ~$842 versus pro-installed carpet at ~$1,089 — labor, not material, decides small rooms. But DIY can't change replacement cycles: carpet still wears out every decade, so the crossover math only moves, it doesn't reverse. Hire out tile and hardwood regardless.
