* Prorated: total × (25 ÷ lifespan) + maintenance estimates (~$0 vinyl / ~$4,000 fiber cement for one repaint). Unlike the roof comparison — where metal wins per-year from day one — fiber cement never catches vinyl on cost. Its premium buys something else entirely.

Here's the honest framing: fiber cement's premium is not a value play — it's a durability, fire-safety, and resale play. Vinyl wins every cost column. Fiber cement wins every column that isn't cost: it won't melt in a wildfire ember storm, hail that totals vinyl leaves it intact, and buyers pay measurably more for it (70–85% ROI vs. ~60–70%). Whether that's worth $9,375 depends on your zip code's hazards and your time horizon — not on the sticker.

The insurance angle most comparisons skip

Fiber cement's Class A non-combustible rating isn't just a spec-sheet line — in wildfire and hail zones it converts directly to money. Many insurers offer siding-based discounts for non-combustible exteriors, and some carriers surcharge or decline wood siding in high-risk zones. Over a 40-year fiber-cement lifespan, even a modest annual premium difference compounds into thousands — the one cost column where fiber cement can beat vinyl without trying. Get the quote from your insurer before choosing: it's the only number in this comparison that comes from your actual policy.

When vinyl still wins

  • Budget is the binding constraint. $9,375 extra today is real money. Siding you can afford beats siding you can't.
  • You're selling within 10–15 years. You won't own the repaint cycle; buy the cheaper siding and let the next owner decide.
  • Low hazard area. No wildfire risk, mild hail — vinyl's weaknesses never get tested.
  • Rental property. Tenants don't pay extra for fiber cement; the ROI math collapses.

When fiber cement wins

  • Wildfire or hail zone. Non-combustible and impact-resistant isn't theoretical — it's the difference between a claim and a shrug. Check insurance discounts.
  • You're staying 20+ years. The 40–50-year lifespan means one install in your ownership, with ~15 years to spare at year 25.
  • Resale matters. 70–85% reported ROI vs. ~60–70% for vinyl — on a $28,675 job that's $2,800–$5,700 more back at sale (estimates).
  • You want the "done" feeling. Repaint once in 25 years vs. vinyl's fade-and-replace cycle has real lifestyle value.

Vinyl vs. fiber cement FAQs

Is fiber cement siding worth the extra money over vinyl?

On pure cost, no — vinyl is ~27% cheaper over 25 years on the same house. Fiber cement justifies its ~49% upfront premium through Class A fire rating, hail-proof impact resistance, and 70–85% resale ROI. It's a durability and insurance purchase, not a value purchase.

How much more does fiber cement siding cost than vinyl?

About 49% more installed: ~$28,675 vs. ~$19,300 for a 2,500-sqft wall (standard tier, pro, tear-off and wrap included, October 2026). The gap narrows slightly over 25 years because fiber cement's longer life spreads its cost thinner.

Does fiber cement siding last longer than vinyl?

Yes — 30–50 years vs. 20–40. But "lasts longer" doesn't mean "costs less": even prorated, fiber cement plus one repaint runs ~$877/yr vs. vinyl's ~$643/yr over 25 years. Longevity and value are different questions.

Which is better in a hailstorm or wildfire zone?

Fiber cement, decisively. Non-combustible and impact-resistant — hail that dents or cracks vinyl leaves it intact. Many insurers discount fiber cement in fire and hail zones, which compounds over decades.

Can I install vinyl siding myself but not fiber cement?

Generally yes. Vinyl is the most DIY-friendly siding: light panels, interlocking system, basic tools. Fiber cement is pro-only for most homeowners — silica dust hazard, 2.5-lb/sqft panels, and flashing details that void the 30-year warranty if done wrong.
