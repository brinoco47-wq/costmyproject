DIY truck vs. moving container vs. full-service movers

CostMyProject Research Team Data checked October 2026

Three ways to move the same belongings, three wildly different prices — and the winner changes with distance. Here's the head-to-head with 2026 numbers, not vibes.

The 30-second version Locally, DIY truck rental wins by a mile (often under $200 vs. $500+). On long hauls, containers are the value sweet spot — roughly $1,000–$3,000 cheaper than full-service at 500+ miles, with storage included. Full-service wins on large local moves and whenever your time is worth more than the gap.

When each one wins

  • Rent the truck: local moves, tight budgets, and anyone comfortable driving a large vehicle. The savings are biggest here — often 3–5× cheaper than movers.
  • Book the container: 500+ mile moves, lease gaps (the storage month is free optionality), and anyone who'd rather not drive a truck through mountains. The hybrid — container + hired loaders for a few hours — is the enthusiast's pick.
  • Hire the movers: large homes, valuable/fragile-heavy households, physical limitations, or corporate relocations. Get binding written estimates and verify USDOT numbers.

The hybrid play: container + hired loaders

The move type the industry doesn't advertise: rent the container for the driving, hire local muscle for the lifting. Two loaders for 3–4 hours typically run $60–$80/hr each — $360–$640 total — and professional loading genuinely reduces damage, because weight distribution in a container matters more than in a truck you're driving yourself.

Run it on Scenario 2 (2 BR, 500 miles): container ~$2,550 + loaders ~$500 = ~$3,050 vs. full-service ~$3,610. You save $500–$650, skip the driving, and get pro-quality loading. The hybrid only loses to DIY truck on raw price — and it wins on backs, weekends, and marriages.

What drives the price up or down

  • Distance bands. DIY truck pricing jumps at the local/one-way boundary; containers and movers scale near-linearly with miles. The container's value peak is roughly 500–1,500 miles.
  • Season. May through September is peak moving season — expect 20–30% above off-season (October–April) pricing on movers and containers alike, plus tighter availability.
  • Day of the month. First and last weekends are the most expensive days to move; mid-month, mid-week dates price lowest.
  • Packing. Full packing service for a 3-bedroom runs $1,030–$2,200 in 2026 — the single biggest add-on. Packing yourself erases it entirely; fragile-only service (about 40% of full pack) splits the difference.
  • Access fees. Stairs ($50–$200/flight), long carries ($75–$200), shuttle service ($200–$500+), and bulky items ($150–$600+/item) hit full-service quotes hardest. See the hidden fees guide for the full list.
  • Valuation coverage. Basic released-value coverage ($0.60/lb) is included by law and pays almost nothing; full-value protection runs ~1% of declared value. On a $30,000 shipment that's ~$300 — cheap peace of mind.
  • Fuel and truck size. DIY trucks get ~10 mpg loaded; a 26-ft truck on a 2,500-mile run burns ~250 gallons. One-way rates bundle fuel into the quote — local daily rentals don't, so add it yourself.

Red flags in mover quotes

  • Phone-only quote, no survey. A long-distance price given without an in-home or video survey is a guess — and non-binding guesses grow on delivery day.
  • Non-binding estimate on an interstate move. Insist on binding or binding-not-to-exceed; non-binding means the final bill can legally balloon.
  • No USDOT number. Verify it free at the FMCSA website in 60 seconds. No number, no booking.
  • Deposit over 25% or cash-only. Legitimate movers ask little or nothing upfront for local moves and modest deposits for long-distance — always payable by card, with a paper trail.
  • Quote far below the others. The classic hostage-load setup: a too-good price, then your goods held for "additional fees" at delivery. The middle quote is usually the honest one.
  • Broker won't say who's driving. Brokers must disclose they're brokers. If they dodge the question of which carrier actually moves you, walk away.

How to save on any of the three

  • Move off-peak. Mid-month, mid-week, October–April: 20–30% off the identical job, whichever move type you pick.
  • Declutter before you get quotes. Movers charge by weight and hours; every donated box saves twice — and a lower binding estimate starts from a smaller inventory.
  • Pack yourself. Erases the $1,030–$2,200 full-packing line entirely. Start 3–4 weeks out with non-essentials.
  • Source free boxes. Liquor stores, bookstores, and Buy Nothing groups beat $3–$5 retail boxes; budget $100–$275 for a full DIY materials kit otherwise.
  • Compare truck quotes both ways. One-way rates shift with equipment flow — checking nearby pickup locations can change the price.
  • Ask the container company about the second month. The first storage month is usually included; the second runs ~$200–$400 — still cheaper than emergency storage-in-transit fees.

FAQs

Is PODS cheaper than hiring movers?

On long-distance moves, usually — by roughly $1,000–$3,000 at 500+ miles, with a storage month included. Locally it's a wash or slightly worse: a 1-bedroom container (~$1,235 with supplies) vs. two movers and a truck (~$1,100).

Is U-Haul cheaper than PODS?

On sticker price, almost always — $75–$160 for a local U-Haul day vs. $400–$800 for a local PODS. But PODS bundles delivery, pickup, and storage, and you never drive the truck. Add fuel, lodging, and your time before declaring a winner.

What is the cheapest way to move out of state?

A one-way DIY truck rental if you'll drive it (~$2,600–$3,900 for 2,500 miles, fuel included). Then containers, then freight trailers on some routes, then full-service. Your exact ranking: the calculator.

How much does it cost to rent a moving truck for a week?

Locally, roughly $300–$600 for a week: $19.95–$39.95/day plus $0.79–$1.29 per mile, with fuel (~10 mpg loaded) on top. One-way weekly rentals are priced as a bundled quote rather than daily, so price your exact lane — a 15-ft truck for a week-long local project is a very different number from a cross-country one-way.

Do moving containers include storage?

Yes — typically about a month of storage is built into the quoted price, and you can load over several days instead of racing a truck deadline. Need longer? Extra months run roughly $200–$400 each. That built-in flexibility is the container's killer feature when move-out and move-in dates don't align.

Should I hire movers just to load my container?

Often it's the best value in moving: two loaders for 3–4 hours at $60–$80/hr each ($360–$640 total) gives you professional weight distribution and stacking — the thing that actually prevents damage in transit. On a 500-mile 2-bedroom move, container (~$2,550) + loaders (~$500) still beats full-service (~$3,610) by $500+.

What does full-value protection cost?

Roughly 1% of your declared shipment value — about $300 on $30,000 of goods. The basic released-value coverage ($0.60/lb) is included by law but pays almost nothing (a 50-lb TV = $30). If you're shipping anything you'd hate to replace, full-value is the cheapest insurance you'll buy all year.
