What's included — and what isn't

✓ Included in every estimate

  • Flooring material, per your tier and price
  • Underlayment or setting materials (pad, thinset, grout)
  • Pro install labor, when you select "Hire a pro"
  • Waste & cuts allowance (default 10%, rounded into order quantity)
  • Old-floor removal and subfloor prep, when you toggle them on
  • Trim and transitions, when you enter an amount

✗ Not included

  • Furniture moving (often $50–$150/room if the crew does it)
  • Subfloor replacement (rotted plywood, not just leveling)
  • Stairs — priced per step, not per square foot (see measuring guide)
  • Radiant-heat systems under the floor
  • Repairs to the material itself down the line (see lifetime costs)

If a contractor's bid covers items on the right-hand list and ours doesn't, ask them to itemize — that's exactly how you compare bids honestly.

Three ways people get the math wrong

  • Forgetting waste. A 1,000 sq ft job needs ~1,100 sq ft of material. Quotes that skip the waste line look cheaper until the installer runs short mid-job.
  • Comparing material price without install. Tile at $3/sq ft sounds cheaper than LVP at $5 — until $6–$10/sq ft of tile labor enters. Always compare installed cost per square foot.
  • Ignoring the "invisible" layers. Underlayment, thinset, grout, and pad add $0–$1/sq ft. They're real money and the first thing vague quotes hide.

Flooring cost FAQs

How much does it cost to install 1,000 sq ft of flooring?

With a pro in 2026: roughly $5,500–$8,500 for laminate, $6,500–$9,000 for LVP, $8,000–$18,000 for engineered hardwood, $12,000–$25,000 for solid hardwood, $7,000–$20,000 for tile, and $4,000–$8,000 for carpet — including ~10% waste. Punch your numbers into the calculator above for your material and market.

What is the cheapest flooring to install?

Installed, carpet and laminate ($3–$8/sq ft) are cheapest. But measure cost per year of use: solid hardwood at $12–$25/sq ft installed lasts 50+ years — roughly $0.25–$0.50 per sq ft per year, often cheaper than carpet over its life. Our material comparison runs the lifetime math.

How much extra flooring should I order for waste?

10% for simple rectangular rooms, 12–15% for diagonal patterns, L-shaped rooms, or many closets. Stairs are measured separately (treads + risers), not folded into the waste percentage. See our measuring guide.

Is it cheaper to install flooring yourself?

Usually — labor is 40–60% of a pro job. LVP and laminate are genuinely DIY-friendly (click-lock, one weekend for a room). Tile and solid hardwood are where DIY most often goes wrong; a bad tile job costs more to fix than the labor you saved. Toggle DIY/pro in the calculator to see both numbers.

How accurate is this calculator?

It's a budgeting estimate built on October 2026 price ranges and installer labor data — good for budgeting and sanity-checking bids. Every result shows a ±25% planning range because subfloor condition and regional labor move real quotes. See how we compute costs for the full method and limitations.
