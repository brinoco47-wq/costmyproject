What's included — and what isn't

✓ Included in every estimate

  • Flooring material, per your tier and price
  • Underlayment or setting materials (pad, thinset, grout)
  • Pro install labor, when you select "Hire a pro"
  • Waste & cuts allowance (default 10%, rounded into order quantity)
  • Old-floor removal and subfloor prep, when you toggle them on
  • Trim and transitions, when you enter an amount

✗ Not included

  • Furniture moving (often $50–$150/room if the crew does it)
  • Subfloor replacement (rotted plywood, not just leveling)
  • Stairs — priced per step, not per square foot (see measuring guide)
  • Radiant-heat systems under the floor
  • Repairs to the material itself down the line (see lifetime costs)

If a contractor's bid covers items on the right-hand list and ours doesn't, ask them to itemize — that's exactly how you compare bids honestly.

How long does flooring installation take?

Rule of thumb from our installation guide: a pro crew installs 400–600 sq ft/day of carpet, 150–250 of click-lock LVP or laminate, 100–200 of hardwood, and 75–150 of tile. On a 1,000 sq ft job that means carpet takes about 2 days, LVP 4–6 days, and tile can stretch to 7–14 calendar days because thinset and grout need cure days between visits. Stairs, patterns, and subfloor repairs add days to every material.

DIY is slower: click-lock LVP needs basic tools and about one weekend per room. Small jobs under 200 sq ft usually carry a $300–$500 minimum charge, so a single bathroom often costs more per square foot than a whole house.

8-point flooring quote checklist

  1. Installed cost per sq ft, not material price. Compare bids on the installed total — tile's cheap material hides expensive labor.
  2. Waste allowance in writing. 10% for simple rooms, 12–15% for diagonal or L-shaped layouts.
  3. Removal and leveling itemized. If they're not on the quote, they will be on the final bill.
  4. Underlayment named. Pad, thinset, grout, or vapor barrier — and its cost per sq ft.
  5. Stairs priced per step. Never let stairs hide inside a per-square-foot number.
  6. Trim and transitions listed. Typically $150–$400 for a few rooms; T-moldings run $15–$40 each installed.
  7. Timeline in days. Carpet ~2 days per 1,000 sq ft, LVP 4–6, tile 7–14 — a one-day promise for tile is a red flag.
  8. ±25% planning range. Any bid far below the calculator's range deserves a second look at what's missing.

Three ways people get the math wrong

  • Forgetting waste. A 1,000 sq ft job needs ~1,100 sq ft of material. Quotes that skip the waste line look cheaper until the installer runs short mid-job.
  • Comparing material price without install. Tile at $3/sq ft sounds cheaper than LVP at $5 — until $6–$10/sq ft of tile labor enters. Always compare installed cost per square foot.
  • Ignoring the "invisible" layers. Underlayment, thinset, grout, and pad add $0–$1/sq ft. They're real money and the first thing vague quotes hide.

Six terms that show up in every flooring quote

Contractors speak in shorthand. Knowing these six terms keeps you from nodding along to a bid you don't understand. Wear layer is the clear top coat on LVP and laminate, measured in mils — 12-mil suits bedrooms, while 20-mil handles kitchens, pets, and rentals. Janka hardness rates how well solid hardwood resists dents (red oak is 1,290 lbf; Brazilian cherry is 2,820) — useful when you're choosing between pretty and practical. Acclimation means letting wood or laminate sit in the install room for 48–72 hours so it adjusts to your home's humidity; skipping it is how floors buckle. The expansion gap — a 1/4 to 3/8-inch perimeter gap hidden under baseboards — gives floating floors room to move with the seasons. Over concrete slabs, a moisture (vapor) barrier underlayment blocks ground moisture from ruining the floor from below. And transition strips (T-moldings and reducers) bridge height differences where new flooring meets old — budget $15–$40 each installed (October 2026 planning estimates).

Flooring cost FAQs

How much does it cost to install 1,000 sq ft of flooring?

With a pro in 2026: roughly $5,500–$8,500 for laminate, $6,500–$9,000 for LVP, $8,000–$18,000 for engineered hardwood, $12,000–$25,000 for solid hardwood, $7,000–$20,000 for tile, and $4,000–$8,000 for carpet — including ~10% waste. Punch your numbers into the calculator above for your material and market.

What is the cheapest flooring to install?

Installed, carpet and laminate ($3–$8/sq ft) are cheapest. But measure cost per year of use: solid hardwood at $12–$25/sq ft installed lasts 50+ years — roughly $0.25–$0.50 per sq ft per year, often cheaper than carpet over its life. Our material comparison runs the lifetime math.

How much extra flooring should I order for waste?

10% for simple rectangular rooms, 12–15% for diagonal patterns, L-shaped rooms, or many closets. Stairs are measured separately (treads + risers), not folded into the waste percentage. See our measuring guide.

Does flooring labor cost more than the material?

Often yes for hard surfaces. Tile labor ($4–$10/sq ft) can exceed the tile itself; hardwood labor ($4–$8/sq ft) roughly matches mid-range wood. For carpet and laminate, labor is the smaller share ($0.50–$4/sq ft). The calculator splits materials vs. labor on every result.

Is it cheaper to install flooring yourself?

Usually — labor is 40–60% of a pro job. LVP and laminate are genuinely DIY-friendly (click-lock, one weekend for a room). Tile and solid hardwood are where DIY most often goes wrong; a bad tile job costs more to fix than the labor you saved. Toggle DIY/pro in the calculator to see both numbers.

How accurate is this calculator?

It's a budgeting estimate built on October 2026 price ranges and installer labor data — good for budgeting and sanity-checking bids. Every result shows a ±25% planning range because subfloor condition and regional labor move real quotes. See how we compute costs for the full method and limitations.

How long does flooring installation take?

A pro crew installs 400–600 sq ft/day of carpet, 150–250 of click-lock LVP or laminate, 100–200 of hardwood, and 75–150 of tile. A 1,000 sq ft LVP job runs 4–6 days; carpet takes about 2; tile can stretch to 7–14 calendar days because thinset and grout need cure days between visits. See the timeline section above.
