What's included — and what isn't

✓ Included in every estimate

  • Decking materials per square foot: boards, framing lumber, fasteners
  • Pro install labor per square foot, when you select "Hire a pro"
  • Height adjustment (raised ≈ +30%, rooftop/second-story ≈ +55% vs. ground-level)
  • Stairs, at your step count and price
  • Railing, at your linear footage and price
  • Built-in benches, when you add them
  • Old-deck tear-off & disposal, when you toggle it on
  • Permit & fees, when you enter an amount

✗ Not included

  • Permit drawings / engineered plans ($500–$2,500 where required)
  • Lighting ($50–$200 per fixture installed) and electrical runs
  • Pergolas or roof structures ($3,500–$15,000+)
  • Hot tub or outdoor-kitchen structural reinforcement
  • Landscaping repair, sod, or drainage work around the deck

If a contractor's bid covers items on the right-hand list and ours doesn't, ask them to itemize — that's exactly how you compare bids honestly.

Three ways people get the math wrong

  • Comparing board prices instead of installed prices. Composite boards at $12/sqft sound like 3× pressure-treated at $4 — but installed it's $31 vs. $18/sqft, because the frame is pressure-treated lumber either way. Always compare installed cost per square foot.
  • Forgetting railing and stairs. A 320-sqft deck quote at $45/sqft = $14,400 — then 60 LF of railing ($2,700) and a stair run ($450) push it past $17,500. On small elevated decks, railing and stairs can rival the deck surface itself.
  • Pricing a raised deck like a ground-level one. Posts, beams, deeper footings, railing, and stairs mean a raised deck costs roughly 30% more per square foot than the same deck at grade. Toggle your height above — it's the single biggest cost lever after material.

Deck cost FAQs

How much does a deck cost per square foot in 2026?

Installed at ground level: pressure-treated wood $15–$30, cedar/redwood $22–$45, composite $30–$58, PVC $35–$85 per square foot. Stairs, railing, height, old-deck removal, and permits add on top — punch your numbers into the calculator above.

Is a composite deck worth the extra cost over wood?

Often yes over a decade. A 400-sqft composite deck costs ~$12,400 installed with ~$100/yr upkeep; pressure-treated wood costs ~$7,200 installed but needs ~$1,000 in staining every 2 years plus a rebuild around year 12. Wood's total typically overtakes composite's around year 11 — see our 25-year head-to-head.

How much does deck height change the price?

A lot. Ground-level decks use simple footings. A raised deck (2–8 ft) needs posts, beams, railing, and stairs — roughly +30% on the deck-surface price. A rooftop or second-story deck needs engineering and deeper footings — roughly +55%. These are industry rules of thumb; toggle your height in the calculator.

Is it cheaper to build a deck yourself?

Labor is 40–60% of an installed deck quote, so DIY saves thousands — pressure-treated wood is the most DIY-friendly material. But framing mistakes are structural: elevated decks need code-compliant footings, proper ledger attachment, and railing. Toggle DIY/pro in the calculator to see both numbers, then be honest about your skill level.

Do I need a permit to build a deck?

Usually yes — typically $100–$800 depending on the municipality, with inspections at the footing and final stages. Low ground-level decks sometimes skip permits, but anything attached to the house or elevated almost always needs one. Contractors can usually pull it for you.

How accurate is this calculator?

It's a budgeting estimate built on October 2026 installed-price ranges and pro labor data — good for budgeting and sanity-checking bids. Every result shows a ±25% planning range because soil, access, height, and regional labor move real quotes. See how we compute costs for the full method and limitations.
