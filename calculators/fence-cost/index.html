What's included — and what isn't

✓ Included in every estimate

  • Fence materials per linear foot: panels/pickets or fabric, posts, concrete, hardware
  • Pro install labor per linear foot, when you select "Hire a pro"
  • Height adjustment (4 ft ≈ −15%, 8 ft ≈ +30% vs. 6-ft baseline)
  • Terrain adder on labor for sloped or rocky ground, when selected
  • Walk and driveway gates, at your count and price
  • Old-fence removal, when you toggle it on
  • Permit & fees, when you enter an amount

✗ Not included

  • Property survey ($200–$1,200 if your line is uncertain — get one before you build)
  • Land clearing, tree/root removal, or major grading
  • Gate automation / openers ($500–$5,000+)
  • Staining or sealing a wood fence after install (budget $300–$700 per 150 ft every 2–3 years)
  • Retaining walls or drainage work under the fence line

If a contractor's bid covers items on the right-hand list and ours doesn't, ask them to itemize — that's exactly how you compare bids honestly.

Three ways people get the math wrong

  • Forgetting gates. A $28/LF quote × 150 LF = $4,200 — then the walk gate ($250) and permit ($75) push it past $4,500. Gates need heavier posts and hardware; per-foot math alone always comes in low.
  • Comparing material price without install. Chain-link at $10/LF sounds half the price of wood at $20 — but installed it's $17 vs. $31/LF, and the privacy difference is the whole point. Always compare installed cost per linear foot.
  • Ignoring the ground. A slope that forces stepped panels or rocky soil that needs an auger can add 15–35% to labor. Flat-yard quotes don't survive contact with a hillside — toggle your terrain above.

Fence cost FAQs

How much does a fence cost per linear foot in 2026?

Installed at 6 ft: chain-link $13–$25, wood privacy $18–$45, vinyl $30–$55, aluminum $37–$60, composite roughly $44–$72 per linear foot — before gates, removal, permits, and terrain. Punch your numbers into the calculator above for your material and yard.

How many linear feet of fence do I need?

Rectangular yard: 2 × (length + width). A 40×35 ft backyard = 150 LF. Irregular lots: walk each run with a measuring wheel and add them up, or enter LF directly in the calculator.

Is it cheaper to build a fence yourself?

Usually — labor is 30–50% of a pro job. Wood privacy and chain-link are the most DIY-friendly; most DIYers report 2–4 weekends for 150 LF with a helper and a rented auger. Vinyl and aluminum need precise post spacing, which is where DIY most often goes wrong. Toggle DIY/pro in the calculator to see both numbers.

Do I need a permit to build a fence?

Most cities require one — typically $20–$400, though some waive it for fences under 6 ft. Check your municipality before you dig, and call 811 for a free utility locate. Fence companies pull permits routinely; DIYers forget this step most.

How accurate is this calculator?

It's a budgeting estimate built on October 2026 installed-price ranges and installer labor data — good for budgeting and sanity-checking bids. Every result shows a ±25% planning range because soil, slope, and regional labor move real quotes. See how we compute costs for the full method and limitations.
