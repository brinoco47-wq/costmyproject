Pitch factors, waste, and squares to order

The pitch factor is pure geometry — the hypotenuse of the roof slope, sqrt(1 + (rise/12)²). A 2,000 sq ft footprint at 6/12 pitch is 2,236 sq ft of actual roof. Use the factor for your pitch, then add a waste allowance for cuts and valleys.

Plan on a waste allowance of 10% for a simple gable, 15% for a hip roof with dormers, 20%+ for a cut-up roof with several valleys — these are planning allowances, not measurements. On the worked example roof (22.4 squares at 6/12), a 10% allowance means ordering 24.6 squares (about 74 bundles at 3 bundles per square).

What insurance pays after storm damage: ACV vs RCV

After a storm, the question homeowners actually ask is not what a roof costs — it is what insurance pays. The answer depends on one clause: ACV (actual cash value) pays the depreciated value; RCV (replacement cost value) pays the full replacement cost, with the depreciated part held back until the work is finished and the final invoice is in.

Run our own $17,120 national example through the math, with assumptions stated: an 18-year-old architectural roof on a 28-year expected life has 10/28 of its life left, so its depreciated value is 10/28 × $17,120 = $6,114. With a $1,000 deductible assumed, the first check on an ACV policy is $6,114 − $1,000 = $5,114 — leaving a $12,006 gap to the full replacement cost. On an RCV policy, that gap is recoverable after completion, once you submit the final invoice.

This is an illustrative example using straight-line depreciation, not a coverage determination: insurers use their own depreciation schedules, some cap by roof age, and hail/wind deductibles (often 1–5% of Coverage A) can be much larger than the $1,000 assumed here. Read your policy's loss-settlement clause before a storm, not after — and never sign an assignment-of-benefits form without reading it.

What's included — and what isn't

✓ Included in every estimate

  • Roofing materials per sqft of roof surface: shingles/panels/tiles, underlayment, fasteners
  • Pro install labor per sqft, with pitch and stories adjustments, when you select "Hire a pro"
  • Pitch conversion: footprint × 1.031–1.414 to true roof surface (pure geometry)
  • Tear-off & disposal per layer, when you select it
  • Ice-and-water shield upgrade, skylight re-flashing, and permit, when you add them

✗ Not included

  • Decking replacement for rotted plywood/OSB (~$85/sheet installed, found at tear-off)
  • Structural reinforcement for heavy tile on homes not built for it
  • Gutters and downspouts ($5–$14 per linear foot)
  • Solar panel removal/reinstall (~$1,500–$3,000)
  • Interior repair for existing leak damage

If a contractor's bid covers items on the right-hand list and ours doesn't, ask them to itemize — that's exactly how you compare bids honestly.

Surprise costs roofers don't put in the first quote

The worked example covers materials, labor, one-layer tear-off, and permit. These are the extras that most often move the final bill:

  • Rotten decking. Decking replaced during tear-off is usually billed per sheet on top of the quote. Ask the per-sheet price before tear-off starts, not when the roof is already open.
  • Steep pitch. Labor surcharges run +15% at 9/12 pitch and +25% at 12/12 in our model — a steep roof is a different labor job than a walkable one.
  • Second story. Stories add +10% (two stories) to +20% (three stories) on labor in our model — access time, not materials.
  • A second tear-off layer. The example assumes one layer at $1.50/sq ft. Two layers roughly double the tear-off line — on the worked example roof, that's about $3,354 more.
  • Flashing, vents, and skylights. Valleys, chimneys, and skylight flashing are priced per item in most bids. Count them before you compare quotes.

Before you sign a roofing quote

  1. Confirm how many tear-off layers the quote includes, and the per-sheet decking replacement price.
  2. Confirm pitch and stories adjustments are already in the labor line, not added later.
  3. Ask whether permit and disposal are included — both are in our worked example.
  4. Get the shingle brand, line, and warranty (material + workmanship) named in writing.
  5. Ask how weather delays are handled — an open roof and a storm forecast need a plan.

Three ways people get the math wrong

  • Pricing the footprint instead of the roof. A 2,000-sqft home at 6/12 pitch has 2,236 sqft of roof — 12% more than the floor plan. At 12/12 it's 41% more. Quotes that skip the pitch factor are the #1 reason "per square foot" numbers disagree across the web.
  • Forgetting tear-off. Stripping one layer of old shingles runs ~$1.50/sqft — on a 2,236-sqft roof that's $3,354, nearly 20% of an architectural-shingle job. "New roof $8,000" quotes that skip tear-off aren't comparable to ones that include it.
  • Comparing material prices instead of installed prices. Metal panels at $6.75/sqft sound like 2.7× architectural shingles at $2.50 — but installed it's $11.75 vs. $6.00/sqft, because metal's specialized labor narrows the gap. Always compare installed cost per square foot, then divide by lifespan years for the honest number.

Roof cost FAQs

How much does a roof cost per square foot in 2026?

Installed, per square foot of roof surface: 3-tab asphalt $3.50–$5.50, architectural asphalt $4.50–$7.50, standing-seam metal $10–$16, wood shake $8.50–$14, clay/concrete tile $11–$18. Tear-off, pitch, stories, and permits add on top — punch your numbers into the calculator above.

How do I calculate my roof's square footage from my home's footprint?

Multiply footprint (length × width) by a pitch factor: 1.031 for 3/12, 1.118 for 6/12, 1.250 for 9/12, 1.414 for 12/12. The factor is the hypotenuse of the slope — pure geometry. A 2,000-sqft footprint at 6/12 is 2,236 sqft of roof, or about 22.4 roofing "squares" (100 sqft each).

Is a metal roof worth the extra cost over asphalt shingles?

Per year of service, yes. A 2,000-sqft-footprint architectural roof costs ~$17,120 installed with a ~27.5-year life (~$623/yr); standing-seam metal costs ~$33,890 with a ~55-year life (~$616/yr). Metal costs ~98% more upfront but is cheaper per year from day one — see our 30-year head-to-head.

How much does roof pitch change the price?

Two ways. Steeper pitch means more surface (a 12/12 roof has 41% more area than its footprint), and crews charge 15–25% more labor on steep pitches for safety gear and slower work. Toggle your pitch in the calculator — after material, it's the biggest cost lever.

Can I install a new roof myself to save money?

Technically possible, strongly discouraged. Labor is 40–65% of a roof quote, so the savings look tempting — but falls are a leading cause of construction deaths, most manufacturer warranties require professional installation, and a bad install voids both. Toggle DIY/pro above to see both numbers, then hire a pro.

How accurate is this calculator?

It's a budgeting estimate built on October 2026 installed-price ranges and pro labor data — good for budgeting and sanity-checking bids. Every result shows a ±25% planning range because region, pitch, access, decking condition, and complexity move real quotes. See how we compute costs for the full method and limitations.
