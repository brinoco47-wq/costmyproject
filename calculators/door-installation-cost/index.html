What this calculator estimates

This tool prices door installation from the decisions that actually move the number: how many doors, the door type (interior slab rehang, interior prehung, exterior entry, french/patio pair, or sliding patio), the material (hollow-core, solid wood, fiberglass, or steel), hardware and finish upgrades, and your region's labor rates. Every result shows the line-by-line math and a ±25% planning range — because this is a budgeting estimate, not a quote, and real bids move with frame condition, rot repair, lockset choices, and local labor markets.

How the math works

  • Door + install base: per-door installed base — interior slab $230 (door $80 + 1.5 hr labor), interior prehung $400 (door $180 + 1 hr labor), exterior entry $800 (door $450 + 3 hr labor + weatherproofing), french pair $1,800 per door ($1,200 door + 4 hr labor), sliding patio $1,400 ($900 door + 3.5 hr labor).
  • Material multiplier: hollow-core 0.85×, steel 1.0×, fiberglass 1.15×, solid wood 1.25× — applied to the door portion only, since labor barely changes with material.
  • Labor scaling: the labor portion is multiplied by your region rate (0.85–1.2×) and your ZIP adjustment if entered. Materials stay at national prices.
  • Hardware: reuse $0, standard lockset $60, premium $180 — per door.
  • Finish: pro paint/stain $120 per door, both sides. Unfinished saves it all if you'll DIY.
  • Frame repair: $250 per door when the frame is rotted, split, or out of square — covers jamb repair, shimming and re-trimming.

Every figure is an October 2026 US planning estimate. Real quotes vary — that's why results show a ±25% planning range. This is a budgeting estimate, not a quote.

Prehung vs. slab: the real math

This is the most misunderstood choice in doors. A slab (door only) looks cheaper on the shelf — $50–$150 for hollow-core vs. $150–$250 prehung — but the installed math often reverses:

Slab (rehang)Prehung
Door cost$50–$150$150–$250
Labor$150–$300 (1–2 hrs, skilled)$100–$200 (under 1 hr)
Total$200–$450$250–$450
NeedsPerfect existing frameNothing — brings its own frame

Rule of thumb: if the frame is perfect and square, a slab saves ~$50–$100 per door. If the frame is even slightly off — and most 20-year-old frames are — the carpenter burns that savings truing it up, and prehung wins on both cost and result. For new openings, there's no contest: prehung, every time.

Material guide: what your money buys

  • Hollow-core molded ($50–$150 slab): cardboard honeycomb inside, featherweight, dents if you look at it hard. Fine for closets and pantries. Terrible for bedrooms — you can hear everything through them.
  • Solid-core molded ($120–$280 slab): the value sweet spot for interiors. Particleboard or foam core kills sound transmission and feels substantial. The upgrade from hollow-core costs ~$100/door installed and transforms how a house feels.
  • Solid wood ($250–$800+ slab): beautiful, heavy, repairable — and it moves with humidity. Best for high-visibility interior doors and traditional entry doors. Needs finishing on all six edges or it warps.
  • Fiberglass ($300–$1,500 entry): the modern exterior default. Molded wood-grain looks convincing, never rots, dents less than steel, insulates well. The rational choice for most entry doors.
  • Steel ($200–$600 entry): cheapest exterior option, strongest security, but dents show and scratches rust. Great for side and back doors where looks matter less.

Exterior doors also carry energy ratings — look for ENERGY STAR certification and check the DOE's door guidance on U-factor and air leakage. A $1,200 fiberglass door with proper weatherstripping outperforms a $3,000 wood door with gaps, every winter, forever.

Hardware and locksets: the $30–$300 swing

Hardware is where door budgets quietly double. Budget honestly:

  • Interior passage/privacy: $15–$60 per set. Privacy (bedroom/bath) costs slightly more than passage (closet/hall).
  • Entry handleset: $60–$150 standard, $150–$300 premium (solid forged brass, smart deadbolt).
  • Deadbolt alone: $30–$80 standard, $100–$250 smart. A $40 deadbolt with a reinforced strike plate beats a $200 decorative handleset with a flimsy strike — burglars attack the frame, not the lock.
  • Hinges: usually included with prehung; $5–$15 each for slab upgrades. Ball-bearing hinges ($10–$20 each) are worth it on heavy solid doors.

Security truth: the DOE and locksmiths agree — a 3-inch screw into the stud through the strike plate ($2 in screws) adds more real security than a $250 lock on a 3/4-inch screw. Do the screws regardless of what lockset you buy.

When the frame is the problem

About a third of "door replacement" quotes are really frame repairs wearing a door costume. Know the signs:

  • Rot: soft, punky wood at the bottom of exterior jambs — $150–$400 to cut out and splice, $250–$600 for full jamb replacement. Caused by missing flashing or failed caulk; fix the water source or it returns.
  • Out of square: door rubs top corner on one side, gap at the bottom on the other. A carpenter can often re-shim a prehung unit in an hour ($100–$200); a slab in a racked frame is a losing battle — go prehung.
  • Split jamb from a kick-in: $100–$250 to sister and re-secure, plus the strike-plate reinforcement that should have been there. Common enough that it's worth checking every exterior door you own.

If rot extends past the jamb into the subfloor, rim joist, or wall framing, you're past door work and into carpentry — $800–$2,000+. That's the moment to get a contractor, not a handyman.

How to save 30% on doors

  • DIY the interior prehungs. It's the highest-ROI DIY in doors: 30–60 minutes per door with shims and a level, saving $150–$300 each. Watch one video, do a closet first.
  • Buy solid-core, not solid-wood, for interiors. ~$100/door cheaper installed, 90% of the sound and feel benefit.
  • Keep the same sizes. Every non-standard opening is custom money — reframing a 32" opening to 36" costs $300–$600 before the door.
  • Reuse hardware where it's fine. Hinges and passage sets don't wear out; moving them to the new door saves $30–$80 each.
  • Paint it yourself. $120/door pro finish vs. $25 in paint and an afternoon. Doors are the easiest thing in the house to paint well — they're flat and portable.
  • Bundle the job. A carpenter's day rate ($300–$500) covers 6–10 interior doors; one door at a time pays the $150 minimum trip charge every visit.

Manufacturer starting points: Therma-Tru and Masonite for fiberglass entry systems, JELD-WEN across interior and patio lines — compare their catalog prices against your quotes so you know how much is door and how much is labor. The NAHB remodeling data backs up what the calculator assumes: on interior doors, labor is the majority of the installed cost.

Door terms worth knowing

The vocabulary that keeps a quote honest:

  • Slab — the door alone, no frame, no hinges. Hangs in your existing frame.
  • Prehung — door pre-mounted in its own frame with hinges installed. The whole unit goes into the rough opening.
  • Jamb — the frame around the door opening: two side jambs and a head jamb.
  • Rough opening — the framed hole in the wall, typically 2" wider and 2.5" taller than the door.
  • Strike plate — the metal plate on the jamb the latch/deadbolt engages. The actual security point.
  • Backset — distance from door edge to lock bore center: 2-3/8" or 2-3/4". Must match your lockset.
  • Bore — the hole drilled for the lockset. Standard 2-1/8" diameter.
  • Weatherstripping — the compressible seal around exterior doors. Q-lon kerf-in is the good stuff; foam tape is temporary.
  • Threshold / sill — the bottom of an exterior door assembly. Adjustable thresholds seal against the door sweep.
  • Brickmould — exterior trim covering the gap between frame and siding. Often where rot starts.
  • Astragal — the vertical strip sealing the gap between french door pairs.
  • Shims — tapered wedges used to plumb and square the frame. A door is only as good as its shimming.

Door installation cost FAQs

How much does it cost to install a door in 2026?

Interior doors run $150–$500 installed in 2026: a hollow-core slab rehung in an existing frame is $150–$350, a prehung solid-core door is $250–$800. Exterior entry doors run $500–$2,500 installed depending on material — steel is cheapest, fiberglass mid-range, wood premium. French doors run $1,000–$4,000 installed and sliding patio doors $1,000–$3,000. Use the calculator above for your door count, type and material.

Is it cheaper to buy a prehung door or a slab?

The slab itself is cheaper — a hollow-core slab is $50–$150 vs. $150–$250 for the same door prehung. But installation flips the math: hanging a slab in an existing frame takes a skilled carpenter 1–2 hours ($150–$300 labor), while a prehung unit (door + frame + hinges pre-assembled) installs in under an hour ($100–$200 labor). For new openings or damaged frames, prehung is almost always cheaper all-in; slabs only win when the existing frame is perfect.

How much does it cost to install an exterior entry door?

A steel entry door installed runs $500–$1,200, fiberglass $800–$2,500, and wood $1,000–$4,000+ in 2026. The door slab is roughly half the total — the rest is the prehung frame, weatherstripping, threshold, lockset ($30–$300), and 2–4 hours of skilled labor. Sidelights or a transom add $300–$1,000 each. Entry doors have among the best ROI of any remodel — a steel entry replacement recoups roughly its full cost at resale.

How much do french doors cost installed?

French doors cost $1,000–$4,000 installed in 2026. The pair itself runs $500–$2,500 depending on material (fiberglass and wood cost most, steel less) and glass options; installation is $400–$800 for the pair because the wide opening needs careful shimming, and often minor framing work. Sliding patio doors are the cheaper alternative at $1,000–$3,000 installed — one operating panel means simpler hardware and a faster install.

Can I install a door myself?

A prehung interior door is one of the most DIY-friendly projects — a careful homeowner with shims, a level and a nail gun can do it in 1–2 hours and save $150–$300 in labor. Exterior doors are harder: the weatherproofing (sill pan, flashing, insulation) is what keeps water out, and mistakes rot the framing. French and sliding patio doors are heavy two-person jobs. DIY the interior slabs; hire out exterior and patio doors.

How much does it cost to replace a door frame?

Replacing just the frame (jamb) while keeping the door runs $150–$400 in labor plus $30–$80 in materials — worth it when the frame is split or out of square but the door is fine. Full frame replacement with rot repair runs $250–$600 per opening, because rotted jambs usually mean pulling trim, cutting back to solid wood, and sometimes reframing the rough opening. If rot extends into the subfloor or wall framing, costs climb past $1,000.

What is the cheapest interior door option?

A hollow-core molded slab, rehung in the existing frame: $50–$150 for the door plus $100–$200 labor, or under $150 total DIY. It's light, cheap, and fine for closets and low-traffic rooms. The step up worth paying for: solid-core molded doors ($120–$250 slab) — dramatically quieter and more solid-feeling for bedrooms and bathrooms, for about $100 more per door installed.

How long does door installation take?

A pro installs a prehung interior door in 30–60 minutes and a slab rehang in 1–2 hours. Exterior entry doors take 2–4 hours (weatherproofing and lockset take time). French or sliding patio doors take 3–6 hours for the pair — the opening is wide, the units are heavy, and alignment is fussy. Whole-house interior door replacement (10 doors) is typically a 1–2 day job for a two-person crew.
