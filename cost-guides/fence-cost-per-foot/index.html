The per-foot trap A per-foot quote that seems cheap often excludes gates, terrain surcharges, and old-fence removal — which can add 20–40% to the project total. Always ask: "is this per-foot price for the fence run only, and what's extra?" Then compare project totals, not per-foot rates.

How to measure your linear footage

Walk the fence line with a tape (or a measuring wheel — $30 well spent) and add up each straight run. The house itself usually covers one side: a 50×100 ft backyard with the house on one 50-ft side needs roughly 250 linear feet (50 + 100 + 100). Then:

  • Add 5–10% waste for posts, corners, and cutting — order 265–275 ft of material for a 250-ft run.
  • Count gates separately — each gate replaces ~4–12 ft of fence run but costs more than the run it replaces (see below).
  • Note the terrain — slopes, rock, and roots change the per-foot math more than the material choice does (see adders).

DIY vs. pro per foot

DIY (materials only)Pro (installed)150-ft cedar example

Project total$1,800–$3,300$4,200–$6,750

DIY fence saves roughly 40–55% — $2,000–$3,500 on a typical run. But the work is post-setting: digging 20+ holes 2–3 ft deep, setting each post plumb in concrete, and keeping the whole line straight. Everything after the posts is comparatively easy. Rent the two-man auger ($75–$100/day); your back will thank you. And call 811 before you dig — hitting a utility line turns savings into a very bad day.

How fence height changes the per-foot price

October 2026 · industry estimates

Everything above assumes 6-ft privacy — the most quoted height. But height moves the per-foot number more than most people expect, because taller fences need longer posts, deeper footings, and more material per foot:

HeightPer-foot multiplier150-ft cedar exampleWhen it makes sense
4 ft (picket / ranch)×0.70–0.80~$3,975–$4,540Front yards, HOAs with height limits, keeping the dog in
6 ft (privacy standard)×1.00 (baseline)$5,675Backyard privacy — the default choice
7–8 ft×1.15–1.25~$6,525–$7,095Busy streets, close neighbors, pools (check local code max)

Two things to know before going tall. First, many municipalities cap residential fences at 6 ft in backyards and 4 ft in front — an 8-ft fence without a variance is a removal order waiting to happen, so check code before you price it. Second, wind load rises with the square of height: an 8-ft solid privacy fence catches dramatically more wind than a 6-ft one, which is why tall fences need deeper footings and heavier posts — the multiplier above bakes that in. If you need height for privacy but not a fortress, a 6-ft fence with a 1-ft lattice topper gets you 7 ft of screening at a fraction of the structural cost of a full 7-ft solid build.

FAQs

How much does a fence cost per linear foot?

Installed, in 2026 planning ranges: chain-link $13–$25, wood privacy $18–$45, vinyl $30–$55, aluminum $37–$60, composite $44–$72 per linear foot. Gates, slopes, rocky soil, and old-fence removal add on top.

How do I calculate linear feet for a fence?

Measure each side of the area and add them up. A 50×100 ft backyard with the house covering one side needs ~250 linear feet. Add 5–10% for posts, corners, and waste — and count gates separately.

What is the cheapest fence per foot?

Chain-link at $13–$25/ft installed. For privacy on a budget, pine privacy fence at $18–$30/ft is cheapest — but budget for staining every 2–3 years, which narrows the long-term gap with vinyl.

How much does a fence gate cost?

$150–$400 for a walk gate, $400–$1,200 for a double drive gate, installed. Gates cost more per foot than fence runs because of framing and hardware — always get them itemized, not blended into the per-foot rate.

Is it cheaper to build a fence yourself?

Yes — materials run ~40–60% of installed cost, saving $2,000–$3,500 on a typical 150-ft run. The hard part is setting posts plumb; rent an auger, call 811 before digging, and budget 2–4 weekends.
