How much does LVP flooring cost?

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

LVP (luxury vinyl plank) is the best-selling floor in America for a reason: hardwood looks at laminate prices, fully waterproof. Here's what it actually costs per square foot — materials, install, and the quality tiers that matter.

LVP quality tiers: what the price buys

October 2026 · per-sq-ft material prices, US retail typical — labeled ranges

LVP price is really a proxy for two specs: wear-layer thickness (in mil — thousandths of an inch) and core type. Everything else — visuals, bevels, brand — is secondary.

TierMaterial / sq ftWear layerCoreBest for
Budget$2–$36–8 milFlexible / thin rigidRentals, flips, low-traffic bedrooms
Mid-range$3–$512–20 milRigid SPC/WPCThe sweet spot — most homes
Premium$5–$820–30+ milThick rigid, attached padBusy households, pets, "forever" floors

The $3–$5 tier is where the value curve bends: you get a rigid core (dimensionally stable, hides subfloor imperfections better), a wear layer thick enough for 15–20 years of residential life, and visuals good enough that guests guess "engineered hardwood." Below $2/sq ft, the 6–8 mil wear layer dents under furniture and scratches show the pattern layer — fine for a rental refresh, wrong for your own living room. Above $6, you're buying commercial-grade wear layers and designer visuals that most homes will never stress. One more spec worth checking at any tier: the click-lock profile. Thicker locking tabs (found on better rigid-core lines) survive disassembly and reinstallation if you ever need to pull up a section for a plumbing repair — thin-profile budget LVP often can't be relocked once removed, turning a small repair into a re-floor.

The wear-layer rule Ignore the brand; read the wear layer. 12 mil is the residential minimum worth buying, 20 mil is the "buy it for life" threshold. A $4/sq ft plank with a 20-mil wear layer outlasts a $6/sq ft plank with 12 mil — the price tag and the durability are not the same ranking.

SPC vs WPC vs glue-down: the install-method price gap

October 2026 · labor ranges from national installer data — labeled ranges

Two choices set the installed price more than the brand does: the core type and the install method. SPC (stone-plastic composite) is the denser, thinner core — better for basements and rooms with big temperature swings, and the cheapest rigid option. WPC (wood-plastic composite) is thicker and softer underfoot, warmer in bare feet, and runs about $1/sq ft more for the same wear layer. Glue-down LVP skips the click-lock entirely: the planks are adhered straight to the subfloor, which kills the hollow "clicky" sound and holds up best under rolling loads — but the adhesive and the slower install roughly double the labor rate.

Labor ranges cross-checked against 2026 installer guides: click-lock $1.50–$3/sq ft, glue-down $3–$6/sq ft, plus $0.50–$1/sq ft for adhesive on glue-down jobs. The practical rule: unless you have radiant heat, a commercial space, or a subfloor so uneven that floating planks would flex, click-lock SPC is the cheapest installed floor that still looks like wood — and it's the only method most DIYers should attempt.

The 20-year math: cost per year of service

October 2026 · installed cost, one replacement counted where the floor won't last 20 years · ±25%

The sticker price lies. A budget floor that needs replacing at year 10 costs twice — the second install, the second round of removal, the second disruption. Mid-range LVP is the cheapest floor per year of service precisely because it only gets installed once in a 20-year window, while budget LVP gets installed twice and premium LVP's extra durability outlives the house's next remodel anyway.

Where LVP wins and where it doesn't

LVP's waterproof core makes it the default answer for kitchens, bathrooms, laundry rooms, basements, and anywhere pets drink water — places where hardwood and laminate are disqualified on physics, not preference. It's also the value king for whole-house flooring: one material running through living areas, halls, and bedrooms looks intentional and costs half of site-finished hardwood. Where it doesn't win: rooms where you want the refinishable longevity of real wood (a 100-year hardwood floor is still the better "forever" buy if you'll never move), and high-end listings where buyers pay a premium for the words "hardwood floors" — appraisers and buyers still rank real wood above vinyl, and in luxury price bands that perception gap is worth real money. The honest framing: LVP is the best floor for most households and the best investment for most budgets, but it is not a hardwood substitute in the eyes of the resale market. If you're flooring to sell within 5 years, mid-range LVP's cost-per-year-of-service is nearly unbeatable — see the flooring 10- and 20-year cost scenarios for the math.

Room by room: where LVP pays off

Not every room wants the same LVP. The waterproof core earns its keep in wet rooms; the comfort core earns it where bare feet live. Match the spec to the room and you stop overpaying for durability you don't need.

  • Kitchen (~200 sq ft): the highest-value LVP room in the house. Spills, dropped pans, pet bowls — all harmless to SPC. Budget $4–$7/sq ft installed, $800–$1,400 total. Pick glue-down or SPC here; WPC's softer core dents under a heavy fridge.
  • Bathroom (~50 sq ft): small square footage makes premium affordable — $8–$12/sq ft installed is only $400–$600 total, and you get the 22-mil wear layer plus a waterproof core where it matters most. Caulk the perimeter; LVP is waterproof, the subfloor underneath isn't.
  • Basement: SPC rigid core only — temperature swings and slab moisture punish flexible cores. Budget $4–$8/sq ft installed, but check the slab first: moisture mitigation ($1–$2/sq ft) is the hidden line here, and it's cheaper than replacing a failed floor.
  • Bedrooms and living rooms: WPC's thicker, softer core is the comfort pick, and mid-range ($5–$8/sq ft installed) is the sweet spot — you feel the difference barefoot, and the wear layer is thick enough for 20 years of daily traffic.
  • Stairs: the exception that proves the rule. LVP on stairs needs stair-nose pieces ($25–$50 each) plus a labor premium, and the result rarely looks as good as carpet or hardwood. Most installers quote stairs separately — if the stair quote exceeds $1,000, carpet the stairs and LVP the landings.
  • Whole house (1,000+ sq ft): one material through every room looks intentional and buys a volume discount — ask for 5–10% off material on 1,000+ sq ft orders, and expect $4,000–$10,000 installed for mid-range, $5,000–$12,000 for premium.

Quote checklist: 8 questions before you sign

LVP quotes vary more than any other flooring quote because so much of the price hides in prep and extras. Run this list on every bid — two quotes that look $1,000 apart are often the same job with different things included.

  1. Wear layer in mil? Get it in writing — 12 mil minimum for your own home, 20 mil for pets and kids. "Commercial grade" without a mil number means nothing.
  2. SPC or WPC core? SPC for basements, kitchens, and temperature swings; WPC where barefoot comfort matters. Make sure the quote names the core, not just the brand.
  3. Is old-floor removal itemized? Glued-down vinyl or tile demo can exceed the LVP install cost. If removal is blended into one per-sq-ft rate, you can't tell what you're paying.
  4. Subfloor leveling — included or extra? Ask what flatness the installer guarantees (3/16" per 10 ft) and what leveling costs if the slab fails it. A $2–$5/sq ft surprise here is the classic budget-breaker.
  5. Underlayment or attached pad? Many rigid-core lines ship with an attached pad — if the quote charges $0.30–$0.60/sq ft for underlayment on top of that, you're paying twice.
  6. Transitions, stair noses, and trim priced? $30–$80 per transition adds up fast across doorways; stair noses are $25–$50 each. Confirm they're in the total, not "TBD on site."
  7. Waste factor? 10% is standard; 7% is fine for big square rooms. Over 12% on a simple rectangle means you're buying the installer's mistakes.
  8. Warranty — product and labor, separately? The plank warranty (often 25 years residential) is the manufacturer's; the labor warranty (1–2 years is normal) is the installer's. Get both in writing, with the installer's name on the labor one.

Red flags: a quote with no wear-layer spec; "free underlayment" on a line that already has an attached pad; a per-sq-ft rate that drops suspiciously below $3.50 installed (corners are being cut on prep); and any installer who says subfloor prep "probably won't be needed" before measuring flatness. Walk away from the last one — the floor will fail in year two and the warranty won't cover it.

FAQs

How much does LVP flooring cost?

$2–$8 per sq ft for materials plus $1.50–$4 per sq ft for pro installation in 2026 (planning ranges) — roughly $3.50–$12/sq ft all-in. A 300 sq ft living room runs about $1,050–$3,600 installed.

What is a good price per square foot for LVP?

$3–$5/sq ft for the material is the sweet spot: 12–20 mil wear layer, rigid core, convincing visuals. Under $2 the wear layer is too thin for a primary home; over $6 you're paying for commercial-grade specs most homes don't need.

Is LVP cheaper than hardwood?

Dramatically — $3.50–$12/sq ft installed for LVP versus $8–$16+/sq ft for solid hardwood. LVP is also waterproof, which is why it's taken over kitchens, bathrooms, and basements where hardwood can't go.

How long does LVP flooring last?

15–25 years residentially, set by wear-layer thickness: ~15 years at 12 mil, 20–25 at 20 mil in normal use. Fading and deep scratches end LVP's life more often than wear-through.

Can I install LVP myself?

Yes — click-lock LVP is one of the most DIY-friendly floors: score-and-snap cutting, no glue or nails, a 300 sq ft room in a weekend. The critical step is subfloor flatness (within 3/16" per 10 ft) — skip the prep and the click locks fail early.

What does it cost to install LVP in a 1,000 sq ft house?

$4,000–$10,000 installed for mid-range click-lock LVP ($4–$10/sq ft), or $5,000–$12,000 for premium. The range is mostly labor — $1–$2/sq ft higher in dense metros — plus old-floor removal and subfloor leveling if your slab needs it. Ask for a 5–10% volume discount on material for 1,000+ sq ft orders.

Is glue-down or click-lock LVP cheaper?

Click-lock is cheaper: labor runs $1.50–$3/sq ft versus $3–$6/sq ft for glue-down, plus $0.50–$1/sq ft for adhesive. Glue-down only wins for commercial spaces, radiant-heat floors, or heavy rolling loads — for most homes, click-lock SPC costs less and installs faster.
