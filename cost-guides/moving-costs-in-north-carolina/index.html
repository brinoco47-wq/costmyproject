Moving costs in North Carolina

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Moving in North Carolina? Local moves run $1,200–$2,500 for a 2-bedroom home, while long-distance moves price by weight and distance — a 2-bedroom going 1,000+ miles runs $3,500–$6,500. Below: the full tables, what drives North Carolina prices, and how to pay less.

The 30-second version Under 100 miles, North Carolina movers bill by the hour (2-mover crews ~$80–$100/hr nationally, higher in North Carolina's big metros). Over 100 miles, it's flat weight-and-distance pricing under federal FMCSA rules. North Carolina moves price right at national averages in our model — see the methodology note below.

Worked example: 2-bedroom local move in North Carolina

How the table range is built — a 2-bedroom apartment moving across Charlotte (~25 miles):

  • Crew: 3 movers × ~7 hours (load, drive, unload)
  • Effective all-in rate: ~$88/hr (crew + truck + standard equipment, North Carolina-adjusted)
  • Estimated total: 21 crew-hours × $88 ≈ $1,850 — the midpoint of the $1,200–$2,500 range above
  • Your planning range (±25%): $1,400–$2,300

Stairs, long carries, packing services, or a month-end date push you toward the top of the range; a ground-floor unit with everything boxed and a mid-month date pulls toward the bottom. This is a budgeting estimate, not a quote — confirm with a binding written estimate.

What drives the price in North Carolina

  • Triangle and Charlotte growth = high demand — Raleigh-Durham and Charlotte are among the fastest-growing metros in the US — mover demand is strong year-round, and summer books out.
  • Mountain terrain in the west — Asheville-area moves deal with steep driveways, elevation, and winding access — legitimate time-adders on local jobs.
  • Hurricane season on the coast — June–November storms can disrupt coastal Carolina moves. Inland moves are largely unaffected, but timing still matters.
  • Mild winters = workable off-season — Unlike northern states, NC winters rarely stop moves — December–February discounts come without the snow risk.
  • College-town turnover — Chapel Hill, Durham, and Raleigh see big August turnover — student demand spikes prices in college neighborhoods specifically.

How to save money on a North Carolina move

  1. Book summer moves 6+ weeks out in Raleigh/Charlotte — growth markets fill fast.
  2. Avoid late August in college towns; student turnover spikes local prices.
  3. Winter moves are discounted and weather-safe — NC's best value window.
  4. For mountain moves, describe access (grade, driveway length) when quoting.
  5. When getting quotes, describe stairs, elevators, and parking at both ends — access surprises are the number-one reason final bills exceed estimates.

FAQs

What is the cheapest month to move in North Carolina?

December through February. Demand dips and — unlike northern states — winter weather rarely disrupts, so the discount is nearly free.

Why are summer moves so expensive in Raleigh and Charlotte?

Fast population growth keeps mover demand high, and summer is peak family-moving season. Book 6+ weeks ahead or pay the premium.

How much does it cost to move within North Carolina?

A 2-bedroom local move runs near national averages (see table). Charlotte-to-Raleigh (~170 miles) prices as a short long-distance job — hourly locally, weight-and-distance over 100 miles.

Do hurricanes affect North Carolina moves?

Coastal moves June–November carry real storm risk; inland metros are largely unaffected. Ask carriers about weather-delay policies if you're moving to the coast in season.

Is a binding estimate necessary?

For interstate moves, strongly recommended — binding or binding-not-to-exceed in writing. It's the standard protection against delivery-day price growth.
