Moving costs in Ohio

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Moving in Ohio? Local moves run $1,150–$2,400 for a 2-bedroom home, while long-distance moves price by weight and distance — a 2-bedroom going 1,000+ miles runs $3,300–$6,200. Below: the full tables, what drives Ohio prices, and how to pay less.

The 30-second version Under 100 miles, Ohio movers bill by the hour (2-mover crews ~$80–$100/hr nationally, higher in Ohio's big metros). Over 100 miles, it's flat weight-and-distance pricing under federal FMCSA rules. Ohio moves price ~5% below national averages in our model — see the methodology note below.

Worked example: 2-bedroom local move in Ohio

How the table range is built — a 2-bedroom apartment moving across Columbus (~25 miles):

  • Crew: 3 movers × ~7 hours (load, drive, unload)
  • Effective all-in rate: ~$85/hr (crew + truck + standard equipment, Ohio-adjusted)
  • Estimated total: 21 crew-hours × $85 ≈ $1,775 — the midpoint of the $1,150–$2,400 range above
  • Your planning range (±25%): $1,350–$2,200

Stairs, long carries, packing services, or a month-end date push you toward the top of the range; a ground-floor unit with everything boxed and a mid-month date pulls toward the bottom. This is a budgeting estimate, not a quote — confirm with a binding written estimate.

What drives the price in Ohio

  • Below-average labor costs — Ohio mover rates sit below the national average — one of the more affordable states for full-service moves, reflected in the ~5% planning discount in our tables.
  • Winter weather discounts — December–February is cheapest, with lake-effect snow near Cleveland as the wildcard. Carriers discount to keep crews busy.
  • Sprawling, affordable metros — Columbus, Cleveland, and Cincinnati are spread out — local moves can include meaningful drive time, but low hourly rates keep totals reasonable.
  • Manufacturing-city housing stock — Older homes with basements are the norm — basement stairs add carry time on local jobs. Mention it when quoting.
  • Florida corridor is the big lane — Ohio-to-Florida is one of the highest-volume interstate routes in the country — competitive pricing, especially for snowbirds heading south in fall.

Ohio moving regulations, busiest routes, and the Florida corridor

Ohio is one of the most affordable states in the country to move in — below-average labor costs, reasonable demand, no extreme weather premiums. The price action is in the regulations nobody reads and one interstate corridor that dominates everything else.

Who regulates Ohio movers. Moves inside Ohio are regulated by the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio (PUCO) — movers need PUCO authority to operate legally. Verify the number before booking; Ohio's low barriers to entry mean plenty of "two guys and a truck" operations, and the PUCO certificate is what separates them from actual moving companies with insurance and claims processes. Interstate moves fall under federal FMCSA rules — check the USDOT number there. For a state where moving is cheap, the expensive mistake is hiring the cheapest unlicensed crew and paying for it in damaged furniture with no recourse.

The Florida corridor dominates. Ohio → Florida is one of the highest-volume interstate moving routes in the entire country — snowbirds, retirees, and permanent relocations keep it packed. The pattern is seasonal and predictable: southbound demand surges in fall (September–November), northbound in spring (March–May). Book 6+ weeks ahead in season. The flip side is the deal: off-season southbound moves (summer) can price 15–25% below peak because carriers are hungry for loads heading the "wrong" way. If your Florida timeline is flexible, summer is the value window nobody talks about.

Other busy lanes. Columbus ↔ Chicago, Cleveland ↔ Pittsburgh, and Cincinnati ↔ Indianapolis/Louisville all run steady volume. Columbus is the state's growth story — the Intel plant and corporate expansions are pulling inbound relocations, which is tightening crew availability on the high end of the market. Cleveland and Cincinnati are stable, affordable, and well-served; quotes there tend to cluster tightly, so two or three bids is usually enough.

Winter is the value season — with a lake-effect asterisk. December through February is cheapest statewide, but lake-effect snow near Cleveland, Toledo, and the Erie shoreline can shut down a move day with no notice. Build buffer days into winter delivery windows, and don't schedule a Cleveland move around a hard lease deadline in January without a backup plan. The rest of the state — Columbus, Cincinnati, Dayton — sees milder winters and fewer disruptions.

Basements change the labor math. Ohio's older housing stock — Cleveland colonials, Cincinnati Victorians, Dayton bungalows — overwhelmingly has basements, and basement stairs add real carry time: every box and furniture piece makes two trips down narrow, often steep stairwells. Mention basement involvement during the quote walkthrough, not on move day. Crews price what they can see; a surprise full basement discovered at 9am gets priced at the crew's discretion, which is never the favorable direction.

College towns run on their own calendar. Columbus (Ohio State), Athens (Ohio University), Oxford (Miami University), and Cincinnati all see concentrated turnover in August when student leases flip — the same pattern as North Carolina's college towns. Mover demand spikes, prices run 10–15% above normal, and the best crews book out. If your dates flex, late July or early September dodges the worst of it, and landlords managing student housing often have preferred-mover relationships worth asking about before you start calling around.

How to save money on a Ohio move

  1. Winter is the value season — December–February quotes run lowest.
  2. Basement-heavy Ohio homes mean more carry time; declutter the basement first.
  3. On the Ohio-to-Florida lane, book fall dates early — snowbird demand fills trucks.
  4. Compare 3 quotes minimum; Ohio's competitive market rewards shopping.

FAQs

What is the cheapest month to move in Ohio?

December through February. Carriers discount winter dates to fill schedules — just keep a snow buffer, especially near Lake Erie.

How much does a local move cost in Ohio?

A 2-bedroom local move typically lands just under national averages (see the table) — Ohio is one of the more affordable states for full-service moving.

How much does it cost to move from Ohio to Florida?

For a 2-bedroom (~1,000 miles), full-service runs in the mid-four-figures (see table). It's a high-volume lane, so competition keeps pricing honest — still get 3 quotes.

Do Ohio movers charge extra for basements?

Not usually as a line item, but basement stairs add billable hours. A full basement of boxes and furniture is real carry time — factor it in.

Is it cheaper to move within Ohio or out of state?

Dramatically cheaper within Ohio — local moves are hourly and Ohio's rates are below average. Crossing into long-distance (100+ miles) flips you to weight-and-distance pricing, a different game entirely.
