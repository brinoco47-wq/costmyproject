Moving costs in Texas

Advertisement

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Moving in Texas? Local moves run $1,250–$2,600 for a 2-bedroom home, while long-distance moves price by weight and distance — a 2-bedroom going 1,000+ miles runs $3,700–$6,800. Below: the full tables, what drives Texas prices, and how to pay less.

The 30-second version Under 100 miles, Texas movers bill by the hour (2-mover crews ~$80–$100/hr nationally, higher in Texas's big metros). Over 100 miles, it's flat weight-and-distance pricing under federal FMCSA rules. Texas moves price ~5% above national averages in our model — see the methodology note below.

Worked example: 2-bedroom local move in Texas

How the table range is built — a 2-bedroom apartment moving across Houston (~25 miles):

  • Crew: 3 movers × ~7 hours (load, drive, unload)
  • Effective all-in rate: ~$92/hr (crew + truck + standard equipment, Texas-adjusted)
  • Estimated total: 21 crew-hours × $92 ≈ $1,925 — the midpoint of the $1,250–$2,600 range above
  • Your planning range (±25%): $1,450–$2,400

Stairs, long carries, packing services, or a month-end date push you toward the top of the range; a ground-floor unit with everything boxed and a mid-month date pulls toward the bottom. This is a budgeting estimate, not a quote — confirm with a binding written estimate.

What drives the price in Texas

  • Sprawl means drive time — Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, and San Antonio are enormous — a 'local' move can include 45+ minutes of paid drive time each way. Ask whether drive time is billed and at what rate.
  • Summer heat is the peak season — June–August is when everyone moves and when it's 100°F. Crews work slower in extreme heat, and heat-related delays are real — morning start times are worth requesting.
  • Hurricane season on the Gulf Coast — June–November storms can delay Houston and coastal moves. Ask long-distance carriers how they handle weather delays before you sign.
  • No state income tax draws inbound demand — Texas is a top inbound-move state, which keeps carriers busy on popular routes — good for availability outbound, competitive pricing inbound.
  • Rural access adds cost — Long driveways, unpaved roads, and properties outside the metros can trigger long-carry or shuttle fees. Mention access details when getting quotes.

How to save money on a Texas move

  1. If you can choose, move January–February — the cheapest window in Texas by a wide margin.
  2. Request the earliest start time available in summer; crews are faster before the afternoon heat.
  3. Get 3+ quotes — Texas has a deep, competitive mover market and prices vary widely.
  4. Ask about fuel surcharges explicitly on long rural drives; they're a common surprise add-on.

Access is where Texas move-day bills most often grow. A ground-floor house with driveway parking and a 20th-floor downtown apartment are different jobs even at the same distance, and movers charge for the difference. Texas mover rules: the two checks that matter

Texas moves split into two regulatory worlds, and the check that protects you depends on which one your move is in. A USDOT number alone does not cover both.

  • Moving within Texas (intrastate): the mover needs a TxDMV household-goods certificate. A USDOT number is a federal identifier, not a Texas license — check the certificate on TxDMV's Truck Stop motor-carrier lookup, and confirm the legal name on the certificate matches the name on your quote and on the truck.
  • Moving across a state line (interstate): the mover needs FMCSA operating authority. Check it in FMCSA's mover search by name or USDOT number, and confirm the company is a carrier, not a broker, with insurance on file.
  • The FMCSA 110% rule (interstate, non-binding estimates): the mover cannot demand more than 110% of the estimate at delivery to release your goods. On a $5,000 estimate that is $5,500 at the curb; any balance above that must be billed later, no sooner than 30 days after delivery. A binding estimate, by contrast, fixes the price for the move it describes.
  • Valuation is not insurance: the default released-value coverage pays only 60 cents per pound per item — a 50-pound TV is covered for $30. Full-value protection costs more but requires the mover to repair, replace, or pay current market value. For a Texas move with electronics or antiques, price full-value protection in the quote rather than discovering released value after damage.

When to move in Texas: the seasonal calendar

Price and crew quality both follow the calendar. Summer is peak season in every Texas metro, and the cheapest weeks of the year are the ones nobody wants.

Texas moving quote checklist

  1. Get three written estimates, at least one binding, and compare the inventory line by line — not just the bottom line.
  2. Verify the license: TxDMV certificate for a Texas-only move, FMCSA authority for a cross-state move, same legal name everywhere.
  3. Disclose stairs, elevators, parking, and bulky items in the survey — every undisclosed access item is a move-day surcharge waiting to happen.
  4. Ask whether drive time, fuel surcharges, and packing materials are included or added, and get the answer in writing.
  5. Confirm the delivery window in writing for long-distance moves, and what happens if the truck is late or weather delays it.
  6. Never sign a blank inventory or bill of lading, and never pay a large cash deposit up front — reputable Texas movers bill mostly on delivery.

FAQs

What is the cheapest month to move in Texas?

January and February. Demand craters after the holidays and before spring, so carriers discount aggressively. Summer (June–August) is the most expensive window.

How much does it cost to move from Texas to California?

For a 2-bedroom home (~1,400 miles), full-service movers typically run in the mid-four-figures (see the table above). Weight is the price driver — decluttering before a TX-to-CA move pays for itself.

Do movers work in Texas summer heat?

Yes, but expect earlier start times, more breaks, and slightly longer job times. Reputable crews plan for it; be wary of anyone who won't commit to a morning start in July.

Are there extra fees for rural Texas moves?

Often. Long carries, unpaved access roads, and large properties can add shuttle or long-carry fees. Describe your property honestly when quoting — surprises on move day cost more.

Should I tip Texas movers?

Tipping is customary but optional: roughly $20–$40 per mover for a half-day job, more for full-day or exceptional work. Cash at the end, per mover.

How do I check a Texas mover is licensed?

For a move inside Texas, ask for the mover's TxDMV household-goods certificate number and look it up on TxDMV's Truck Stop motor-carrier lookup — the legal name there must match your quote and the truck. For a move across a state line, check FMCSA's mover search by name or USDOT number instead. A USDOT number alone does not prove Texas licensing.

What does the FMCSA 110% rule mean for my move?

On an interstate move with a non-binding estimate, the mover cannot demand more than 110% of the estimate at delivery to release your goods — on a $5,000 estimate, that is $5,500 at the curb. Any balance above 110% must be billed later, no sooner than 30 days after delivery. A binding estimate fixes the price for the move it describes instead.

Is full-value protection worth it on a Texas move?

Usually yes if you own electronics, art, or antiques. Default released-value coverage pays only 60 cents per pound per item, so a 50-pound TV is covered for $30. Full-value protection costs roughly 1–2% of declared value and requires the mover to repair, replace, or pay current market value.
