Walk-In Shower Cost 2026

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

A walk-in shower costs $1,500 for a prefab unit and $15,000+ for a large custom-tiled one — with most homeowners spending $6,000–$10,000. This guide compares prefab vs. custom tile, prices glass doors, explains the plumbing and waterproofing underneath, and answers the tub-removal question honestly.

The prefab case is stronger than design culture admits: it's genuinely waterproof (no grout to fail, no membrane to misinstall), it's done in two days, and modern direct-to-stud panels in large formats look far better than the fiberglass tubs of the 1990s. The custom case is about exactly fitting the space — curbless entries for aging in place, benches, niches, and the visual calm of continuous tile. If the budget is tight, a $3,000 prefab with a $1,200 glass door beats a $6,000 tile job cut corners on waterproofing every time. And if you're on the fence, price both: get one prefab quote and one tile quote for the same footprint. The $3,000–$5,000 gap between them is the true cost of "custom" — then decide with open eyes whether the look is worth it to you.

What is the average cost of a walk-in shower?

The national average lands at about $7,000–$8,000 installed in 2026. Three independent 2026 benchmarks converge: HomeAdvisor's shower-remodel average is ~$7,000 (typical range $2,000–$9,000), Angi's new-shower installation average is $8,204 (most projects $4,448–$12,372), and Angi's July 2026 walk-in guide puts common projects at $6,000–$12,000. The average is pulled up by large custom jobs — most real projects cluster at $6,000–$10,000, which is the number to budget against. Use the table at the top to see where your project lands in that band.

All figures here are October 2026 US installed prices (±25%), labor included at national-average rates.

Per-square-foot math is misleading on showers: a 32×32 prefab at ~$1,150 works out to roughly $210/sq ft, while a bigger combo at ~$3,000 is only ~$240/sq ft — because most of the money is fixed (demolition, drain, valve, waterproofing, labor days), not tile by the foot. Size raises the material and tile-setting hours on custom builds, but on prefab swaps a 36×60 often costs barely more than a 32×32 because the install labor is nearly identical. Custom work scales harder: penny or mosaic tile on a 4×6 floor multiplies setting time.

Labor: the 40–60% line item

Angi's 2026 data puts labor at 40–60% of total shower installation cost — on a $7,000 average job, that's $2,800–$4,200 going to hands, not materials. The going trade rates:

  • Plumber: $45–$200/hr — drain relocation, valve replacement, supply lines ($600–$1,600 to add shower plumbing per Angi)
  • Tile setter: $30–$120/hr, or $3–$15 per sq ft of tile laid — the single biggest labor lever on custom jobs
  • Bathroom contractor: $400–$2,000 for the install crew, excluding materials, per Angi's walk-in guide
  • Electrician: $50–$120/hr — only needed if adding a light or exhaust fan

The practical read: labor quotes vary more than material quotes, which is why three itemized bids matter more here than on any other fixture. The biggest labor savings are free — keep the drain and valve where they are — and the biggest labor risk is the cheapest tile bid, because a bad waterproofing job is a second remodel.

How long does a walk-in shower take to install?

  • Prefab / kit swap: 1–2 days. One-day acrylic replacement systems (overlaid tub-shower units) genuinely exist — the waterproofing is manufactured, so there's nothing to cure.
  • Custom tile shower: 1–3 weeks — waterproofing membrane cure times and tile setting pace the job, not the plumber.
  • Curbless / zero-threshold: add roughly a week for subfloor recess work.
  • Glass door: always the tail of the timeline — 2–3 weeks fabrication after final measurement on finished tile.

Permits average ~$250 per Angi's 2026 figures and are usually required when plumbing moves; in strict municipalities the permit inspection cycle can add several days to the schedule. If you need the bathroom back fast, that's an argument for the prefab path.

Two rules that save real money and heartbreak: measure after tile is set (walls move during tiling; pre-ordered glass that doesn't fit is a $1,500 wall ornament), and budget 2–3 weeks fabrication — the glass is the last thing installed, so plan the project timeline around it, not the reverse. A shower curtain ($30) covers the gap fine.

What's underneath: plumbing and waterproofing

The visible shower is maybe 60% of the job cost; the invisible 40% decides whether it lasts 5 years or 25. The non-negotiables:

  • Waterproofing membrane ($300–$800) — liquid membrane or sheet membrane over cement backer board, flood-tested before tile. Tile and grout are not waterproof; the membrane is the shower. Skip it and you'll remodel twice.
  • Shower pan — prefab foam pans with integrated slope ($200–$500) have largely replaced hand-packed mud pans; they're faster and more consistent. Either works if sloped 1/4" per foot to the drain.
  • Drain — standard center drain ($100–$200) vs. linear drain ($400–$900). Linear drains enable curbless, single-slope floors and large-format tile — the modern look — at 3–4× the drain cost.
  • Valve — a pressure-balancing or thermostatic valve ($150–$400 for the valve, $500–$1,000 installed with tile work). If you're opening the wall anyway, replace the 20-year-old valve — it's a $200 part that requires demolishing tile to reach later.
  • Body sprays and rain heads ($800–$2,500+ with plumbing) — the luxury line items. Each additional outlet needs supply lines and sometimes a larger water heater consideration; they also roughly double water use.

Tub-to-shower conversion: cost and the resale question

Converting a tub alcove to a walk-in shower typically adds $1,000–$3,000 to the shower cost: tub removal and disposal ($300–$600), drain relocation ($500–$1,500 — the tub drain and shower drain rarely align), and subfloor repair where the tub apron was ($200–$600). All-in, a tub-to-walk-in conversion in the existing alcove runs roughly $7,000–$12,000 for custom tile.

The resale question matters more than the cost: in a home's only full bathroom, removing the tub narrows your buyer pool — families with small children filter for tubs, and some appraisers won't count a tub-less bath as a full bath. In a master bath where another tub exists in the house, a walk-in is usually a resale positive (accessibility + modern appeal). The rule: keep at least one tub in the house; convert freely after that.

Curbless and accessible showers

Curbless (zero-threshold) entries are the fastest-growing shower request — driven by aging-in-place, not just aesthetics. They cost $2,000–$4,000 more than a curbed shower: the floor must be recessed or built up to create slope without a curb, which means subfloor surgery ($800–$2,000), a linear drain ($400–$900), and full-room waterproofing extending beyond the shower footprint ($500–$1,000). Done right, they're the most future-proof money in the bathroom — a curbless shower with blocking for grab bars ($100–$300 during construction) is a 30-year solution. Done wrong (insufficient slope), they're a flood. This is not the place for the cheapest bid.

How to keep it under control

  • Keep the drain where it is. Moving a shower drain means cutting the subfloor and rerouting — $500–$1,500 for zero visual gain.
  • Prefab pan + tiled walls. The hybrid that splits the difference: a $350 foam pan (guaranteed slope, fast) with custom tile walls — most of the look, much less risk than a mud pan.
  • One glass panel, not three. A fixed panel + open entry (no door) saves $900–$2,000 and suits showers 4+ feet deep. Under 4 feet, you need the door — water travels.
  • Niche during framing, not after. A tiled niche costs $200–$400 when planned; cutting one into finished tile costs triple and looks it.
  • Replace the valve while the wall is open. A $200 valve now vs. demolishing $2,000 of tile later is the easiest insurance in remodeling.

Walk-in shower FAQs

How much does a walk-in shower cost in 2026?

$1,500–$4,000 for a prefab unit installed, $6,000–$10,000 for a custom tiled shower, $10,000–$15,000+ for large luxury builds with frameless glass and stone. Glass doors alone add $900–$2,000.

Is it cheaper to install a walk-in shower or a tub-shower combo?

A tub-shower combo ($2,500 installed) is cheaper than any walk-in. Even a prefab walk-in runs $1,500–$4,000 before glass; a custom tiled walk-in is $6,000+ — two to three times the combo.

Does removing a tub to install a walk-in shower hurt resale?

In the home's only full bath, yes — it narrows the buyer pool and some appraisers won't count it as a full bath. In a master with another tub in the house, a walk-in usually helps resale.

How much does a frameless glass shower door cost?

$900–$2,000 installed, depending on size, glass thickness, and hardware. Measure after tile is set and allow 2–3 weeks fabrication — the glass is always the last thing installed.

What is the cheapest way to get a walk-in shower?

A prefab acrylic base ($300–$800) with direct-to-stud panels ($500–$1,200) and a framed glass door ($400–$800) — $1,500–$4,000 installed. Waterproof on day one at a fraction of tile cost.

What is the average cost of a walk-in shower?

About $7,000–$8,000 installed in 2026 — HomeAdvisor's shower-remodel average is ~$7,000 and Angi's new-shower average is $8,204. Most real projects cluster at $6,000–$10,000.

How much does a 36×60 walk-in shower cost?

A prefab 36×60 swap runs $1,200–$4,200 installed; the same footprint in custom tile is $6,000–$10,000. The fixed costs (demo, drain, valve, waterproofing) are nearly identical either way.

How much does labor cost for a walk-in shower?

Labor is 40–60% of the total — $2,800–$4,200 on a $7,000 job. Plumbers run $45–$200/hr, tile setters $30–$120/hr or $3–$15 per sq ft, and a bathroom contractor charges $400–$2,000 excluding materials.

Can a walk-in shower be installed in one day?

One-day installs are real, but only for prefab acrylic replacement systems — the waterproofing is manufactured, so there's nothing to cure. Custom tile showers need 1–3 weeks, and custom glass still takes 2–3 weeks to fabricate after final measurement.

Do I need a permit for a walk-in shower?

Usually yes when plumbing moves — permits average ~$250, and inspection cycles can add days in strict municipalities. Like-for-like kit swaps often skip the permit, but confirm with your city.
