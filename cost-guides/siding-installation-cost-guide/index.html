Siding Installation Cost Guide 2026: Labor, Tear-Off, Wrap & Permits

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

The siding material is only half the quote. This guide prices the other half: pro labor by material, tear-off, house wrap, trim packages, permits — plus the DIY decision and the red flags that mark a bad bid.

The five cost drivers, in order of impact

  1. Material choice — vinyl to brick veneer spans roughly 3× on installed cost per sqft. Nothing else moves the number this much.
  2. Install labor complexity — fiber cement and brick need specialized crews at 2–3× vinyl's labor rate. Multi-story adds ~10–20% for staging.
  3. Tear-off + sheathing repair — ~$1/sqft for removal, plus whatever rot the crew finds underneath (~$75–$100/sheet). Budget a contingency.
  4. Trim, soffit & fascia — $1,000–$3,000. Often quoted as an allowance; vague allowances are where margins hide.
  5. House wrap + flashing details — ~$0.75/sqft for wrap; flashing around windows, doors, and roof-wall intersections is skilled labor that cheap bids skip.

Tear-off: the line item people forget to budget

Stripping old siding runs $0.50–$1.50 per sqft ($1,000–$3,000 on a typical home) — and it's the most valuable demolition you'll ever pay for, because it exposes the sheathing. What crews commonly find:

  • Rotted sheathing around windows and at the bottom course — ~$75–$100 per sheet installed to replace.
  • Missing or failed house wrap — which is exactly why you're replacing the wrap now.
  • Old asbestos siding (pre-1980s homes) — requires licensed abatement, priced per project, not per sqft. Get a specialist quote.

Keep a 10–15% contingency for sheathing repair on any tear-off job. Quotes that assume "sheathing is fine" without looking are guessing.

House wrap: don't skip it

The weather-resistive barrier behind your siding costs ~$0.50–$1.00/sqft installed (~$2,000 on a typical home) and it's the cheapest insurance in the whole project. It sheds wind-driven rain that gets past the siding and lets vapor escape from inside. A tear-off is the only economical time to replace it — doing it later means removing the siding you just paid for.

Trim, soffit & fascia: the $1,000–$3,000 question

Corners, window/door surrounds, soffits (under-eave panels), and fascia (the board behind the gutter) are usually quoted as a package. Two things to know:

  • Prefinished trim (PVC or fiber-cement boards) costs more upfront but never needs painting — worth it on high-visibility faces.
  • Vague "trim allowance" lines are where lowball bids hide margin. Ask for the linear footage and material specified, or the allowance becomes a change order later.

Permits: $200–$500, and non-negotiable

Most municipalities require a siding permit ($200–$500 typical). Unpermitted siding can stall a future home sale when the buyer's inspector asks for the permit record. Any contractor who suggests skipping the permit is telling you who they are.

The DIY question: vinyl yes, fiber cement no

Siding is more DIY-feasible than roofing, but the material decides:

  • Vinyl — genuinely DIY-able. Lightweight panels, interlocking system, basic tools. A careful homeowner can do a single-story ranch over several weekends.
  • Engineered wood — advanced DIY. Heavier, and every cut edge must be sealed or the warranty voids.
  • Fiber cement — hire it out. Silica dust requires respirators and dust-collecting saws; 2.5-lb/sqft panels are a two-person lift all day; flashing details are unforgiving.
  • Brick veneer — masonry work. Not a DIY project unless you are, in fact, a mason.

6 quote red flags

  1. Lump-sum quote with no line items. No material brand, no per-sqft labor, no tear-off line — you can't compare it to anything, which is the point.
  2. "We don't need a permit for siding." Almost always false, and it tells you about their insurance too.
  3. No mention of house wrap or flashing. The two details that determine whether your walls stay dry. Their absence is a scope cut, not a savings.
  4. Full payment upfront. Standard is a deposit (often 1/3), progress payments, and a holdback until final walkthrough.
  5. No manufacturer certification for fiber cement. Uncertified installers void the 30-year product warranty — ask for the credential.
  6. "We can start tomorrow." Good siding crews book weeks out. Immediate availability in peak season means nobody else hired them.

How to compare bids honestly

Get three bids, then normalize them to the same scope before comparing prices:

  • Same material brand and product line (not just "fiber cement" — which fiber cement)
  • Tear-off: included or excluded? Disposal fees included?
  • House wrap: brand specified, or "we'll wrap it" hand-waving?
  • Trim/soffit/fascia: linear footage and material, not an allowance
  • Flashing details at windows, doors, and roof-wall intersections — in writing
  • Warranty: manufacturer (years, transferable?) + workmanship (years, in writing?)

Run each bid's scope through the siding cost calculator — if a quote lands far outside your ±25% planning range with the same inputs, one of you is missing something. Find out which before signing.

Siding installation FAQs

How much does it cost to have siding professionally installed?

Install labor alone: vinyl $1.50–$4/sqft, engineered wood $3–$5.50, fiber cement and cedar $4–$7, brick veneer $4.50–$9. Labor is typically 40–55% of the total quote; multi-story adds ~10–20% for staging.

How much does it cost to remove old siding?

About $0.50–$1.50 per sqft of wall ($1,000–$3,000 typical home). Keep a 10–15% contingency for sheathing repair — rot around windows and the bottom course is common.

Is house wrap necessary when replacing siding?

Yes. At ~$0.50–$1.00/sqft it's the cheapest insurance in the project, and a tear-off is the only economical time to install it. Skipping it to save ~$2,000 is false economy.

What should a siding quote include?

Material brand/line, labor per sqft, tear-off and disposal, house wrap, trim/soffit/fascia scope, flashing details, permit fees, payment schedule, and both warranties. Lump sums without these lines can't be compared.

How long does siding installation take?

One to two weeks for a typical home: 1–2 days tear-off and wrap, 3–7 days siding and trim, plus detailing. Brick veneer and complex multi-story homes can run 2–4 weeks.
