The takeaway: vinyl is the cheapest siding you can own over 25 years — full stop. Brick veneer is the surprise runner-up: the highest sticker price, but a 75-year life means you buy it once. Material by material: the fine print

Vinyl — the value king

PVC panels at $3.50–$7.50 installed, 20–40 year life, essentially zero maintenance beyond a yearly wash. The most DIY-friendly siding — lightweight, interlocking, forgiving. Downsides: melts in extreme heat/fire, dents in hail, fades over decades, and looks like vinyl. Rational choice for most budgets.

Engineered wood — the wood look without the wood work

Treated wood-strand panels (LP SmartSide-style) at $5.50–$10 installed, 20–40 year life. Looks like real wood, resists termites and rot far better than cedar, needs repainting every 10–15 years. The middle child: nicer than vinyl, less fussy than cedar, less fireproof than fiber cement.

Fiber cement — the armor

Cement-sand-cellulose boards (Hardie-style) at $7–$12 installed, 30–50 year life, Class A non-combustible. Shrugs off hail that dents vinyl, won't burn, holds paint 15–25 years. The catch: 2.5 lbs/sqft panels, silica dust when cutting (pro-only install for most homeowners), and the premium is real. Worth it in fire and hail zones.

Cedar / wood — the aesthetic tax

Real wood at $8–$14 installed with a 20–40 year life — but only with staining every 3–5 years (~$2,500 each, estimate). Fire ratings lag, and some wildfire-zone insurers surcharge it. Beautiful on the right house; the most expensive siding in this table over 25 years.

Brick veneer — the century siding

$9.50–$18 installed, 50–100 year life, Class A fire, basically permanent. A thin brick skin over standard framing (no footing needed — that's full brick, a different project). Plan one mortar repointing around year 25–30 (~$3,000, estimate). Highest sticker, second-lowest 25-year cost.

How to choose in 60 seconds

  • Tightest budget or rental? Vinyl. Cheapest today and cheapest over 25 years.
  • Wildfire or hail zone? Fiber cement or brick veneer — then call your insurer about the discount.
  • Want wood looks without wood maintenance? Engineered wood.
  • Forever home, classic look? Brick veneer — buy it once, repoint it once.
  • Historic district or cedar required? Cedar — budgeted with eyes open about staining costs.

Siding material FAQs

What is the cheapest siding material over 25 years?

Vinyl, by a wide margin — about $10,900 for a 2,500-sqft wall over 25 years (installed + near-zero maintenance). Brick veneer is second (~$13,800) thanks to its 50–100-year lifespan. Cedar is last (~$34,400) because of repeated staining.

Is fiber cement siding worth it over vinyl?

On pure cost, no — vinyl is roughly 40% cheaper over 25 years. Fiber cement earns its premium elsewhere: Class A fire rating, hail-proof impact resistance, and 70–85% resale ROI. In fire or hail zones, insurance discounts and durability can close the gap.

How long does each siding material last?

Vinyl 20–40 years; engineered wood 20–40; fiber cement 30–50; cedar 20–40 (only with regular staining); brick veneer 50–100+. Climate, installation quality, and whether maintenance actually happens move these numbers.

Does new siding increase home value?

Yes — siding replacement is one of the highest-ROI exterior projects. Industry guides report fiber cement at 70–85%+ cost recovery and vinyl around 60–70%. Curb appeal also shortens time on market.

Which siding is best for fire-prone areas?

Fiber cement and brick veneer — both non-combustible, Class A rated. Vinyl melts and cedar burns; some wildfire-zone insurers surcharge wood siding. Many insurers discount fiber cement.
