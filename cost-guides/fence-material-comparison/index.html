Material-by-material notes

Wood privacy

The default American backyard fence. Pressure-treated pine is cheapest ($18–$30/LF installed); cedar ($25–$45) resists rot naturally and looks better longer. The catch is maintenance: skip staining and a 20-year fence becomes a 12-year fence. In wet climates, set posts in concrete and keep pickets 2 inches off the soil.

Vinyl / PVC

The "buy once" option. Costs ~40% more than wood installed, but 25–30 year life with essentially zero upkeep. Quality varies wildly — cheap vinyl chalks and cracks; look for UV inhibitors and thicker walls. Repairs mean replacing whole panels, not patching.

Chain-link

Cheapest installed and nearly indestructible, but zero privacy (add slats for ~$3–$5/LF more). Galvanized lasts 20–25 years; vinyl-coated (black/green) costs a bit more and looks far better. Best value for large lots and dog runs.

Aluminum ornamental

The pool-code and HOA favorite: elegant, 30+ year life, zero maintenance. Not a privacy fence (it's see-through by design) — privacy inserts exist but push cost toward vinyl. Won't rust like steel or wrought iron.

Composite

Wood look, plastic durability, premium price. 25–30 year life, no staining ever. The newest category — fewer installers, so get multiple bids. Makes sense when you want wood's look with vinyl's maintenance (or lack of it).

Material FAQs

Does a fence increase home value?

A well-built fence adds curb appeal and is a selling point for buyers with kids or dogs, but appraisers rarely credit full cost — expect partial recovery, not profit. Chain-link can hurt perceived value in upscale neighborhoods; wood and vinyl read as upgrades.

Wood vs. vinyl: which is really cheaper?

Over 15 years on a 150-LF fence: wood ~$7,475 (install + staining), vinyl ~$6,900 (install + ~$0 maintenance). Vinyl wins if you stay put; wood wins if you sell within ~7 years, before staining costs pile up. Full math in our head-to-head.

What's the longest-lasting fence material?

Aluminum (30+ years) and vinyl/composite (25–30 years) outlast wood (15–20) and chain-link (20–25) — assuming each gets its (minimal) required care. In coastal salt air, aluminum and vinyl beat everything.
