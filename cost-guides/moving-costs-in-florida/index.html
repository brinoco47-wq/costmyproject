Moving costs in Florida

Advertisement

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Moving in Florida? Local moves run $1,300–$2,750 for a 2-bedroom home, while long-distance moves price by weight and distance — a 2-bedroom going 1,000+ miles runs $3,850–$7,150. Below: the full tables, what drives Florida prices, and how to pay less.

The 30-second version Under 100 miles, Florida movers bill by the hour (2-mover crews ~$80–$100/hr nationally, higher in Florida's big metros). Over 100 miles, it's flat weight-and-distance pricing under federal FMCSA rules. Florida moves price ~10% above national averages in our model — see the methodology note below.

Worked example: 2-bedroom local move in Florida

How the table range is built — a 2-bedroom apartment moving across Orlando (~25 miles):

  • Crew: 3 movers × ~7 hours (load, drive, unload)
  • Effective all-in rate: ~$96/hr (crew + truck + standard equipment, Florida-adjusted)
  • Estimated total: 21 crew-hours × $96 ≈ $2,025 — the midpoint of the $1,300–$2,750 range above
  • Your planning range (±25%): $1,500–$2,550

Stairs, long carries, packing services, or a month-end date push you toward the top of the range; a ground-floor unit with everything boxed and a mid-month date pulls toward the bottom. This is a budgeting estimate, not a quote — confirm with a binding written estimate.

What drives the price in Florida

  • Snowbird season inverts the calendar — October–April is Florida's peak: snowbirds arrive, demand spikes, and prices follow. Summer — miserable heat and all — is the discount season, the opposite of most states.
  • Hurricane season overlaps the cheap months — June–November storms can delay or reschedule moves. The August–September discount comes with genuine weather risk — build a buffer into your dates.
  • High-rise condo rules — South Florida and Tampa high-rises routinely require elevator reservations, certificates of insurance, and restricted move hours. Buildings enforce this strictly; your mover can't override it.
  • Heat and humidity slow crews — Summer moves mean slower work and mandatory breaks. It's manageable, but don't expect winter productivity in August.
  • Toll roads on long-distance routes — Florida's turnpike system adds real cost to long hauls — confirm whether tolls are included in your quote or billed extra.

Moving in Florida's big metros: what changes

The statewide ranges above are the starting point — the metro you're moving in (or out of) changes the job in ways the price tables don't show. Same crew size, same boxes, different friction:

  • Miami / South Florida — high-rise rules dominate: elevator reservations, certificates of insurance, weekday-only windows, and tight loading docks. Street parking for a 26-ft truck is its own negotiation. Budget extra time, not just extra money — a missed elevator slot can push the whole job a day.
  • Orlando — apartment-complex moves and tourist-corridor traffic (I-4, the attractions zone) set the pace. Gated communities add check-in time; complexes often restrict truck size and hours just like condos do.
  • Tampa Bay — bridges and causeways shape routing, and Pinellas County is one of the four Florida counties with its own local moving ordinance (see the rules section below). Cross-bay moves price the bridge time into the day.
  • Jacksonville — the most spread-out metro in the state. Drive time between origin and destination is the variable: a "local" Jacksonville move can run 40+ miles, which is why the worked example's 25-mile Orlando assumption doesn't transfer directly.

None of this changes the statewide ranges — it explains where inside the range your quote lands. A ground-floor Jacksonville move and a 20th-floor Miami move are both "local 2-bedroom" jobs with very different days.

Florida mover rules: check before you book

Florida regulates intrastate movers at the state level — this is the check that separates a real mover from a truck-and-a-website:

  • FDACS registration is mandatory. Household movers operating within Florida must register with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services under Chapter 507 of the Florida Statutes. The registration (IM) number must appear on their advertising, vehicles, and contracts.
  • Written estimates are required. A registered mover must give you a written estimate and a written contract with rates and fees disclosed — no handshake pricing. Ask whether the estimate is binding (fixed price) or non-binding (final price can move with actual weight/services).
  • Insurance is mandatory. Florida movers must carry cargo liability and motor vehicle insurance. Ask what liability coverage is included by default and what full-value coverage costs — default coverage is often minimal.
  • Four counties add local rules. Broward, Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, and Pinellas counties have their own local moving ordinances — movers working there must also register locally and follow county rules.
  • Interstate moves switch regulators. Crossing a state line puts the job under federal FMCSA rules: the carrier needs an active USDOT number, and the federal 110% rule caps what you owe at delivery on a non-binding estimate (see our hidden moving fees guide).
  • Complaints go to FDACS. Problems with a Florida mover: contact the Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (1-800-HELP-FLA) or check the mover's registration status before you book, not after.

Florida moving quote checklist

Run every quote through this list before you sign — it catches the extras that turn a $2,000 estimate into a $2,600 bill:

  1. Binding or non-binding? Get the answer in writing, with the estimate type named.
  2. Building paperwork sorted: elevator reservation + certificate of insurance, confirmed a week early.
  3. Tolls included or billed extra? Florida's turnpike system adds real cost on long hauls.
  4. Weather-delay policy in writing — especially for June–November moves.
  5. Stairs, long carries, and bulky items (pianos, safes) priced in the quote, not discovered on move day.
  6. Liability coverage: what's included by default, and what full-value coverage costs.
  7. Delivery window in writing for long-distance moves (1–3 days regionally, 4–7 days around 1,000 miles).

How to save money on a Florida move

  1. Moving May–September? That's Florida's value season — just watch the hurricane forecast.
  2. Book your building's freight elevator the same day you book the mover, not after.
  3. Get the COI (certificate of insurance) sorted a week early — condos reject movers without one.
  4. Ask for a binding estimate; snowbird-season demand makes non-binding quotes drift upward.

FAQs

Why do Florida moving prices spike in winter?

Snowbird season (October–April) floods the state with seasonal residents. Mover demand surges while the rest of the country is in its off-season, so Florida prices peak exactly when other states bottom out.

What is the cheapest month to move in Florida?

August or September — peak heat, peak hurricane risk, lowest demand. If your dates are flexible and you can tolerate the weather gamble, that's the value window.

Do Florida condos really require elevator reservations?

Most high-rises do, plus a certificate of insurance from the mover and restricted hours (often weekdays only). Confirm your building's move policy before getting quotes — it shapes the whole job.

How do hurricanes affect scheduled moves?

Carriers may delay or reschedule around named storms, and delivery windows stretch. Ask about the carrier's weather-delay policy in writing, especially June–November.

Is it cheaper to move to Florida in summer?

Usually yes for the move itself — but weigh it against the weather risk and the fact that you'll be unpacking in 95°F heat. For many people the shoulder months (May, late September) are the sweet spot.

How do I verify a Florida mover is registered?

Check the mover's FDACS registration: intrastate movers must be registered with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services, and their IM registration number must appear on advertising, vehicles, and contracts. For interstate moves, also check the carrier's USDOT number with FMCSA.

Do I need a binding estimate for a Florida move?

Florida movers must give you a written estimate either way. A binding estimate fixes the price; a non-binding estimate can change with the actual weight and services. In snowbird season, when demand lets quotes drift, binding is the safer choice — get the estimate type named in writing.

Which Florida counties have extra mover rules?

Broward, Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, and Pinellas counties have their own local moving ordinances on top of state FDACS registration. If your move touches one of them, confirm the mover is registered locally as well as with the state.
