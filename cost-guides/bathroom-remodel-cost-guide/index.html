Bathroom Remodel Cost Guide 2026

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

A bathroom remodel costs $3,000 for a cosmetic refresh and $75,000+ for luxury — with most full remodels landing between $12,000 and $25,000. This guide breaks down cost by scope tier and bathroom size, shows exactly where the money goes, and works a real 5×8 example line by line.

Bathroom remodel cost by scope (2026)

ScopeTypical rangeWhat changes
Cosmetic refresh$3,000–$8,000Paint, fixtures, mirror, lighting, hardware, toilet seat — tub, tile, and layout stay
Mid-range full remodel$12,000–$25,000New tub/shower, tile, vanity, toilet, fixtures — same layout, professional install
Major remodel$20,000–$45,000Gut to the studs, layout tweaks, new plumbing runs, premium tile and glass
Luxury$35,000–$75,000+Stone tile, frameless glass walk-in, heated floors, double vanity, designer fixtures

Ranges assume a 35–60 sq ft bathroom — the classic hall bath or a modest master. Per-square-foot math is misleading in bathrooms: a toilet costs the same whether the room is 40 or 120 sq ft, so small baths run $300–$625/sq ft while large masters can drop under $250/sq ft at the same finish level. All figures are October 2026 US planning estimates (±25%); use the bathroom remodel cost calculator for your exact size and choices.

Where the money goes

On a typical mid-range remodel, the budget splits roughly like this:

CategoryShareNotes
Tub / shower + tile~35%The two most labor-intensive lines; tile setting is slow, skilled work
Labor (all trades)~25%Plumber, electrician, tile setter, drywall — sequenced in 40 sq ft
Plumbing / electrical / drywall~15%Valves, venting, GFCI circuits, exhaust fan, waterproofing
Vanity + top~10%The furniture piece of the room; tops and hardware add up
Fixtures + toilet~12%Faucet, showerhead, lighting, mirror, toilet
Paint + accessories~3%The finishing 3% that makes the other 97% look intentional

The single most useful budgeting insight: tile and the wet area drive the total. A prefab tub-shower combo ($2,500 installed) versus a tiled walk-in shower ($6,000+) is a $3,500+ decision before you've picked a single faucet. Make that call first; everything else is fine-tuning.

Worked example: 5×8 hall bath, mid-range

The most common US bathroom is the 5×8 hall bath — 40 sq ft, tub on one end, toilet and vanity along the wall. Here's a mid-range full remodel, line by line (October 2026 planning estimates):

Line itemEstimate
Tub-shower combo, installed$2,500
Standard toilet, installed$450
Single vanity, semi-custom, installed with top$1,800
Porcelain tile, 100 sq ft @ $14 (floor + surround)$1,400

Plumbing / electrical / drywall allowance$2,500
Labor (35% of materials)$3,448
Total$13,298

Planning range (±25%): $10,000–$16,600. The same bath as a cosmetic refresh (paint, fixtures, mirror, lighting, hardware — tub and tile stay) runs about $4,500. As a luxury job (stone tile, glass walk-in, heated floor, double vanity) it passes $30,000. Same 40 sq ft; scope is everything.

What drives the price up

  • Moving plumbing. Relocating a toilet means rerouting a 3–4" drain through the floor structure — $4,000+ for the plumbing move alone, before flooring and subfloor repair. The classic 5×8 layout exists because the wet wall stacks efficiently; fight it only with good reason.
  • Tiled showers vs. prefab units. A prefab surround ($1,500–$2,500 installed) versus a fully tiled shower ($4,000–$8,000+) is the biggest single fork in any bathroom budget.
  • Tile choice and layout. Porcelain at $14/sq ft installed vs. natural stone at $22+; intricate patterns (herringbone, small mosaics) add 30–50% in setting labor over large-format tile.
  • What's behind the walls. Rot, mold, cast-iron drains, and ungrounded wiring are the classic surprise finds in pre-1980 baths. Budget a 10–15% contingency on any gut — the ±25% planning range exists for exactly this.
  • Glass. A frameless glass shower door ($900–$2,000) is measured after tile is set and takes 2–3 weeks to fabricate — it affects both budget and timeline.

How to save without cheapening it

  • Keep the layout. The $4,000+ plumbing move is the easiest money to not spend.
  • Reglaze instead of replacing the tub. Professional refinishing runs $400–$600 vs. $2,500 for a new combo — if the tub is structurally sound, reglazing buys a decade.
  • Porcelain that looks like marble. $14/sq ft installed, zero sealing, and guests can't tell — versus $22+/sq ft for real stone plus annual maintenance.
  • Keep the vanity boxes. New quartz top ($400–$800) + soft-close hardware ($3–$8 per door) transforms a dated vanity for a fraction of $1,800 new.
  • One statement, not five. A $1,200 fixture package with one great mirror and good lighting reads "designed"; five competing upgrades read "busy." Spend the design budget once, in one place.
  • Winter bids, milestone payments. Contractors discount in slow months; never pay more than 10–15% up front.

ROI: what a bathroom remodel returns

Remodeling magazine's Cost vs. Value data consistently puts mid-range bathroom remodels at roughly 60–70% cost recouped at resale — among the best returns of any room, and better than kitchens. The mechanism is simple: a dated or damaged bathroom actively repels buyers, while a clean, neutral, well-lit one photographs well and appraises fine. The ROI killers are over-personalization (bold tile, vessel sinks in a family bath) and overbuilding past the neighborhood — a $45,000 bath in a $250,000 house doesn't come back. Remodel for the decade of use; let resale be the bonus.

Timeline: how long it takes

ScopeConstructionPlanning before demo
Cosmetic refresh1–2 weeks1–2 weeks (ordering fixtures)
Mid-range full remodel2–4 weeks2–4 weeks (permits, fixture lead times)
Major remodel4–8 weeks4–6 weeks (design, permits, special orders)

The bottlenecks are always the same: tile setting (slow, sequential), custom glass (measured after tile, 2–3 week fabrication), and inspections. If this is your only bathroom, plan the outage — a mid-range job means 2–4 weeks without that shower. A gym membership or a neighborly arrangement is cheaper than rushing the tile setter.

DIY vs. pro: where the line is

Safe DIY: demolition (carefully), painting, installing the vanity, mirror, lighting fixtures, hardware, toilet (if you're handy — the wax ring is unforgiving but learnable). Hire out: anything behind the tile (waterproofing membrane — get this wrong and you remodel twice), shower valve and drain plumbing, electrical (GFCI, vent fan on a dedicated circuit), and tile setting if you've never done it (lippage on a shower floor is forever). The classic budget play: DIY the cosmetic 20% (demo, paint, accessories), pro the wet 80%.

Bathroom remodel financing: cash, HELOC, or contractor financing?

A mid-range bathroom remodel at $13,000–$25,000 sits in an awkward middle: too big for most credit cards, too small for a full home-equity loan to feel worth the paperwork. The options, ranked by total cost:

  • Cash / savings. Cheapest by definition — no interest, no fees, and contractors often discount 3–5% for payment without card processing fees. If the refresh can wait six months while you save, it almost always wins.
  • HELOC or home-equity loan. For major remodels ($20,000+), a HELOC at current rates is usually the lowest-cost borrowed money, and the interest may be tax-deductible if the remodel substantially improves the home (check current IRS rules — deductibility has narrowed). The paperwork overhead only makes sense above ~$15,000.
  • Contractor financing. Many bath contractors offer 12-month same-as-cash or low-APR plans through lending partners. Convenient, but read the deferred-interest clause: miss the payoff date on "same as cash" and retroactive interest at 20%+ applies to the whole balance.
  • Credit card. Only for the refresh tier — and only a rewards card you pay off monthly. Financing $15,000 at 22% APR turns a $13,000 bathroom into a $17,000 one.

The rule of thumb: finance the structure (plumbing, waterproofing, tile — the 25-year stuff) and cash-flow the surfaces (fixtures, mirror, paint — the things you can upgrade later). Never finance a luxury scope on a mid-range budget; the finishes are the first thing value-engineering should cut, not the last thing debt should cover.

Permits and the unsexy essentials

Most full remodels need a permit ($200–$600) — plumbing and electrical changes trigger it in nearly every jurisdiction. Unpermitted plumbing can void insurance claims if it leaks, and it surfaces at sale when the buyer's inspector asks questions. The three invisible lines that matter most: waterproofing behind tile ($300–$800 — membrane or backer board, non-negotiable), an exhaust fan vented to the outside ($150–$400 — not into the attic), and blocking for grab bars ($100–$300 — cheap during a gut, a wall-opening job later). Budget them; they're the difference between a 15-year bathroom and a 5-year one.

Bathroom remodel cost FAQs

How much does a bathroom remodel cost per square foot?

Expect roughly $200–$400 per sq ft for a mid-range full remodel, $100–$200 for a cosmetic refresh, and $500–$900+ for luxury. Small bathrooms cost more per sq ft because fixtures and plumbing cost the same regardless of floor area.

What is the most expensive part of a bathroom remodel?

Tile installation plus the tub/shower — together about 35–40% of a typical budget. Tile is slow, skilled work, and a tiled walk-in shower alone runs $6,000+. Labor overall is another ~25%.

Can you remodel a bathroom for $5,000?

Yes, as a cosmetic refresh: keep the tub, tile, and layout; replace the toilet seat, faucet, mirror, lighting, and paint; add new hardware. You won't touch plumbing or tile at this budget, but a dated bath can look new for $3,000–$8,000.

Does a bathroom remodel increase home value?

Mid-range bathroom remodels recoup roughly 60–70% at resale — among the best returns of any room. A dated or damaged bathroom actively drags offers down, so even a modest refresh usually pays for itself in price and speed of sale.

How long does a bathroom remodel take?

Cosmetic refresh: 1–2 weeks. Mid-range full remodel: 2–4 weeks. Major remodel with layout changes: 4–8 weeks plus 2–4 weeks of planning and permitting. Tile setting and custom glass are the usual bottlenecks.

Should I hire a general contractor or separate trades for a bathroom?

For a full remodel, a general contractor is usually worth the 10–20% markup — bathrooms pack four trades into 40 sq ft and sequencing mistakes are expensive. For a cosmetic refresh, hiring trades directly or DIY-ing paint and fixtures saves real money.
