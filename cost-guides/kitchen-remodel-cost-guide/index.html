Kitchen Remodel Cost Guide 2026

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

A kitchen remodel is the most expensive room project most homeowners ever take on — and the one with the widest price spread. This guide breaks down what kitchens actually cost in 2026 by scope tier, where every dollar goes, and how to keep the budget under control. All figures are October 2026 US planning estimates; use the kitchen remodel cost calculator for your exact kitchen.

Kitchen remodel cost by scope

The word "remodel" covers four very different projects. Knowing which one you're doing is half the budgeting battle.

ScopeTypical rangeWhat changes
Cosmetic refresh$8,000–$18,000Paint, cabinet refacing or repainting, new hardware, lighting, backsplash — same layout, same cabinet boxes
Mid-range full remodel$25,000–$60,000New semi-custom cabinets, quartz or granite counters, new flooring, mid-range appliance suite — layout usually stays
Major remodel$60,000–$130,000Layout changes (moving sink, walls, or gas lines), custom or high-end semi-custom cabinets, pro-style appliances, upgraded electrical/plumbing
Luxury$100,000–$250,000+Custom cabinetry, marble or statement stone, professional-grade appliances, structural work, designer fees

The national average sits around $25,000–$30,000 for a mid-range project — but "average" hides the real story. A 200 sq ft kitchen at luxury spec can cost ten times a 100 sq ft refresh. Size and scope, not zip code, drive the number.

Where the money goes

Kitchen budgets break down with remarkable consistency across projects. For a typical mid-range remodel:

CategoryShare of budgetNotes
Cabinets~30%The single biggest line item — and the biggest lever. Refacing vs. custom is a 3–4× swing.
Labor~20%Installation, demo, and trade work. Rises with scope complexity, not material prices.
Appliances~15%A basic suite is ~$3,000; a pro suite is $15,000+. The easiest line to overspend.
Countertops~10%Laminate $15–$30/sq ft installed vs. quartz $60–$140/sq ft — a 4–5× choice.
Plumbing / electrical / fixtures~10%Explodes if you move the sink or add circuits. Stays flat if the layout stays.
Flooring~7%LVP $3–$8/sq ft vs. hardwood $12–$25/sq ft installed.
Paint, lighting, misc~8%Backsplash, paint, pendant lights, hardware, permits, and the inevitable surprises.

The takeaway: cabinets and layout decisions control roughly half your budget. Everything else is tuning.

What drives the price up (and down)

The big three levers

  1. Layout changes. Moving a sink, gas line, or wall adds $3,000–$8,000 in plumbing, electrical, drywall, and flooring patch work — before you buy a single finish. It's the #1 budget killer because it touches every trade.
  2. Cabinet tier. Refacing ($4,000–$8,000 flat) vs. stock ($100–$200/linear ft) vs. semi-custom ($200–$400/lf) vs. custom ($400–$800/lf). On 25 linear feet, that's a $4,000-to-$20,000 swing. Our cabinet cost guide compares all four in detail.
  3. Appliance tier. A basic suite costs ~$3,000; a mid-range suite ~$5,500; a pro-style suite $15,000+. Nobody "needs" a $4,000 range — but it's the most common splurge, so budget it deliberately.

Smaller levers that still matter

  • Countertop material: laminate ($15–$30/sq ft) to marble ($75–$200/sq ft) — see the countertop guide.
  • Region: coastal metros run 15–20% above the national average; rural areas run ~15% below.
  • Structural surprises: water damage behind the sink, ungrounded wiring, or a load-bearing wall you wanted gone. Budget a 10–15% contingency on any project over $20,000.
  • Permits and design: $500–$2,000 for permits; a designer runs 10–20% of the project or $100–$250/hour.

How to save real money (without a cheap-looking kitchen)

  1. Keep the layout. The sink, stove, and fridge stay where they are. This single decision saves $3,000–$8,000.
  2. Reface instead of replace — if the boxes are sound. $4,000–$8,000 vs. $7,500–$20,000 for new.
  3. Choose quartz over marble, semi-custom over custom. Guests can't tell the difference; your wallet can.
  4. Buy appliances as a suite during holiday sales. Memorial Day, Labor Day, and Black Friday routinely cut 20–30% off package prices.
  5. Do the demo and painting yourself. Demo is $500–$1,500 of pure labor you can do in a weekend; painting saves another $1,000–$2,000.
  6. Get 3+ bids and itemize. Bids that lump everything into one number hide padding. Ask for line items — our calculator's breakdown is a good template for what to demand.

ROI and resale value

Kitchens sell houses — but they don't pay for themselves. Industry cost-vs-value data consistently shows:

  • Minor kitchen remodel: recoups roughly 70–80% at resale. The best ROI in the kitchen category.
  • Major kitchen remodel: recoups roughly 50–60%. Buyers like it, but won't pay dollar-for-dollar for your premium finishes.
  • Luxury remodel: recoups 50% or less. Do it for yourself, not the appraisal.

The ROI sweet spot is the mid-range remodel in a mid-range neighborhood: nice enough to be the best kitchen on the block, not so nice that you're the over-improved outlier. Over-improving past your neighborhood's price ceiling is the fastest way to lose money on a kitchen.

Cabinet lead times are the usual bottleneck: stock ships in 1–2 weeks, semi-custom takes 4–8 weeks, custom takes 8–14 weeks. Order cabinets before demo starts — never after.

Small kitchens cost more per square foot (fixed costs like appliances spread over fewer feet); large kitchens cost more in total but less per foot. If a bid lands far outside these bands, ask the contractor to walk you through the line items — either you found a bargain or something's missing.

Financing a kitchen remodel

Most kitchens aren't paid for out of checking. The common options, cheapest first:

  • Cash / savings: the cheapest "financing" — no interest, and contractors often discount 3–5% for cash or check payment (no card fees).
  • Home equity loan or HELOC: typically the lowest rates for large projects (often 7–9% in 2026), interest may be tax-deductible when used for home improvement. Best for $25,000+ projects.
  • Cash-out refinance: only makes sense if current mortgage rates beat yours — rarely the case in 2026.
  • Contractor financing: convenient but compare the APR — 0%-for-12-months promos are common, then rates jump to 15–25%.
  • Credit cards: fine for materials you'll pay off in 1–2 months (and the points are nice); terrible as a 2-year plan at 20%+ APR.

Rule of thumb: if the financing cost pushes the project past what the kitchen adds in value and enjoyment, shrink the scope. A $30,000 kitchen at 8% over 7 years costs about $39,000 all-in — budget the true number, not the sticker number.

When NOT to remodel

Honest advice: skip the remodel if you're selling within a year and the kitchen is merely dated (not broken) — you'll rarely recoup a major project that fast. Skip it if the budget requires financing the whole thing at credit-card rates. And skip the luxury tier if yours would be the nicest kitchen in the neighborhood — over-improvement is real, and appraisers won't credit your marble. The best kitchen project is the one sized to your home, your timeline, and your actual cooking.

Kitchen remodel FAQs

How much does a kitchen remodel cost in 2026?

Most US kitchen remodels land between $15,000 and $75,000. Cosmetic refresh: $8,000–$18,000. Mid-range: $25,000–$60,000. Major: $60,000–$130,000. Luxury: $100,000+. Try the calculator for your kitchen.

What is the most expensive part of a kitchen remodel?

Cabinets — about 30% of the budget. Then labor (~20%), appliances (~15%), countertops (~10%), plumbing/electrical (~10%), and flooring (~7%).

How long does a kitchen remodel take?

Refresh: 2–5 weeks total. Mid-range: 2–4 months. Major: 5–8 months. Luxury: 6–12 months. Cabinet lead times (4–14 weeks) are the usual bottleneck.

What is the ROI of a kitchen remodel?

Minor remodels recoup ~70–80% at resale; major ~50–60%; luxury ~50% or less. Remodel for your own use first — resale is a bonus, not the business case.

Can I remodel a kitchen for $15,000?

Yes, as a cosmetic refresh: keep the layout and boxes, reface or repaint cabinets, laminate or butcher-block counters, new hardware and lighting. Skip layout changes — that's what pushes projects past $25,000.

Should I move the plumbing in a kitchen remodel?

Only if the layout genuinely doesn't work. Moving a sink or gas line adds $3,000–$8,000 across trades. A great kitchen in the existing footprint beats a mediocre one with moved plumbing.
