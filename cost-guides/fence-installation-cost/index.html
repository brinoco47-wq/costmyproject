Fence Installation Cost

Advertisement

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Labor is 30–50% of a fence project — and the part of the quote with the most padding. Here's what installers actually charge per linear foot, what gates/permits/terrain add, and how to read a bid like a contractor.

Quote red flags

  • One lump sum, no breakdown. Honest bids show per-foot material + labor, gates, removal, and permit as separate lines. A single number hides padding.
  • Gates priced as plain footage. A gate needs heavier posts, hinges, and latches — if the bid treats 150 LF as all identical, the gate math is buried somewhere.
  • No post spec. "Set posts" means nothing. You want: depth (30+ in), concrete (yes), and spacing (6–8 ft). Shallow posts are how fences lean in year three.
  • Allowances instead of prices. "Removal: allowance $300" means they haven't looked. Get a fixed per-foot removal price.
  • Same-day pressure. "This price is only good today" is a sales tactic, not a material constraint. Real fence pricing doesn't expire overnight.
  • No permit mentioned. If they don't pull one where required, that's your liability when the city notices — or when you sell.
The 3-bid rule, upgraded:
Get three itemized bids, then run the same inputs through our calculator. Any bid more than ~25% above the calculator's range needs an explanation in writing — slope, access, or premium materials are legitimate reasons; vibes are not.

What drives the labor price up (and down)

Two crews can bid the same 150 feet and land $1,500 apart — because "labor" isn't one number. Here are the factors that move it, and by roughly how much (October 2026 US planning estimates; treat each as ±25%).

Three worked examples (real numbers)

150 LF wood privacy, 6 ft, flat yard — Texas

Installed mid-range price for wood privacy: ~$30/LF (within the $18–$45 range), so 150 LF = $4,500. Add one walk gate ($250), a permit ($75), and haul-off of the old fence (150 LF × $5 = $750). All-in: ~$5,575 — planning range ±25%.

100 LF vinyl privacy, 6 ft, slight slope — Florida

Installed mid-range for vinyl: ~$42/LF (within $30–$55), so 100 LF = $4,200. Add a double driveway gate ($800), slope adder on labor (~+15% on ~$1,500 labor = $225), and removal of old chain-link (100 LF × $5 = $500). All-in: ~$5,725.

200 LF chain-link, 4 ft, sloped rocky yard — Colorado

Chain-link installed: ~$19/LF (within $13–$25), so 200 LF = $3,800. Rocky digging adds ~+35% on the labor portion (~$1,500 × 0.35 = $525). Add a permit ($150) and one walk gate ($250). All-in: ~$4,725. Note how the rock premium alone is ~11% of the project — always ask about soil.

The pattern:
Gates, removal, and terrain — not the fence boards — are where bids diverge. When comparing quotes, line up these three lines first; the per-foot material+install rate is usually within 10% across honest bids.

Regional labor differences

Fence labor tracks local construction wages, so the same fence costs meaningfully different amounts by region. These multipliers are industry-planning estimates (October 2026), useful for sanity-checking a bid against national ranges — ±25% as always.

South (TX, GA, FL, the Carolinas)~0.85×Lower labor rates, year-round building season, competitive market
Midwest (OH, IN, MO, KS)~0.95×Near-national rates; winter discounts are common
Mountain (CO, UT, AZ)~1.0–1.1×Average rates, but rocky-soil and slope adders hit often

A $4,500 fence in Texas plausibly costs $5,400–$5,800 in California for the identical scope — that's geography, not gouging. What is gouging is a bid 25%+ above your region-adjusted calculator number with no written explanation.

How to save $1,000+ on installation

  • Demo it yourself. Pulling the old fence and hauling it saves $3–$10/LF — $450–$1,500 on a 150-ft run. It's the easiest DIY money in the project.
  • Book off-season. Late fall and winter bids in cold climates run 10–20% under spring. Ask "what's your slow-season rate?" — crews answer this honestly.
  • Split the neighbor side. A shared property-line fence is commonly split 50/50. One conversation can save you half the job.
  • Fewer gates, standard sizes. Every extra gate is $150–$1,500 installed. Plan access once instead of twice.
  • 6 ft, not 8 ft. Dropping to standard height saves ~15–25% on labor and avoids the engineering that tall fences trigger in some cities.
  • Stain the wood yourself later. Some installers upsell pre-staining at a steep markup. Install now, stain in 60 days yourself for half the price.

What NOT to cut: post depth, concrete, and the permit. Saving $400 on shallow posts buys you a leaning fence in year three — the most expensive "savings" in fencing.

Installation FAQs

How long does fence installation take?

A pro crew typically finishes 150–200 LF in 1–3 days once materials and permits are ready. DIY with a helper: 2–4 weekends. Rocky soil or many gates add days either way.

Should I get a survey before building a fence?

If your property line is uncertain — yes, $200–$1,200 now beats moving a fence later or a neighbor dispute. If you have recent survey pins visible, you're probably fine. When in doubt, talk to the neighbor first; shared fences split costs in many areas.

What does "call 811" do?

It's the free national utility-locate service: they mark buried gas, electric, and telecom lines within a few days. Hitting a line is dangerous and expensive — pros do this reflexively, DIYers skip it most.

How much does a 150-foot wood privacy fence cost installed?

At 2026 installed prices of $18–$45 per linear foot, 150 feet runs roughly $2,700–$6,750 for the fence itself. Labor is $8–$15/LF of that. Add a walk gate ($150–$400), old-fence removal ($3–$10/LF), and a permit ($20–$400) for the all-in number. A typical mid-range job lands around $4,500–$5,500 — planning estimate, not a quote.

Do fence prices change with the season?

Yes, in cold climates. Spring is peak season and peak pricing; late fall and winter bids often run 10–20% lower as crews try to stay busy. In the South, where building is year-round, seasonal swings are small. Either way, book 3–6 weeks ahead — the cheapest month doesn't help if every good crew is booked.

Should fence posts be set in concrete?

Yes — every post, no exceptions on a privacy fence. The rule: one-third of the post underground (minimum 30 inches for a 6-ft fence), a gravel base for drainage, and concrete around the post, domed at the top to shed water. Dry-set or shallow posts are the #1 reason fences lean within three years, and "we set posts" in a quote means nothing until the depth and concrete are written down.

What should a fence contract include?

Total linear footage and height; material spec (species, board thickness, panel brand); post depth, concrete, and spacing in writing; each gate counted with its own price; removal and haul-off as a line item; who pulls the permit; start and completion dates; warranty length (1–3 years on workmanship is standard); and a payment schedule — never pay more than 50% up front, with the balance due on completion.
