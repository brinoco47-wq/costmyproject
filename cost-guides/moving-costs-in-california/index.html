Moving costs in California

Advertisement

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Moving in California? Local moves run $1,450–$3,000 for a 2-bedroom home, while long-distance moves price by weight and distance — a 2-bedroom going 1,000+ miles runs $4,200–$7,800. Below: the full tables, what drives California prices, and how to pay less.

The 30-second version Under 100 miles, California movers bill by the hour (2-mover crews ~$80–$100/hr nationally, higher in California's big metros). Over 100 miles, it's flat weight-and-distance pricing under federal FMCSA rules. California moves price ~20% above national averages in our model — see the methodology note below.

Worked example: 2-bedroom local move in California

How the table range is built — a 2-bedroom apartment moving across Los Angeles (~25 miles):

  • Crew: 3 movers × ~7 hours (load, drive, unload)
  • Effective all-in rate: ~$106/hr (crew + truck + standard equipment, California-adjusted)
  • Estimated total: 21 crew-hours × $106 ≈ $2,225 — the midpoint of the $1,450–$3,000 range above
  • Your planning range (±25%): $1,650–$2,800

Stairs, long carries, packing services, or a month-end date push you toward the top of the range; a ground-floor unit with everything boxed and a mid-month date pulls toward the bottom. This is a budgeting estimate, not a quote — confirm with a binding written estimate.

What drives the price in California

  • The highest mover labor rates in the country — California metro labor runs well above the national average — expect hourly crew rates at the top of (or above) the $80–$100/hr national band, especially in the Bay Area and Los Angeles.
  • Traffic adds billable hours — A crosstown move in LA or the Bay Area can add 1–3 hours of paid drive time. Movers bill portal-to-portal in many markets, so congestion is literally on your invoice.
  • San Francisco access is its own line item — Steep streets, scarce parking, and permit requirements mean SF moves often need reserved parking permits and extra carry time — ask about it upfront, not on move day.
  • High-rise elevator logistics — Downtown LA, SF, and San Diego buildings commonly require freight-elevator reservations and a certificate of insurance from your mover. No reservation can mean no move that day.
  • Wildfire season disruptions — September–November wildfire activity can close highways and delay long-distance shipments. If you're moving in fall, ask your carrier about their delay policy.

How to save money on a California move

  1. Move mid-month and mid-week — the 1st and weekends book first and price highest.
  2. In San Francisco, sort the parking permit before you book the mover; it's the most common day-of failure.
  3. Declutter ruthlessly: long-distance pricing is weight-based, and California long hauls are long.
  4. Book 6–8 weeks out for summer — the state's peak season sells out in the big metros.

California moving quote checklist

Before you sign, confirm each of these in writing — a quote that skips them is where surprise charges come from:

  1. Binding vs. non-binding: a binding estimate caps the price for the listed inventory; a non-binding estimate can rise with actual weight or hours. Know which one you hold.
  2. Access items itemized: stairs, long carry, elevator time and any shuttle, each priced — not bundled as "extra services TBD".
  3. Parking permit responsibility: who reserves it in San Francisco, and what happens to the bill if no permit is in place.
  4. Elevator and insurance paperwork: freight-elevator reservation confirmed and the building's certificate of insurance supplied by the mover before move day.
  5. Valuation cover: released value ($0.60/lb by law) is the default and is not full cover; full-value protection runs roughly 1% of declared value.
  6. Delivery window in writing: 1–3 days regionally, 4–7 days around 1,000 miles, 7–14 days cross-country — with the delay policy for wildfire-season disruptions.
  7. Drive time: whether local hourly billing runs portal-to-portal, so LA or Bay Area traffic is priced into the quote, not discovered on it.

FAQs

When is peak moving season in California?

May through September. Families move between school years and the weather is ideal, so demand — and prices — peak. January through March is typically cheapest, with winter rates noticeably lower in most metros.

Why are California moves so expensive?

Labor costs are the highest in the nation, traffic inflates billable hours, and dense cities add permits, parking fees, and elevator logistics. A California local move commonly lands ~20% above the national average for the same home size.

Do I need a parking permit for movers in San Francisco?

Usually yes. SF requires temporary no-parking permits for moving trucks in many neighborhoods — arrange it days ahead. Without one, your crew may have to park blocks away and carry everything, which adds hours to your bill.

Is it cheaper to move within California or out of state?

Per mile, in-state moves are cheaper — but a 1,400-mile California-to-Texas move is a long-distance job priced by weight and distance ($4,000+ for a 2-bedroom), while a 30-mile local move is hourly. Compare the tables above, not the vibes.

How far in advance should I book movers in California?

Six to eight weeks for a summer move in LA, SF, or San Diego; 3–4 weeks is usually fine off-season. Long-distance carriers need more lead time than local crews.

How much does it cost to move from Los Angeles to San Francisco?

About 380 miles, so it falls in the under-500-mile distance band: $2,750–$5,050 for a 2-bedroom and $5,850–$9,750 for a 4-bedroom, with 1–3 day delivery typical. High-rise elevator reservations, parking permits and any shuttle at the San Francisco end sit on top of that band — get them itemized in the quote.

Do California movers charge for time stuck in traffic?

Often, yes. In many markets local hourly billing runs portal-to-portal, so the drive between jobs — including LA and Bay Area congestion — is billable time. A crosstown move can add 1–3 hours that way, so ask whether your quote includes drive time before you compare hourly rates.

What should a California moving quote include before I sign?

Whether the estimate is binding or non-binding, itemized access charges (stairs, long carry, elevator, shuttle), who reserves any San Francisco parking permit, the freight-elevator reservation and certificate of insurance for high-rises, your valuation cover (released value at $0.60/lb by law vs. full-value protection at roughly 1% of declared value), and the delivery window in writing.
