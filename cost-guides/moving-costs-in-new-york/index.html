Moving costs in New York

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Moving in New York? Local moves run $1,450–$3,000 for a 2-bedroom home, while long-distance moves price by weight and distance — a 2-bedroom going 1,000+ miles runs $4,200–$7,800. Below: the full tables, what drives New York prices, and how to pay less.

The 30-second version Under 100 miles, New York movers bill by the hour (2-mover crews ~$80–$100/hr nationally, higher in New York's big metros). Over 100 miles, it's flat weight-and-distance pricing under federal FMCSA rules. New York moves price ~20% above national averages in our model — see the methodology note below.

Worked example: 2-bedroom local move in New York

How the table range is built — a 2-bedroom apartment moving across Queens (~25 miles):

  • Crew: 3 movers × ~7 hours (load, drive, unload)
  • Effective all-in rate: ~$106/hr (crew + truck + standard equipment, New York-adjusted)
  • Estimated total: 21 crew-hours × $106 ≈ $2,225 — the midpoint of the $1,450–$3,000 range above
  • Your planning range (±25%): $1,650–$2,800

Stairs, long carries, packing services, or a month-end date push you toward the top of the range; a ground-floor unit with everything boxed and a mid-month date pulls toward the bottom. This is a budgeting estimate, not a quote — confirm with a binding written estimate.

What drives the price in New York

  • NYC walk-ups bill by the stairwell — No elevator means every box is carried up stairs — crews charge for the time, and some add explicit stair fees. A 4th-floor walk-up can add hundreds versus an elevator building.
  • Co-op and condo board requirements — Many NYC buildings require a certificate of insurance, move-in deposits, and approved move dates/times. Boards can and do turn away movers who show up unprepared.
  • Parking and traffic in the five boroughs — Double-parking a 26-foot truck in Manhattan is a special skill. Expect parking permits, tickets baked into quotes, and traffic-inflated drive time.
  • Upstate is a different market — New York City metro pricing runs far above upstate. A Buffalo or Rochester local move looks much closer to national averages — the 20% planning adjustment here is NYC-weighted.
  • Winter weather is real — January–March is cheapest but lake-effect snow and nor'easters can delay moves. Build flex days into winter move plans.

How to save money on a New York move

  1. Lock your building's freight elevator and COI requirements before you lock a mover.
  2. Mid-week, mid-month moves dodge the NYC lease-turnover crunch (and its prices).
  3. For NYC outbound long-distance, compare New Jersey–based carriers too — the market is regional.
  4. Measure your largest furniture against stairwells and doorways before move day, not during it.

FAQs

Why is moving in NYC so expensive?

Stairs, elevator logistics, parking, traffic, co-op requirements, and the highest labor costs in the country stack up. A NYC local move commonly runs ~20% above national averages — and walk-ups cost more than elevator buildings.

Do NYC movers charge extra for stairs?

Many do — either explicit stair fees or simply more billable hours. Disclose your floor and elevator situation when quoting; it's the single biggest NYC price variable.

What is a COI and why does my building want one?

A certificate of insurance proving your mover carries liability coverage. Most NYC co-ops and condos require it before move day — no COI, no move. Reputable movers issue them routinely; ask a week ahead.

What is the cheapest month to move in New York?

January through March, when demand is lowest. You'll trade savings for weather risk, so keep a flex day or two.

Should I use a NYC or New Jersey mover for an outbound move?

Get quotes from both — the metro market is regional and NJ-based carriers often price NYC-origin jobs competitively. Compare binding estimates, not just hourly rates.
