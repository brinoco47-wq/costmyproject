Moving costs in New York

Advertisement

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Moving in New York? Local moves run $1,450–$3,000 for a 2-bedroom home, while long-distance moves price by weight and distance — a 2-bedroom going 1,000+ miles runs $4,200–$7,800. Below: the full tables, what drives New York prices, and how to pay less.

The 30-second version Under 100 miles, New York movers bill by the hour (2-mover crews ~$80–$100/hr nationally, higher in New York's big metros). Over 100 miles, it's flat weight-and-distance pricing under federal FMCSA rules. New York moves price ~20% above national averages in our model — see the methodology note below.

Worked example: 2-bedroom local move in New York

How the table range is built — a 2-bedroom apartment moving across Queens (~25 miles):

  • Crew: 3 movers × ~7 hours (load, drive, unload)
  • Effective all-in rate: ~$106/hr (crew + truck + standard equipment, New York-adjusted)
  • Estimated total: 21 crew-hours × $106 ≈ $2,225 — the midpoint of the $1,450–$3,000 range above
  • Your planning range (±25%): $1,650–$2,800

Stairs, long carries, packing services, or a month-end date push you toward the top of the range; a ground-floor unit with everything boxed and a mid-month date pulls toward the bottom. This is a budgeting estimate, not a quote — confirm with a binding written estimate.

What drives the price in New York

  • NYC walk-ups bill by the stairwell — No elevator means every box is carried up stairs — crews charge for the time, and some add explicit stair fees. A 4th-floor walk-up can add hundreds versus an elevator building.
  • Co-op and condo board requirements — Many NYC buildings require a certificate of insurance, move-in deposits, and approved move dates/times. Boards can and do turn away movers who show up unprepared.
  • Parking and traffic in the five boroughs — Double-parking a 26-foot truck in Manhattan is a special skill. Expect parking permits, tickets baked into quotes, and traffic-inflated drive time.
  • Upstate is a different market — New York City metro pricing runs far above upstate. A Buffalo or Rochester local move looks much closer to national averages — the 20% planning adjustment here is NYC-weighted.
  • Winter weather is real — January–March is cheapest but lake-effect snow and nor'easters can delay moves. Build flex days into winter move plans.

If your move crosses markets — say, a Brooklyn walk-up to a Buffalo single-family home — price it as an NYC-origin job: the origin crew, stairs, and elevator booking drive the cost, and the destination rarely discounts it. Long-distance pricing (weight × distance) takes over beyond about 100 miles, so a NYC-to-Buffalo move (~370 miles) prices as an interstate-style corridor job even though it never leaves the state.

NYC building rules that change your quote

More NYC moves go wrong on paperwork than on price. Before you accept any quote, get these four items in writing from your building — then hand them to every mover you quote:

  1. Freight elevator reservation. Many co-ops and condos allow moves only in a booked window (often 9 a.m.–5 p.m., weekdays only), with a refundable deposit. Miss the window and the crew waits on your clock — or the move slips a day.
  2. Certificate of insurance (COI). The building's required coverage limits, additional-insured wording, and notice period. Reputable movers issue COIs routinely, but they need a week, not a day — a mover who says "we'll sort it on the day" is a red flag.
  3. Move-in / move-out deposits and fees. Some buildings charge a non-refundable move fee ($200–$500 is common) plus a refundable deposit against hallway or elevator damage. Budget both, and photograph common areas before the crew starts.
  4. Street and loading rules. Manhattan and parts of Brooklyn restrict truck size on some streets, and a 26-foot truck may need a smaller shuttle or a longer carry. Ask the mover how they handle your block specifically — a generic "we'll figure it out" usually means a long-carry fee on the invoice.

Do the same check at the destination building, not just the origin. A smooth load followed by a refused unload — no COI, no elevator booking — is one of the most expensive failure modes in NYC moving, because you pay the crew while the paperwork gets sorted.

When to move in New York: seasonal pricing

New York's lease cycle runs the calendar. The same crew, same furniture, same distance can price 15–25% apart between peak and off-peak — the biggest single lever after home size.

PeriodPrice levelWhat to expect
May – September (peak)HighestBook 4–6 weeks ahead; month-end and weekends sell out first; expect top-of-range quotes
October – November (shoulder)MidBetter availability and mid-range pricing; the sweet spot for a planned move
December – March (off-peak)LowestCheapest quotes, but snow, ice, and nor'easters can delay the day — keep a flex day
April (early shoulder)Mid-lowDemand builds through the month; early April still prices like winter

Within any month, mid-week and mid-month dates beat month-end weekends. NYC's lease turnover concentrates on the 1st and the last weekend — movers know it, and their calendars price it. If your lease allows a mid-month start, that one choice can save more than any haggling over hourly rates.

Getting a New York quote: the checklist

Use this as a script on every quote call. A mover who answers all six cleanly is worth more than a mover who is $200 cheaper and vague on two of them.

  1. Binding vs. non-binding. A binding estimate locks the price for the listed inventory; a non-binding estimate can rise on the day. For interstate moves, federal rules require a written estimate — get it in writing either way.
  2. Full inventory walk-through. Video or in-person, room by room, including the balcony, storage cage, and basement. Phone quotes from a room count miss the items that become surprise fees.
  3. Origin and destination access, in writing. Floor, elevator or stairs, parking, carry distance, and any shuttle need — at both ends, not just the origin.
  4. Valuation coverage. Released value (60 cents per pound, included) vs. full-value protection (costs more, covers repair or replacement). Know which one your quote includes before you sign.
  5. Licensing check. Interstate carriers should appear on the federal FMCSA mover database; intrastate New York moves fall under state regulation. Verify before paying any deposit, and never pay a large deposit up front.
  6. Delivery window, not just a date. Long-distance delivery is a window (days, sometimes weeks). Get the window in writing, plus what happens if it slips — and keep essentials (documents, medication, chargers, a change of clothes) with you, not in the truck.

Red flags that end the call: no written estimate, a deposit above 20% up front, a company name that does not match its paperwork, or a quote far below the other two you received. A lowball quote that doubles on move day is the oldest pattern in this market — three comparable quotes are the cheapest insurance there is.

How to save money on a New York move

  1. Lock your building's freight elevator and COI requirements before you lock a mover.
  2. Mid-week, mid-month moves dodge the NYC lease-turnover crunch (and its prices).
  3. For NYC outbound long-distance, compare New Jersey–based carriers too — the market is regional.
  4. Measure your largest furniture against stairwells and doorways before move day, not during it.

FAQs

Why is moving in NYC so expensive?

Stairs, elevator logistics, parking, traffic, co-op requirements, and the highest labor costs in the country stack up. A NYC local move commonly runs ~20% above national averages — and walk-ups cost more than elevator buildings.

Do NYC movers charge extra for stairs?

Many do — either explicit stair fees or simply more billable hours. Disclose your floor and elevator situation when quoting; it's the single biggest NYC price variable.

What is a COI and why does my building want one?

A certificate of insurance proving your mover carries liability coverage. Most NYC co-ops and condos require it before move day — no COI, no move. Reputable movers issue them routinely; ask a week ahead.

What is the cheapest month to move in New York?

January through March, when demand is lowest. You'll trade savings for weather risk, so keep a flex day or two.

Should I use a NYC or New Jersey mover for an outbound move?

Get quotes from both — the metro market is regional and NJ-based carriers often price NYC-origin jobs competitively. Compare binding estimates, not just hourly rates.

How far ahead should I book a mover in New York?

Four to six weeks ahead in peak season (May–September), two to three weeks in off-peak months. NYC elevator bookings and COI paperwork need their own lead time, so start the building conversation before you start the mover conversation.

What is the cheapest way to move out of New York City?

A moving container or a hybrid move — you pack and load, a carrier drives — typically saves $1,000–$3,000 versus full-service on 500+ mile moves. It costs you a weekend of loading and a street-parking plan for the container, so it suits flexible schedules better than tight elevator windows.

Do I need a parking permit for a moving truck in New York City?

It depends on the street and the truck size. Your mover should know the rules for your block and arrange any needed permits or a shuttle plan — ask how they handle your specific street before quote day, not on move day.
