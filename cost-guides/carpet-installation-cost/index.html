How much does carpet installation cost?

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Carpet is the cheapest installed floor and the warmest underfoot — but the sticker price hides the pad, and the pad is half the comfort. Here's the real per-square-foot math by carpet type, plus stairs.

The short answer

ComponentCost per sq ftPer sq yd (how carpet is sold)
Carpet materials$1–$5$9–$45
Pad / cushion$0.35–$0.80$3–$7
Pro installation labor$0.50–$1.50$4.50–$13.50
All-in installed$3–$8$27–$72

October 2026 planning ranges, ±25%. Labor and installed ranges from the site's flooring data; material/pad splits are derived planning ranges. Budgeting estimates, not quotes.

Carpet is sold by the square yard (9 sq ft), which is why quotes look confusing next to hard-floor per-sq-ft pricing — divide any sq-yd price by 9 to compare apples to apples. The headline number to watch isn't the carpet, it's the all-in: a $2/sq ft carpet with cheap pad and basic install lands around $3.50/sq ft, while a $4 carpet with premium pad lands near $7. Same "carpet," double the price, triple the lifespan and comfort. Labor is the cheapest of any floor type ($0.50–$1.50/sq ft) because stretch-in is fast — a crew carpets a whole house in a day.

Carpet types: what the price buys

TypeMaterial / sq ftFeel & lookBest for
Textured / frieze$1.50–$4Twisted yarns hide footprints and vacuum marksFamily rooms, the all-rounder
Cut pile (Saxony/plush)$2–$5Formal, velvety — shows every footprintLow-traffic living/dining rooms

Patterned$3–$6Cut-and-loop designs; hides wear bestStairs and halls (hides traffic lanes)

Material ranges are October 2026 retail planning estimates. Fiber matters as much as style: nylon costs more but resists crushing and cleans best; polyester is cheaper and stain-resistant but mats down faster in traffic lanes; triexta (SmartStrand) splits the difference.

The fiber decision is the durability decision. Nylon at a 20–30% premium over polyester is the pro's default for any room that gets daily traffic — it springs back from furniture dents and survives professional cleaning cycles that flatten polyester permanently. Polyester's stain resistance is genuinely excellent (it doesn't absorb), so it's the right call for dining rooms and kid zones where spills are the enemy and traffic is moderate. Don't overthink style names — within a fiber, density (face weight × tuft twist) predicts lifespan far better than the marketing term on the sample board.

The pad rule Never accept the free basic pad. Upgrading from 3/8" rebond to 1/2" 8-lb memory-foam pad costs ~$0.25/sq ft extra (~$75 on a bedroom) and transforms how the carpet feels and how long it lasts — it doubles as the difference between a carpet that looks tired in 5 years and one that looks good in 10. The pad is the cheapest longevity you can buy in flooring.

Stairs: priced per step, not per square foot

Stair typeTypical cost
Straight stairs, carpet only$40–$75 per step
Stairs with premium pad$60–$100+ per step
Patterned carpet on stairsAdd 20–30% (pattern matching eats material)

Industry estimates. A 14-step straight staircase at $60/step adds ~$840 — always quoted separately from the per-sq-ft room price.

Stairs are where carpet quotes surprise people, because every step is hand-cut, wrapped, and stapled — it's upholstery work, not flooring. Patterned carpet on stairs is the classic budget-buster: matching the pattern across treads wastes 20–30% more material, and installers charge for the fussiness. If the budget is tight, run the patterned carpet in the rooms and a coordinating solid on the stairs — nobody notices, everybody's wallet notices.

These numbers are why carpet still owns bedrooms: $568 all-in for a bedroom is less than the labor alone would cost for hardwood in the same room. For a kid's bedroom you'll redecorate anyway, that's a winning trade.

When carpet beats hard floors (and when it doesn't)

Carpet wins on three axes: upfront cost (cheapest installed floor, full stop), comfort and acoustics (nothing else softens a bedroom or quiets a second floor), and safety (toddlers, elderly parents — falls hurt less). It loses on lifespan (8–12 years vs. 15–100 for hard floors), allergies (it traps what hard floors release to the vacuum), and wet areas (never in baths, kitchens, or below-grade without serious moisture control). The resale picture is mixed: fresh neutral carpet photographs fine and costs little, but buyers increasingly expect hard floors in living areas — many sellers now credit the buyer for flooring rather than installing carpet they'll rip out. FAQs

How much does it cost to install carpet?

Carpet installs for $3–$8 per sq ft all-in in 2026 (planning ranges, ±25%): $1–$5/sq ft for the carpet, $0.35–$0.80 for pad, and $0.50–$1.50 for labor. A 120 sq ft bedroom runs roughly $400–$700 installed.

How much does it cost to carpet stairs?

Stairs are priced per step, not per square foot: $40–$75 per step for standard carpet, $60–$100+ with premium pad. A 14-step staircase adds roughly $600–$1,200 — always quoted separately from room pricing.

Is the carpet pad really worth upgrading?

Yes — it's the best value in flooring. Upgrading from basic rebond to 1/2-inch 8-lb pad costs about $0.25/sq ft extra and dramatically improves comfort and lifespan. Skimp on carpet before you skimp on pad.

Nylon vs polyester carpet — which is better?

Nylon costs 20–30% more but resists crushing and cleans better — the pro default for daily-traffic rooms. Polyester is cheaper with excellent stain resistance, better for dining rooms and kid zones where spills outrank traffic.

How long does carpet last?

8–12 years for residential carpet with decent pad — the shortest lifespan of any floor type. Traffic lanes matte down first; professional cleaning every 12–18 months is what separates the 8-year carpets from the 12-year ones.
