The bundling rule

Bundle add-ons with the wall job A standalone "just paint my ceilings" job carries full setup, masking, and cleanup costs — often a half-day minimum. Added to an in-progress wall repaint, the same ceiling is mostly marginal labor. Contractors routinely discount bundled trim/ceiling work 20–40% versus standalone quotes. Always ask: "what's the add-on price if you do it while you're here?"

DIY notes that actually matter

  • Ceilings: use dedicated ceiling paint (flat, low-spatter, ~$30–40/gal). A 12×15 ceiling needs one gallon. Roll away from the window light source to hide lap marks, and keep a wet edge — ceilings punish stop-and-start.
  • Doors: remove them and paint flat on sawhorses if you can; the finish difference vs. in-place brushing is dramatic. Budget a full day per batch for dry time between sides.
  • Trim: semi-gloss or satin enamel, and don't skip the light sanding between coats — it's the difference between "painted trim" and "factory trim."
  • Order of operations: ceilings first, then walls, then trim. Pros do it this way because drips only fall downward.

FAQs

How much does it cost to paint a ceiling?

$1–$2 per sq ft professionally — a 12×15 living room ceiling runs $180–$360. DIY: about $40–$60 for a gallon of ceiling paint and a roller cover.

How much to paint interior doors?

$75–$150 per door pro, both sides. DIY materials are trivial (a quart of enamel covers several doors) — you're paying for detailed brushwork and the drying logistics.

Is it cheaper to do trim at the same time as walls?

Almost always. Bundled add-ons skip duplicate setup/cleanup charges, and contractors commonly discount them 20–40% versus standalone jobs.
