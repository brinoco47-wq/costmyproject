Roof Costs in Florida (2026)

Advertisement

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

A roof replacement on a standard 2,000 sq ft Florida home costs about $15,790 at 6/12 pitch with architectural shingles — $1,330 below the national average of $17,120. The entire difference is labor: Florida construction wages run below the national average, while materials, tear-off, and permits cost the same. This page shows the Florida math, how it compares to the South average, and what the Florida number does not include.

The Florida worked example: same roof, Florida labor

Same roof as our national example: a 40×50 ft colonial, 2,000 sq ft footprint, 6/12 pitch (roof surface 2,236 sq ft), architectural shingles, one-layer tear-off, $350 permit. Only the labor line changes — scaled by the Florida labor multiplier of ×0.83.

How the Florida multiplier is calculated

The ×0.83 figure is the Florida state average for construction laborers from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (May 2023), ratioed to the national mean wage — the same dataset behind this site's ZIP-by-ZIP labor adjustment. In our ZIP3 data, all 26 Florida ZIP3 areas carry the state-average value, so the state figure is stable, not driven by one metro. Materials are not scaled: shingles cost the same in Florida as nationally in this model. Enter your ZIP in the roof cost calculator for your metro's exact multiplier.

What the Florida number does not include

  • Pitch and stories. Steep pitches (+15% at 9/12, +25% at 12/12) and second stories (+10–20%) raise labor on top of the Florida base.
  • Decking replacement. Rotten decking found during tear-off is billed per sheet on top of the quote.
  • A second tear-off layer. The example assumes one layer; two layers roughly double the $3,354 tear-off line.
  • Storm and code effects. The multiplier is labor-only. Hurricane code requirements show up in individual Florida quotes, not in this average.

The honest way to use this page: treat $15,790 as the Florida baseline for this exact roof, then adjust for your pitch, stories, layers, and decking — or run your roof in the calculator with your ZIP.

Storm damage note: what insurance pays depends on whether your policy settles at ACV (depreciated value) or RCV (full replacement, with depreciation recoverable after completion). Our national worked example — $17,120 roof, 18 years old on a 28-year life, $1,000 deductible assumed — pays a $5,114 first check on ACV versus the full cost minus deductible on RCV. The full math, with assumptions stated, is on the roof calculator's insurance section. Insurers use their own depreciation schedules; this is not a coverage determination.

Florida is one state with several wind climates, and the $15,790 baseline is a state average — not a Miami price and not a Tallahassee price. Florida wind code: what a re-roof triggers

Florida's roof rules are the strictest in the country, and a re-roof can trigger work beyond the shingles themselves. The High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) covers Miami-Dade and Broward counties, where ultimate design wind speeds typically run about 170–180 mph for a standard residential building (Risk Category II, varying by site) — the highest wind-load pressures in the country. Outside the HVHZ, design speeds are lower but still hurricane-grade across most coastal areas.

Three triggers matter when you budget a Florida re-roof:

  • Roof-to-wall connections. When an existing single-family home in a wind-borne debris region — generally where the basic wind speed is 140 mph or greater, covering most of Florida's coastal areas — gets a full roof replacement, the Florida Building Code requires the roof-to-wall connections (hurricane straps or clips) to be strengthened. For homes with an insured value of $300,000 or more, that mandatory retrofit work is capped at 15% of the total re-roofing cost.
  • Decking threshold. Replacing more than 50% of the roof decking also triggers a roof-to-wall connection upgrade, with enhanced fasteners and a secondary water barrier as part of the upgrade.
  • Product approval in the HVHZ. Roof assemblies in the HVHZ need Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA) or equivalent certification. A product approved elsewhere in Florida is not automatically approved in the HVHZ — check the NOA on your quote's product line.

None of this is inside the $15,790 baseline, which is labor-adjusted only. If your home predates the 2002 code changes, ask your contractor to price the connection upgrade as its own line — capped as above — rather than discovering it mid-job.

Choosing a material in Florida's climate

Florida punishes roofs differently from the rest of the country: intense UV, heat cycling, and humidity shorten shingle life, while wind ratings and weight decide how a roof behaves in a storm. The ranges below are contractor-published 2026 Florida ranges for a standard 2,000 sq ft home, not our measurements — our computed baseline ($15,790, architectural shingles) sits inside the shingle range, which is a useful consistency check.

The lifetime math is where Florida flips the usual ranking: two or three shingle replacements over 50 years can cost more than one metal roof, before any insurance savings. That does not make metal right for every home — tile suits homes already built for its weight, and shingles suit short-horizon owners — but compare materials on lifetime cost, not on day-one price alone.

Wind mitigation: the inspection that can cut premiums

After a Florida re-roof, book a wind mitigation inspection and keep the report. The inspector documents the features insurers price: roof covering, roof-deck attachment, roof-to-wall connection, secondary water resistance, and opening protection, on the state form OIR-B1-1802, which you submit to your insurer. Florida law requires insurers to offer premium discounts for qualifying wind-mitigation features, and a new roof with straps, sealed deck, and secondary water barrier typically qualifies across several categories at once.

How large the discount is varies by carrier, policy, and location — contractor-published claims range from about 10% to 40% of the wind portion of a premium, and that range is reported, not promised. Treat any agent's percentage as an estimate until your insurer applies it to your policy. The inspection itself is cheap relative to even one year of savings, and the report also helps at resale: it is documented proof of what the roof is, not just a contractor's word.

Florida permit and quote checklist

Use this list on every Florida quote, in addition to the national sign-off items on the roof calculator:

  • Permit in the contractor's name — confirm who pulls it and that the permit fee is in the quote, not added later.
  • HVHZ product approval — Miami-Dade NOA number on the product line for Miami-Dade and Broward jobs.
  • Connection upgrade priced separately — hurricane straps/clips as its own line, with the 15% cap noted where it applies.
  • Decking per-sheet price agreed upfront — so rot found at tear-off is billed at a known rate, not negotiated mid-job.
  • Underlayment type and secondary water barrier — named on the quote, since this is what keeps water out if tiles or shingles fail.
  • Flashing new vs reused, ventilation plan, disposal and nail sweep — all stated, not assumed.
  • Both warranties with durations — manufacturer and workmanship, separately.
  • Wind mitigation inspection booked at closeout — so the OIR-B1-1802 form reaches your insurer while the work is fresh.

Two local quotes remain the rule: Florida quotes vary more than the state average suggests, because wind zone, product approval, and decking condition all move the price. A quote that itemizes the lines above is comparable; one that does not is not.

Florida roof cost FAQs

Why is Florida cheaper than the South average?

Because the Florida state-average labor multiplier (×0.83) is below the South regional mean (×0.89). The South mean blends higher- and lower-wage states, so a Florida-specific figure lands lower. The gap is $469 on the example roof, all labor.

How is the Florida multiplier calculated?

From BLS OEWS May 2023 mean wages for construction laborers: the Florida state average ratioed to the national mean. 26 Florida ZIP3 areas in our data carry the state-average value (×0.83).

Does the Florida estimate include storm or code costs?

No. The multiplier adjusts labor only; materials, tear-off, and permit are national figures. Hurricane code requirements appear in individual quotes — get at least two local Florida quotes before deciding.

Does a Florida re-roof trigger hurricane-strap work?

Often, yes: a full roof replacement on an existing home in a wind-borne debris region requires roof-to-wall connections to be strengthened, and replacing more than 50% of the decking triggers the same upgrade. For homes insured at $300,000 or more, the mandatory retrofit cost is capped at 15% of the re-roofing total.

Why do shingles last shorter in Florida?

UV radiation, heat cycling, and humidity degrade shingles faster than in cooler climates: about 15–20 years in Florida conditions versus 25–30 years on packaging. That shorter life is why lifetime cost, not day-one price, is the right way to compare shingles with metal or tile.

What is a wind mitigation inspection?

An inspection that documents wind-resistant features — roof covering, deck attachment, roof-to-wall connection, secondary water resistance, opening protection — on state form OIR-B1-1802 for your insurer. Florida law requires insurers to offer discounts for qualifying features; the discount size varies by carrier and policy.
