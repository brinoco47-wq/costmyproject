How much does laminate flooring cost?

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Laminate is the budget wood-look that refuses to die — and modern 12mm planks look shockingly good. Here's the per-square-foot math, the thickness tiers that matter, and the honest laminate-vs-LVP decision.

The short answer

Laminate materials$1–$5
Pro installation labor$1.50–$4

Laminate and LVP share an installed range ($3–$8 vs. $3.50–$12) because they share an install method — click-lock floating floors go down at the same speed. The entire price story of laminate is therefore the material story: $1–$2 budget planks versus $3–$5 premium ones, with thickness as the visible proxy for quality. A 12mm laminate at $4/sq ft looks and feels like a different product than a 7mm at $1.50 — thicker core, deeper embossing, quieter underfoot — while installing for exactly the same labor price.

Two specs separate good laminate from bad beyond thickness: the AC rating (AC3 is residential-grade, AC4 handles commercial traffic — buy AC4 for hallways and kitchens) and whether the pad is attached (it should be at $2.50+; separate underlayment adds $0.30–$0.60/sq ft and a second install pass). The pattern-repeat issue on budget lines is real and visible: cheap laminate repeats its wood-grain photograph every 4–6 planks, and once you see the repetition you can't unsee it. Premium lines use 10+ unique plank visuals. In the store, lay out six planks from different boxes — if you can spot the twins, keep shopping.

The water rule Standard laminate and standing water are enemies — a dishwasher leak swells the HDF core irreversibly, and unlike LVP there's no drying it out. "Water-resistant" laminate (wax-sealed edges, water-repellent core) buys you hours against spills, not days against leaks. Kitchens and baths: LVP or tile. Everywhere else: laminate is fair game.

This is the comparison shoppers actually need, because the two products look interchangeable in the store and aren't. The one-line version: laminate is the tougher surface, LVP is the safer floor. Laminate's aluminum-oxide wear layer out-scratches vinyl at every price point — big dogs are laminate's best customers. LVP's waterproof core out-survives laminate wherever water appears — dishwashers, ice makers, and muddy paws are LVP territory. Price being nearly equal, the decision is purely about your house: dry rooms with scratch risk → laminate; any water risk → LVP. Mixed house? Many homeowners run laminate in bedrooms and LVP in the wet zones — the profiles are close enough in height to transition cleanly. The full LVP breakdown is in how much LVP flooring costs.

FAQs

How much does laminate flooring cost?

Laminate installs for $3–$8 per sq ft all-in in 2026 (planning ranges, ±25%): $1–$5/sq ft for materials plus $1.50–$4 for labor. A 300 sq ft living room runs roughly $900–$2,400 installed, or about half that DIY.

What is a good thickness for laminate flooring?

8–10mm is the sweet spot for most homes; 12mm+ premium planks look and sound dramatically better. Below 8mm the floor sounds hollow and shows pattern repeats. Also check the AC rating — AC4 handles hallways and kitchens better than AC3.

Is laminate or LVP better?

Laminate has the tougher scratch-resistant surface (better for big dogs); LVP is fully waterproof (better for kitchens, baths, basements). Installed costs are nearly identical ($3–$8 vs $3.50–$12). Dry rooms with scratch risk → laminate; any water risk → LVP.

How long does laminate flooring last?

10–20 years residentially. The aluminum-oxide wear layer resists scratches well, but moisture is the killer — one major leak can total a laminate floor, while surface wear rarely does.

Can I install laminate flooring myself?

Yes — click-lock floating laminate is one of the easiest DIY floors: score, snap, click, stagger, a room per weekend. The two rules: acclimate 48 hours and leave a 3/8-inch expansion gap at every wall, or summer humidity will buckle it.
