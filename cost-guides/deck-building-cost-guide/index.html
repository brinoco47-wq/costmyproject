Deck Building Costs: Labor, Permits, Stairs & Railing

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

The decking boards are only half the story. Labor is 40–60% of a pro quote, and stairs plus railing can rival the deck surface itself on a small elevated deck. Here's where every dollar goes — and how to read a bid like a builder.

Pro labor: the biggest line item

Professional deck labor runs roughly $10–$30 per square foot in 2026, depending on height, access, and complexity — typically 40–60% of the total installed quote. One reviewed invoice for a medium-complexity 16×16 deck showed a labor subtotal near $4,900 (site prep $600, footings $800, framing $1,200, decking $1,600, railing $700). Metro areas run higher; simple ground-level rectangles run lower.

Labor figures: Home Depot installer breakdowns and FixUpFirst 2026. Your deck calculator splits every estimate into materials vs. labor so you can see this ratio on your own numbers.

DIY savings are real but framing mistakes are structural — if any part of the build makes you nervous, that's the part to hire out. A hybrid approach (DIY the demolition and staining, pro the framing) is common.

6 quote red flags

  1. No license or insurance proof. Ask for both; verify the license number with your state board.
  2. No permit plan. "We don't need a permit for this" is sometimes true and often a lie — check your municipality yourself.
  3. One lump sum, no line items. Materials, labor, stairs, railing, permit, and removal should each have a number. No breakdown = no comparison.
  4. Pressure to sign today. Legitimate contractors give written quotes good for 30 days.
  5. Full payment upfront. Standard: a deposit (often 10–30%), progress payments, and a holdback until final inspection passes.
  6. A price far below the other bids. The cheapest bid usually skipped something structural — thinner joists, shallow footings, no flashing. Get three bids; throw out the outlier.

Inspection tips (don't skip these)

  • Footing inspection happens before concrete is poured — the inspector checks depth and size. Missing it can mean digging them back up.
  • Ledger attachment (where the deck meets the house) causes most deck collapses — it needs lag bolts/through-bolts plus flashing, never nails alone.
  • Final inspection covers railing height (usually 36"+), baluster spacing (under 4"), and stair geometry. Failed inspections delay everything.
  • Ask your contractor who schedules inspections — it should be in the contract.

Deck building FAQs

How much is labor to build a deck in 2026?

Roughly $10–$30 per square foot — 40–60% of a typical installed quote. Height, access, and complexity move it most.

How much does a deck permit cost?

Usually $100–$800 by municipality, plus inspections. Engineered permit drawings add $500–$2,500 where required.

How long does it take to build a deck?

A pro crew: 3–7 days for a typical deck. DIY: 2–4 weekends with a helper for a simple ground-level build. Permits and inspections add calendar time on top.

What time of year is cheapest to build a deck?

Late fall and winter — contractors discount when demand drops. You'll wait longer for concrete to cure in freezing weather, but the savings are real.
