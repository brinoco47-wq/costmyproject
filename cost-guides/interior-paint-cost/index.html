How much does it cost to paint a room?

Advertisement

CostMyProject Research Team Data checked October 2026

The honest answer: $140–$300 DIY per average room, or $700–$1,100 hiring a pro — and labor, not paint, is almost the entire difference. Here's the room-by-room math.

The 30-second version Paint is cheap; hours are expensive. Labor runs 70–85% of a professional paint job. A gallon of good paint ($44) covers a whole bedroom — the other $800 in a pro quote is someone's skilled time, prep work, and overhead.

This split (reported by Digital Estimating's 2026 guide) is why cutting paint quality to save money is almost always the wrong trade: upgrading from a $44 gallon to a $74 gallon on a two-gallon room adds $60 to a $900 job — under 7% — for measurably better hide and durability.

What makes quotes vary

  • Prep work is the hidden variable. Patching drywall, sanding, caulking, and stain-blocking can double labor hours on a rough room — and two quotes that both say "paint bedroom" may assume wildly different prep.
  • Ceiling height. 9-ft and vaulted ceilings add area and slow the work (ladders, cutting in). Expect 15–30% more than the 8-ft baseline.
  • Color change. Dark-to-light needs primer plus full two-coat coverage; similar-color repaints are the cheapest job there is.
  • Trim and doors. Often quoted separately — see our ceiling, door & trim guide.
  • The "per square foot" trap. Some painters quote per paintable square foot, others per floor square foot, others per room. A $3/sq ft quote on floor area is roughly a $1/sq ft quote on wall area — always ask which basis.

What every painting quote should itemize

Two quotes for "paint the bedroom" can differ 2× and both be legitimate — because they're quoting different scopes. Before comparing, make every bidder answer these seven questions in writing:

  1. Paint spec. Exact brand, product line, and sheen — not "premium paint." A contractor-grade line and a flagship line can differ $30+/gal.
  2. Coats. How many finish coats, and is primer included? One-coat quotes are cheaper until they aren't.
  3. Prep scope. Patching, sanding, caulking — itemized or hourly? Prep is the #1 hidden variable between bids.
  4. Areas covered. Walls only? Ceilings, trim, doors, closets? Get the room-by-room list.
  5. The square-foot basis. Per paintable sq ft, per floor sq ft, or per room? Normalize to cost per paintable square foot before comparing.
  6. Protection & cleanup. Furniture moving, floor protection, daily cleanup — included or extra?
  7. Warranty & payment. Workmanship warranty length, and never pay more than a third up front.

Run each quote's numbers through our paint calculator as a sanity check: if a bid lands far outside the ±25% planning range with no clear scope reason, ask why.

DIY or hire a pro?

DIY wins on bedrooms, bathrooms, and single accent walls — a weekend, $100–$200, and genuinely satisfying results with today's forgiving paints. Hire out stairwells, vaulted ceilings, whole-house repaints, and anything needing serious drywall repair; the speed and finish quality gap is real, and pros carry insurance for the ladder work.

The middle path: DIY the easy rooms, hire the hard ones. And get three quotes minimum — then sanity-check each against the calculator before signing.

What region does to the price

Labor is 70–85% of a pro job, so where you live moves the quote more than paint choice ever will. Two independent 2026 guides agree on the ordering: Digital Estimating puts the South Central and Southeast lowest ($2.25–$4.75 per sq ft of floor area) and the Northeast and West Coast highest ($3.75–$7.50), and Estimators.us reports the same pattern for labor rates alone (South and Midwest lowest, Northeast and West highest). What ceilings, trim, and doors add

The room table above is walls only, and walls-only quotes are where most budget surprises start. From our ceiling, door & trim guide (Digital Estimating 2026 rates): ceilings run $1–$2 per sq ft of ceiling area, interior doors $75–$150 each (both sides), baseboards and trim $1–$4 per linear foot, and crown molding $2–$6 per linear foot. On a 12×15 living room that turns a ~$1,090 walls-only pro job into roughly $1,750 with ceiling, trim, and two doors included — before any bundling discount. The bundling rule matters: added to a wall job already in progress, the same ceiling is mostly marginal labor, and contractors routinely discount bundled trim and ceiling work 20–40% versus standalone quotes. Always ask "what is the add-on price while you are already here?" before accepting a separate line item.

How long a room actually takes

Digital Estimating's 2026 guide times a pro crew at 2–3 hours for a bathroom, 4–5 hours for a bedroom, 5–7 hours for a living room, and 3–5 working days for a whole 2,000 sq ft interior with a two-to-three-person crew — add 1–2 hours per room for trim and baseboards. That is why a "one-day whole-house" quote deserves skepticism, and why DIY timelines stretch: the same bedroom that takes a crew half a day takes a weekend DIYer two days once drying time between coats is counted. Heavy prep, wallpaper removal, or a dark-to-light color change extends every figure above.

How to save without regretting it

  • Buy 5-gallon buckets for big jobs — Behr Premium Plus drops from $43.98 to $39.20/gal in the 5-gal size at Home Depot.
  • Do your own prep. Moving furniture, removing outlet covers, and light patching yourself can shave real hours off a pro bid.
  • One color throughout cuts labor — no extra cutting-in, no leftover partial gallons.
  • Don't skip the second coat to save $44. A one-coat job that flashes and shows through costs a full repaint later.

FAQs

How much does it cost to paint a 12×12 bedroom?

DIY: roughly $140 (2 gallons of mid-range paint plus supplies). Pro: roughly $700–$1,000 at typical $2.50/sq ft labor — the US range spans $1–$6/sq ft by market. Run your exact room through the paint calculator.

Why do painter quotes vary so much?

Labor rates vary $1–$6 per sq ft by market, prep assumptions differ enormously between bidders, and quotes use different bases (paintable sq ft vs. floor sq ft vs. per room). Normalize every quote to cost per paintable square foot before comparing.

Should I buy the paint or let the painter supply it?

Painters often get 10–30% contractor discounts, so their supply price can beat retail — but get the exact product spec (brand, line, sheen) in writing so you're not unknowingly downgraded to a cheaper line.

Does my region really change the price that much?

Yes — labor is 70–85% of the job, and 2026 guides put Southern labor lowest and Northeast/West Coast labor highest. On a 10×12 bedroom, our regional table runs from ~$844 in the South to ~$1,071 in the Northeast at the same paint spec. Get quotes from local painters and normalize each to cost per paintable square foot before comparing.

How long does it take to paint a bedroom?

A pro crew takes about 4–5 hours for a standard bedroom (Digital Estimating 2026), plus 1–2 hours if trim and baseboards are included. DIY takes longer once drying time between coats is counted — plan a full weekend for a bedroom, not an evening.
