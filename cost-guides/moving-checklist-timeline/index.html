The 8-week moving checklist & timeline

CostMyProject Research Team Data checked October 2026

Most moving stress — and most moving overspend — comes from doing things in the wrong order. This timeline puts every task where it belongs, with the money-saving moves flagged.

The 30-second version Book movers 4–8 weeks out (8–12 for long-distance/summer). Move mid-month, mid-week, off-season to cut 20–30%. Declutter before you get quotes — movers charge by weight and hours, so every donated box is money saved twice.

8 weeks out: decisions

  • Pick your move type. DIY truck, container, or full-service? Price all three for your distance in our moving calculator — the winner changes with mileage.
  • Set the date with money in mind. Mid-month beats month-end; Tuesday–Thursday beats Saturday; October–April beats June. If your dates are flexible, this one choice is worth 20–30%.
  • Start the declutter. Sell, donate, or trash what you don't want to pay to move. Long-distance movers charge by the pound — decluttering is the only discount you control completely.

6 weeks out: booking

  • Get three written quotes. For long-distance, insist on binding or binding-not-to-exceed estimates from an in-home or video survey. Verify each company's USDOT number (free, 60 seconds, FMCSA website).
  • Book the truck or container if you're going DIY — one-way truck and container availability tightens in summer.
  • Notify your landlord (usually 30–60 days' notice required) and start the address-change list: USPS, bank, insurance, subscriptions, voter registration.

4 weeks out: packing begins

  • Buy boxes and supplies. Budget roughly $100 (studio) to $275 (3 BR) for DIY materials — or price full packing service ($1,030–$2,200 for a 3 BR in 2026) if you'd rather not.
  • Pack non-essentials first: books, decor, off-season clothes, rarely-used kitchen gear. Label every box with room + contents.
  • Handle the specials: disassemble furniture, photograph electronics setups before unplugging, drain fuel from mowers, and set aside anything movers won't take (cash, documents, plants, hazardous materials).

2 weeks out: confirmations

  • Confirm everything in writing: mover arrival window, parking/elevator reservations at both ends, and your building's certificate-of-insurance requirements (some need 72-hour lead time).
  • Pack the kitchen and daily rooms. Leave out one week's essentials.
  • Arrange the gap plan: if move-out and move-in dates don't align, a container's built-in storage month or a short storage rental beats emergency storage-in-transit fees.

Moving week

  • Pack a first-night box: bedding, toiletries, chargers, basic tools, toilet paper, snacks. It rides with you, not the truck.
  • Do the walkthrough: photo every room empty (deposit protection), meter readings, keys collected.
  • Moving day: be present for loading, keep valuables and documents with you, tip the crew ($20–$40 per mover for a full day is customary, not mandatory).

After the move (week 1)

  • Unpack the kitchen and bedrooms first; break down boxes as you go (many movers haul away used boxes free if asked).
  • File any damage claims immediately — released-value claims have tight windows, and photos from delivery day are your evidence.
  • Update your driver's license and vehicle registration (most states give you 30 days).

The cost logic behind the timeline

Every timing decision in a move is secretly a pricing decision. Booking early locks rates before seasonal surges. Choosing a Tuesday over a Saturday cuts 20–30%. Decluttering before quotes — not after — lowers the binding estimate itself. And the single most expensive sentence in moving is "we need movers this weekend": last-minute bookings pay premium rates for leftover crews. The timeline below puts each money decision at the latest week it still works.

8 weeks out: lock the rate

This is the pricing week. Movers, containers, and one-way trucks all price on expected demand — booking before the surge locks the lower number.

  • Get three written quotes — for long-distance, insist on binding or binding-not-to-exceed estimates from an in-home or video survey. Verify each company's USDOT number (free, 60 seconds, FMCSA website).
  • Know deposit norms: local movers typically ask $0–$300 or nothing upfront; long-distance movers ask $200–$500 or ~10% of the estimate. Red flag: more than 25% upfront, or cash-only. Pay deposits by card for a paper trail.
  • Price all three move types for your distance in the moving calculator — DIY truck, container, full-service. The winner changes with mileage.
  • Set the date with money in mind. Mid-month beats month-end; Tuesday–Thursday beats Saturday; October–April beats June. If your dates are flexible, this one choice is worth 20–30%.

7 weeks out: quotes, paperwork, permits

  • Finish quote comparison. Compare binding totals, not hourly rates — a lower hourly rate with add-ons often loses to a higher all-in rate. Ask each mover for their full accessorial tariff (stairs, long carry, shuttle) upfront.
  • Start the address-change list: USPS, bank, credit cards, insurance, subscriptions, voter registration, pharmacy. USPS forwarding is cheap; missing a bill is not.
  • Research parking permits. Many cities require a reserved parking permit for moving trucks ($50–$150, applied for 1–2 weeks ahead). A ticket or a tow on moving day costs far more.
  • Notify your landlord (usually 30–60 days' notice required) and request any move-out documentation for deposit recovery.
  • Pull medical, dental, and school records if you're changing providers or districts — doing it now avoids rush fees and fax purgatory later.

6 weeks out: book it — the price-lock cutoff

  • Sign and pay the deposit. After this week, summer long-distance prices climb and crew choice shrinks. Confirm the binding estimate, arrival window, and valuation coverage in writing before money changes hands.
  • Book the truck or container if you're going DIY — one-way truck and container availability tightens sharply in summer.
  • Start the declutter in earnest. Sell, donate, or trash what you don't want to pay to move. Long-distance movers charge by the pound — decluttering is the only discount you control completely.

Why booking is a money move: carriers raise prices as their calendars fill. The quote you get at 8 weeks is frequently the lowest one you'll see — waiting until 2 weeks out routinely adds 10–25%.

5 weeks out: the declutter sprint

This is the highest-ROI week of the whole move. The math: on an 800–1,200 mile move, full-service pricing runs ~$0.65–$0.90 per pound — so every 100 lbs you shed saves roughly $65–$90 on a long-distance move, plus fewer boxes and fewer labor hours locally.

  • Sell furniture now — large items take 2–3 weeks to sell at fair prices; last-week fire sales recover pennies. Price to move it, not to maximize it.
  • Donate the rest and get receipts — donation value is deductible, and charities in most cities pick up furniture free.
  • Declutter BEFORE final quotes if you still can: a binding estimate is built on the inventory the estimator sees. Fewer items, lower binding number.
  • Digitize paper: scan filing cabinets and shred — paper is shockingly heavy, and nobody needs 2019 utility bills in the new place.

4 weeks out: packing begins

  • Buy boxes and supplies. Budget roughly $100 (studio) to $275 (3 BR) for DIY materials — or price full packing service ($1,030–$2,200 for a 3 BR in 2026) if you'd rather not. Free boxes from liquor stores, bookstores, and Buy Nothing groups can zero out the box line entirely.
  • Pack non-essentials first: books, decor, off-season clothes, rarely-used kitchen gear. Label every box with room + contents — movers stack faster (fewer billable hours) when boxes are clearly marked.
  • Photograph valuables and electronics setups before unplugging — photos are your evidence if you file a damage claim, and they make reassembly at the new place take minutes instead of hours.
  • Handle the specials: disassemble furniture, drain fuel from mowers and gas cans, and set aside anything movers won't take (cash, documents, plants, hazardous materials, propane tanks).

3 weeks out: first confirmations

  • Reconfirm the mover in writing: date, arrival window, crew size, and total. Get the building's certificate-of-insurance (COI) requirement handled now — some buildings need 72-hour lead time, and no COI can mean no move that day.
  • Reserve elevators and loading docks at both ends. Many buildings allow only one move per day — slots go to whoever books first.
  • Transfer or start utilities: electric, gas, water, internet at the new place (schedule installation appointments now — ISP slots book out), and schedule final readings at the old one.
  • Book your own travel for long-distance moves: budget $120–$180/night for 1–2 hotel nights on the drive, plus fuel and meals. If you're flying ahead, book now while fares are reasonable.

2 weeks out: confirmations

  • Confirm everything in writing: mover arrival window, parking/elevator reservations at both ends, and your building's certificate-of-insurance requirements (some need 72-hour lead time).
  • Pack the kitchen and daily rooms. Leave out one week's essentials — you'll thank yourself at 9pm on moving eve.
  • Arrange the gap plan: if move-out and move-in dates don't align, a container's built-in storage month or a short storage rental ($100–$250/month) beats emergency storage-in-transit fees, which carriers price punitively.
  • Confirm the parking permit (if required) and scout the closest legal truck parking at both addresses — every 100 feet of long carry is a potential $75–$200 fee.

1 week out: the final sweep

  • Pack a first-night box: bedding, toiletries, chargers, basic tools, toilet paper, snacks, medications. It rides with you, not the truck.
  • Defrost the freezer (24 hours), empty and clean the fridge, and run the last laundry — movers won't take a running appliance.
  • Withdraw tip cash. Movers appreciate cash tips; ATMs near the new place may be an unknown. See the tipping guide below.
  • Reconfirm the arrival window 48 hours out — a quick call catches the scheduling errors that ruin moving days.
  • Charge everything and check the weather at both ends; have tarps ready if rain threatens.

Moving day: the money moments

  • Read the bill of lading before signing. Check the valuation coverage selected, the inventory numbers, and the delivery window. Once signed, it governs everything.
  • Be present for loading (or send a trusted adult): direct which rooms load first, keep valuables and documents with you at all times, and do a final sweep of closets, attic, and storage.
  • Do the walkthrough: photo every room empty (deposit protection), take meter readings, collect all keys.

Tipping guide (customary, not mandatory)

SituationCustomary tip
Local move, half day (≤4 hrs)$10–$20 per mover
Local move, full day$20–$40 per mover
Long-distance, loading crew$30–$50 per mover
Long-distance, delivery crew$30–$50 per mover

Tip in cash, handed to each mover individually — never add it to a card payment and assume it reaches the crew. Tip more for stairs, pianos, exceptional care, or brutal conditions. On long-distance moves the loading and delivery crews are different people: tip both.

After the move (week 1)

  • Unpack the kitchen and bedrooms first; break down boxes as you go (many movers haul away used boxes free if asked).
  • File any damage claims immediately — interstate claims can technically be filed up to 9 months out, but released-value claims have tight practical windows, and photos from delivery day are your evidence. Note what coverage you bought: released value pays $0.60/lb; full-value pays repair or replacement.
  • Update your driver's license and vehicle registration (most states give you 30 days) and confirm USPS forwarding caught everything.
  • Change the locks — $100–$200 for a locksmith, or ~$15 per deadbolt DIY. You don't know who has keys to your new place.
  • Review final utility bills at the old address and confirm deposits are returned.

If plans change: cancellation and reschedule norms

  • Cancel 7+ days out: most movers refund the deposit in full. Read your order-for-service — the policy is in there.
  • Cancel inside 7 days: expect to forfeit the deposit ($200–$500 typical on long-distance).
  • Reschedule inside 72 hours: $200–$500 rescheduling fees are common — the crew was already assigned to you.
  • Containers are more flexible than full-service on date changes; truck rentals usually allow free cancellation until pickup.
  • Never no-show. A no-show can forfeit the deposit and burn the relationship — a phone call is always cheaper.

FAQs

How far in advance should I book movers?

4–8 weeks for local, 8–12 weeks for long-distance or summer moves. Under 2 weeks you'll pay premium rates for whoever's available — not whoever's best.

What is the cheapest day to move?

Mid-month, Tuesday–Thursday, October–April. Demand peaks on summer weekends and month-end turnovers; sliding off-peak routinely cuts 20–30% off the identical job.

When should I start packing for a move?

3–4 weeks out for non-essentials, final week for kitchens and daily-use rooms, first-night box last. Or hire it out: pro packers clear a 3-bedroom home in about a day at $1,030–$2,200 in 2026.

How much deposit should a mover ask for?

Local moves: often $0–$300, sometimes nothing. Long-distance: typically $200–$500 or about 10% of the estimate. More than 25% upfront — or cash-only — is a red flag; legitimate movers take cards and put the deposit terms in the written order-for-service.

How much should I tip movers?

Customary (not mandatory): $10–$20 per mover for a half-day local move, $20–$40 per mover for a full local day. On long-distance moves, tip the loading crew and the delivery crew separately — $30–$50 per mover each. Hand cash to each mover directly.

What if I need to reschedule my move?

Seven or more days out, most movers reschedule free and refund deposits. Inside 72 hours, expect a $200–$500 rescheduling fee — the crew was already assigned. Containers are more flexible on date changes than full-service movers; truck rentals usually cancel free until pickup.

Do I have to be present on moving day?

Not legally — but be there, or send a trusted adult. Someone must sign the bill of lading, verify inventory numbers, direct room placement at delivery, and do the final walkthrough. An absent customer is also how "extra" access fees go unchallenged.
