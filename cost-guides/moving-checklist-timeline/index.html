The 8-week moving checklist & timeline

CostMyProject Research Team Data checked October 2026

Most moving stress — and most moving overspend — comes from doing things in the wrong order. This timeline puts every task where it belongs, with the money-saving moves flagged.

The 30-second version Book movers 4–8 weeks out (8–12 for long-distance/summer). Move mid-month, mid-week, off-season to cut 20–30%. Declutter before you get quotes — movers charge by weight and hours, so every donated box is money saved twice.

8 weeks out: decisions

  • Pick your move type. DIY truck, container, or full-service? Price all three for your distance in our moving calculator — the winner changes with mileage.
  • Set the date with money in mind. Mid-month beats month-end; Tuesday–Thursday beats Saturday; October–April beats June. If your dates are flexible, this one choice is worth 20–30%.
  • Start the declutter. Sell, donate, or trash what you don't want to pay to move. Long-distance movers charge by the pound — decluttering is the only discount you control completely.

6 weeks out: booking

  • Get three written quotes. For long-distance, insist on binding or binding-not-to-exceed estimates from an in-home or video survey. Verify each company's USDOT number (free, 60 seconds, FMCSA website).
  • Book the truck or container if you're going DIY — one-way truck and container availability tightens in summer.
  • Notify your landlord (usually 30–60 days' notice required) and start the address-change list: USPS, bank, insurance, subscriptions, voter registration.

4 weeks out: packing begins

  • Buy boxes and supplies. Budget roughly $100 (studio) to $275 (3 BR) for DIY materials — or price full packing service ($1,030–$2,200 for a 3 BR in 2026) if you'd rather not.
  • Pack non-essentials first: books, decor, off-season clothes, rarely-used kitchen gear. Label every box with room + contents.
  • Handle the specials: disassemble furniture, photograph electronics setups before unplugging, drain fuel from mowers, and set aside anything movers won't take (cash, documents, plants, hazardous materials).

2 weeks out: confirmations

  • Confirm everything in writing: mover arrival window, parking/elevator reservations at both ends, and your building's certificate-of-insurance requirements (some need 72-hour lead time).
  • Pack the kitchen and daily rooms. Leave out one week's essentials.
  • Arrange the gap plan: if move-out and move-in dates don't align, a container's built-in storage month or a short storage rental beats emergency storage-in-transit fees.

Moving week

  • Pack a first-night box: bedding, toiletries, chargers, basic tools, toilet paper, snacks. It rides with you, not the truck.
  • Do the walkthrough: photo every room empty (deposit protection), meter readings, keys collected.
  • Moving day: be present for loading, keep valuables and documents with you, tip the crew ($20–$40 per mover for a full day is customary, not mandatory).

After the move (week 1)

  • Unpack the kitchen and bedrooms first; break down boxes as you go (many movers haul away used boxes free if asked).
  • File any damage claims immediately — released-value claims have tight windows, and photos from delivery day are your evidence.
  • Update your driver's license and vehicle registration (most states give you 30 days).

FAQs

How far in advance should I book movers?

4–8 weeks for local, 8–12 weeks for long-distance or summer moves. Under 2 weeks you'll pay premium rates for whoever's available — not whoever's best.

What is the cheapest day to move?

Mid-month, Tuesday–Thursday, October–April. Demand peaks on summer weekends and month-end turnovers; sliding off-peak routinely cuts 20–30% off the identical job.

When should I start packing?

3–4 weeks out for non-essentials, final week for kitchens and daily-use rooms, first-night box last. Or hire it out: pro packers clear a 3-bedroom home in about a day at $1,030–$2,200 in 2026.
