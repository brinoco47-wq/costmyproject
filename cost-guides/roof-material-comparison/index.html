The takeaway: tile and metal are the cheapest roofs you can buy over 30 years — the premium is all in the first invoice. Material by material: the fine print

3-tab asphalt — the budget play

Flat, single-layer shingles. Cheapest installed ($3.50–$5.50/sqft) and shortest-lived (15–20 years). Thin enough that wind and hail age them visibly. Rational choice: rental properties, homes you'll sell within 10 years, or when the budget genuinely has no flex.

Architectural (dimensional) asphalt — the default answer

Multi-layer laminated shingles with a dimensional look. The sweet spot for most homeowners: $4.50–$7.50 installed, 25–30 year life, Class A fire rating, huge installer base (easy to get competitive bids). If you don't know which material to pick, pick this.

Standing-seam metal — the lifetime roof

Concealed-fastener steel or aluminum panels. $10–$16 installed, 40–70 year life, Class A fire, sheds snow, shrugs off hail that would total shingles. Lighter than asphalt (~1–1.5 lbs/sqft — no structural worries). The catch: specialized labor, so get bids from actual metal roofers, not shingle crews who "also do metal."

Wood shake / shingle — the aesthetic tax

Cedar shakes at $8.50–$14 installed with a 25–40 year life — but only with treatment every ~5 years (~$1,500 each, estimate). Fire ratings lag (Class B/C unless treated), and some wildfire-zone insurers won't touch it. Beautiful on the right house; the most expensive roof in this table over 30 years.

Clay / concrete tile — the century roof

$11–$18 installed, 50–100 year life, Class A fire, basically immortal in hot climates. Two catches: weight (6–10 lbs/sqft — many homes need structural reinforcement, which is NOT in these numbers) and fragility underfoot (repairs need a tile-savvy crew). Plan an underlayment refresh around year 25 (~$5,000, estimate).

How to choose in 60 seconds

  • Selling within 10 years? 3-tab or architectural — you won't own the replacement either way.
  • Staying 15–25 years? Architectural shingles. Best value per dollar in the realistic window.
  • Staying 30+ years, or want the last roof you'll buy? Standing-seam metal (no structural questions) or tile (if your framing can take it).
  • Hail, wildfire, or hurricane zone? Metal or Class 4 impact shingles — then call your insurer about the discount.
  • Historic district or cedar look required? Wood — budgeted with eyes open about treatment costs.

Roof material FAQs

Which roof material lasts the longest?

Clay/concrete tile (50–100 years) and standing-seam metal (40–70 years). Architectural asphalt: 25–30. Wood shake: 25–40 with treatment. 3-tab asphalt: 15–20. Climate and install quality move every number — a perfect install in a mild climate outlives a sloppy one in hail country by a decade.

Which roof material is cheapest over 30 years?

Tile (~$18,300) and metal (~$18,500) edge out architectural shingles (~$19,300) on a 2,236-sqft roof, because shingles need a second installation inside 30 years. 3-tab runs ~$25,800 and wood ~$33,600 with treatments. See the table above for the full math.

Can my roof hold the weight of tile?

Maybe not — tile is 6–10 lbs/sqft, 3–4× a shingle roof. Homes framed for shingles often need reinforcement. Have a structural engineer or experienced roofer verify before you sign; reinforcement costs are not in any number on this page.

Are metal roofs noisy in the rain?

No — that's a barn myth. Residential standing-seam goes over solid decking, underlayment, and attic insulation, which dampen rain to roughly shingle levels. The noisy version is uninsulated metal over open purlins, which isn't how homes are roofed.

Does roof material affect homeowners insurance?

Often. Impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles and metal roofs earn discounts in hail and hurricane zones; wood shake can raise premiums or be uninsurable in wildfire areas. Get the insurance quote before you choose the material.
