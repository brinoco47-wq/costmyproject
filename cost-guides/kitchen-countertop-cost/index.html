Kitchen Countertop Cost 2026

CostMyProject Research Team Prices checked October 2026

Countertops are the most-touched surface in your kitchen and about 10% of a remodel budget — but the material choice swings the line item by 5× or more. This guide prices every major material per square foot installed, compares durability honestly, and covers edge profiles and the hidden costs. All figures are October 2026 US planning estimates. "50 sq ft" is the standard reference for a 10×10 kitchen including a small island. Prices include templating, fabrication, and installation — but read on for what they don't include.

Material-by-material breakdown

Laminate ($15–$30/sq ft)

The budget champion. Modern high-definition laminates convincingly mimic marble and wood grain, install in a day, and cost a third of quartz. Weaknesses: no hot pans directly on the surface, and the dark seam line at the edge gives away the material. Best for: refreshes, rentals, and anyone who'd rather spend the $5,000 difference on appliances.

Butcher block ($40–$100/sq ft)

Warm, forgiving, and repairable — scratches sand out. Maple and oak sit at the low end; walnut and end-grain at the high end. The maintenance is real: mineral oil monthly for the first year, then quarterly. Water around the sink is the enemy — it must be sealed there religiously or it darkens and swells. Best for: islands and baking zones rather than the full perimeter.

Granite ($50–$120/sq ft)

Natural stone, each slab unique, excellent heat tolerance. Price depends heavily on the specific stone — common Ubatuba-type granites sit near $50–$70; exotic blues and movement-heavy slabs push $100+. Needs sealing every 1–3 years. Best for: buyers who want real stone character and cook with hot pans.

Quartz ($60–$140/sq ft)

Engineered stone (quartz + resin): consistent pattern, zero sealing, stain-resistant, huge color range. The default choice in most 2026 mid-range and up remodels for good reason — it asks nothing of you. Slightly less heat-tolerant than granite (trivets still recommended). Best for: busy families who want stone looks with laminate-level maintenance.

Marble ($75–$200/sq ft)

The luxury statement — and the highest-maintenance. Etches with acids (lemon, wine, tomato), stains without vigilant sealing (every 6–12 months), and develops a patina that purists love and perfectionists hate. Carrara sits at the low end; Calacatta and rare varieties at the top. Best for: show kitchens and bakers (cool surface for pastry), not for anyone who'll stress over a lemon slice.

Price overlaps so much that the decision should be about lifestyle, not cost: pick quartz if you want zero maintenance, granite if you want natural stone and cook hot. Either is a 25-year-plus surface.

Edge profiles: the $10–$30/linear ft detail

A standard eased or pencil edge is usually included in the per-sq-ft price. Upgraded profiles cost extra per linear foot of edge:

  • Included: eased, pencil, straight — clean and modern, $0 add-on.
  • $10–$20/lf: bevel, half-bullnose — softens the edge, hides chips better.
  • $20–$30+/lf: ogee, waterfall, mitered — the furniture-grade looks. A waterfall island edge can add $500–$1,500 alone.

On 30 linear feet of edge, the jump from eased to ogee is roughly $600–$900 — worth knowing before you fall in love with a sample.

Hidden costs people miss

  • Demo and disposal: $300–$800 to remove old counters (tile and old stone are heavy).
  • Sink cutouts: usually 1 included; extra cutouts (prep sink, range) $100–$250 each. Undermount sink installation often bundled — confirm.
  • Backsplash: not part of the countertop quote. Tile backsplash runs $10–$30/sq ft installed; a 30 sq ft backsplash is $300–$900 more.
  • Plumbing reconnect: $150–$400 for the plumber to reconnect sink and dishwasher after install.
  • Seams: slabs max out around 10 ft; L-shaped kitchens need seams. Ask where they'll fall — a seam across the main work zone is worth paying to avoid via a second slab.
  • Support for overhangs: breakfast-bar overhangs over 10–12 inches need corbels or steel brackets ($50–$200 each).

How to choose

  1. Budget under $2,000? Laminate — and spend the savings where it shows more (lighting, hardware).
  2. Want warmth on a budget? Butcher block on the island + laminate on the perimeter is a classic designer trick.
  3. Want "forever" with zero fuss? Quartz. It's the default for a reason.
  4. Love natural stone and cook seriously? Granite — seal it and forget it for years.
  5. Building a showpiece? Marble — but go in eyes open about etching and sealing.

Other materials worth knowing

  • Solid surface (e.g., Corian): $45–$90/sq ft installed. Seamless look, repairable (scratches buff out), huge color range. Scratches more easily than quartz but every scratch is fixable — a fair trade many homeowners prefer.
  • Concrete: $70–$140/sq ft installed. Fully custom shapes and colors, industrial-modern look. Heavy, needs sealing, and develops hairline cracks — buy it for the aesthetic, not the practicality.
  • Stainless steel: $80–$150/sq ft. The pro-kitchen look; hygienic and heat-proof but shows every fingerprint and scratches visibly. Rare in homes outside serious cook spaces.
  • Tile: $20–$60/sq ft installed. Cheap and DIY-friendly, but grout lines stain and the uneven surface annoys many cooks. Largely replaced by large-format porcelain slabs ($60–$120/sq ft) that mimic marble with minimal grout.

How to read a countertop quote

Countertop quotes are where "per square foot" gets slippery. Before signing, confirm these five things in writing:

  1. Is the price per slab sq ft or per installed sq ft? Fabrication waste (cutouts, seams) means you buy more slab than ends up on your counters — 10–20% more. Clarify which number you're paying.
  2. How many slabs, and can I pick mine? For natural stone, always view the actual slabs — photos lie about veining. Some shops charge a "slab selection" visit fee; it's worth it.
  3. What's the edge, and what does upgrading cost? Get the per-linear-foot upcharge in writing before you fall for the ogee sample.
  4. Who handles the sink, plumbing, and backsplash? The three trades that show up after the counters are where surprise charges live.
  5. What's the seam plan? Ask them to mark seam locations on the template drawing. A seam through the middle of your main prep zone is a daily annoyance for 25 years.

Countertop FAQs

How much do kitchen countertops cost per square foot?

Installed 2026 prices: laminate $15–$30, butcher block $40–$100, granite $50–$120, quartz $60–$140, marble $75–$200 per sq ft. A 50 sq ft quartz kitchen runs ~$3,000–$7,000.

Is quartz or granite cheaper?

Granite averages slightly less ($50–$120 vs. $60–$140), but ranges overlap. Choose on lifestyle: quartz for zero maintenance, granite for natural character and heat tolerance.

What is the cheapest kitchen countertop?

Laminate at $15–$30/sq ft installed — about a third of quartz. Modern laminates look convincingly like stone; just keep hot pans off them.

Do countertops need to be sealed?

Granite every 1–3 years, marble every 6–12 months. Quartz, laminate, and solid surface never need it. Butcher block needs oiling instead.

How long do kitchen countertops last?

Laminate 10–20 years, butcher block 20+ with oiling, granite and quartz 25–50+, marble 50+ structurally. Stone usually outlasts the cabinets beneath it.
