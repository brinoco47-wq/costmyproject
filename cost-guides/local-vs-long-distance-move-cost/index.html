Local vs. long-distance moving costs

CostMyProject Research Team Data checked October 2026

The single most important thing to know about moving prices: under 100 miles, movers sell you hours; over 100 miles, they sell you weight-times-distance. Everything about how to shop, compare, and save follows from that split.

The 30-second version Local = hourly rate × crew hours (national average ~$1,250). Long-distance = flat rate from shipment weight × miles (national average ~$4,300). A move's distance doesn't just change the price — it changes what you're actually buying.

What counts as "long-distance"

The industry line is 100 miles: under it, local; over it — or across a state line — long-distance (interstate). Interstate moves are federally regulated by the FMCSA: the carrier must give you a written estimate, a "Your Rights and Responsibilities" booklet, and arbitration options. If a long-distance mover won't put it in writing, walk away — that's the regulation telling you something.

Why the same stuff costs 4× more at 1,200 miles

Hours → weightThe billing base flips at 100 miles
Days, not hoursYour truck is committed for a week, not an afternoon
MinimumsLong-distance carriers enforce floor charges (~$1,200+)

A 3-bedroom local move (~$1,800 in our model) becomes ~$6,900 at 1,200 miles — same belongings, different economics. The truck, driver, fuel, insurance, and regulatory overhead scale with distance while your stuff just sits there.

How to shop each type

  • Local: get hourly quotes and ask what's included in the rate (truck fee? materials? stairs?). A lower hourly rate with add-ons often beats a higher all-in rate — or the reverse. Compare totals, not rates.
  • Long-distance: demand a binding or binding-not-to-exceed written estimate based on an in-home or video survey. Non-binding estimates can — and do — balloon on delivery day.
  • Both: verify the USDOT number at FMCSA's website before signing anything. It's free and takes 60 seconds.

When each move type wins on value

  • Local + small home: DIY truck rental is the value king — often under $200 all-in vs. $500+ for movers.
  • Local + large home: full-service is worth pricing seriously; a 4-mover crew can finish in a day what takes you a weekend, and the gap is a few hundred dollars, not thousands.
  • Long-distance: containers (PODS, U-Pack) routinely save $1,000–$3,000 vs. full-service on 500+ mile moves, with a storage month built in — the sweet spot for most interstate moves.

FAQs

At what distance does a move become long-distance?

100 miles is the industry line — under it, hourly billing; over it (or across state lines), flat weight-and-distance billing under FMCSA regulation. The 100-mile mark is also where our calculator switches models.

Why are long-distance moves so much more expensive?

The billing base changes from hours to weight-times-distance, the equipment is tied up for days, and carriers carry heavier insurance and regulatory costs — plus enforced minimum charges. Same stuff, different economics.

How long does a long-distance move take?

Plan on 1–3 days regionally, 4–7 days around 1,000 miles, and 7–14 days cross-country for a large home. You'll get a delivery window, not a date — get it in writing.
