Paint finishes explained

CostMyProject Research Team Reviewed September 2026

Sheen is the only paint decision that's both aesthetic and financial: the wrong finish in the wrong room means repainting years early. Here's the no-nonsense version.

The five finishes, ranked by shine

FinishShineWashabilityHides flaws?Use it on
Flat / matteNoneLowBestCeilings, low-traffic bedrooms, imperfect walls
EggshellSoft glowGoodGoodLiving rooms, dining rooms, hallways
SatinSilkyVery goodFairKids' rooms, kitchens, bathrooms, exterior siding

The one rule that matters

Shinier = tougher, but less forgiving Gloss comes from resins that form a harder, more washable film — but that same reflectivity spotlights every bump, roller mark, and patch. Flat paint is the opposite: it forgives bad walls and punishes sticky fingers. Pick your tradeoff per room, not per house.

Room-by-room cheat sheet

  • Ceilings: flat, always. Anything shinier turns your ceiling into a topographical map of every drywall seam.
  • Living room / bedrooms: eggshell is the default; satin if you have kids, pets, or a hallway that takes abuse.
  • Kitchen / bathroom: satin minimum — moisture and grease demand washability. (Bath-specific formulations exist; they're worth it in shower-adjacent zones.)
  • Trim, doors, cabinets: semi-gloss. It's the standard because it works: scuff-resistant, wipeable, and the contrast against matte walls looks intentional.
  • Front door: high-gloss or semi-gloss. It's the handshake of your house; make it durable.

Does sheen change the cost?

At the register, almost never: brands price every sheen of a product line the same per gallon (a gallon of Behr Premium Plus is $43.98 whether it's flat or semi-gloss). The real cost difference is prep. Semi-gloss on trim demands sanded-smooth surfaces and careful brushwork — the labor to make gloss look good is where the money goes. Flat paint on a lumpy wall is the budget move that still looks expensive.

One more cost angle: higher-traffic rooms painted in flat will need repainting sooner. Spending the same $44 on satin for a hallway can easily buy you 3–4 extra years — the cheapest paint is the one you don't rebuy.

FAQs

What's the best paint finish for living room walls?

Eggshell for most living rooms — it hides minor imperfections while wiping clean. Step up to satin for high-traffic family rooms.

Can I mix finishes in one room?

Yes, and you should: the classic combo is flat or eggshell walls with semi-gloss trim. Same color family, different sheens — it adds depth without adding color risk.

Does a glossier finish need more coats?

Not more coats — better prep. The paint covers the same; the shine just reveals what's underneath. Budget the extra effort into sanding and patching, not extra gallons.
